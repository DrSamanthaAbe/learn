Why it happens

Motion gets a baby to sleep. It can't teach a baby to sleep.

1 · Safety

Swings aren't made for sleep.

The American Academy of Pediatrics says sitting devices such as car seats, strollers and swings are “not recommended for routine sleep,” especially under 4 months, and that babies should sleep on “a firm, flat noninclined surface.”Source: American Academy of Pediatrics, safe sleep recommendations (2022).

2 · How it works

The motion is doing the work.

Your baby isn't falling asleep. They're being moved to sleep. When the motion stops, or a sleep cycle ends, they need it again.

3 · The end date

Every swing has a size limit.

The day your baby outgrows it, or learns to sit up in it, the one thing that worked is gone and there is no plan B.

Is it okay for my baby to nap in the swing?

The American Academy of Pediatrics says swings and other sitting devices are not recommended for routine sleep. If your baby does fall asleep in one, their guidance is to move them to a firm, flat surface on their back as soon as you can.

My baby screams in the crib. Will this work for us?

That's the most common starting point we see. The plan is built around your baby, and you're told exactly what to do before night one.
