How do I stop contact napping?

Start with nights, not naps: once your baby falls asleep independently at bedtime, transfer that skill to the first nap of the day, which has the most sleep pressure behind it. Most babies move from contact naps to crib naps in 1–3 weeks when the change is consistent.

Why naps are harder than nights

Sleep pressure is lower during the day, naps are short, and a baby who is used to warmth and motion has a strong preference. That is why most families who try to fix naps first burn out; nights are the easier win, and a baby who can self-settle at 7 p.m. can learn to do it at 9 a.m.

The order that works

  1. Bedtime first. Put baby down awake at night and stay consistent for 3–7 nights.
  2. Then nap 1. It is the most reliable. Same routine as bedtime, shortened. Give it a defined window; if it does not happen, move on and preserve the rest of the day.
  3. Then nap 2. Once nap 1 is solid, repeat.
  4. Rescue the day, not the nap. Early on, a failed crib nap can be followed by an assisted nap so the baby is not wrecked by bedtime. The goal is the pattern, not a perfect day.

Common mistakes

  • Trying crib naps only sometimes. Partial attempts teach the baby that protest works.
  • Wake windows too long or too short. Check the schedule before blaming the method.
  • Giving up after a 30-minute nap. Short naps are normal in the first week; they lengthen as the skill solidifies.

If you want to keep one contact nap a day because you love it, you can — but choose which one and keep it consistent, so the baby is not guessing.

Frequently asked

At what age should I stop contact napping?

There is no deadline. Newborns under 10–12 weeks nap however they can. From about 3–4 months, independent naps become learnable and usually get harder to introduce the longer they wait.

Is contact napping bad?

No. It is a preference, not a problem, until it stops working for your family. Then it is a habit that can be changed.

How long do contact nap transitions take?

Typically 1–3 weeks for naps to become reliable in the crib, after nights are already independent.
