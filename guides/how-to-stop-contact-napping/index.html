How do I stop contact napping?

Contact naps stop when your baby learns to fall asleep independently. That skill usually comes more easily at bedtime than at nap time, so nights are often the place to start, with naps following. How fast depends on your baby’s age, temperament and day. Actually Sleeping writes a plan for your baby and coaches naps until they are solid.

Why contact naps are so hard to change

During the day there is less built-up tiredness than at bedtime, naps are short, and a baby who is used to warmth, movement and your heartbeat has a strong preference for it. That is why many families who try to fix naps first burn out. A baby who can settle independently at bedtime has the skill to do it at nap time, but naps still take their own work.

Actually Sleeping plans start with nights and bring naps along with them. Dr. Sam writes the plan for your baby, a coach is with you live on night one, and naps are coached until they are solid, not left for you to figure out once nights are fixed.

Why naps fall apart when parents try alone

  • Crib naps only some of the time. Partial attempts teach a baby that protest eventually brings back the contact nap.
  • Short naps read as failure. Early crib naps are often short. Parents give up just before they would have lengthened.
  • A schedule that does not fit. A baby who is overtired or not tired enough fights any nap, and the method gets the blame.
  • One bad nap spills into the next. A missed nap leads to an overtired bedtime, and a hard night undoes progress.

Why naps need a plan for your baby

The number of naps, how long your baby can stay awake, their temperament and how they fall asleep now all change what a sensible nap plan looks like. So does real life: daycare, a sibling’s school run, a parent going back to work. A generic schedule from a chart assumes none of that. This is where daily 1:1 check-ins matter most. Naps change from week to week, and a coach adjusts the plan as they do.

Keeping a contact nap you love

Contact naps are lovely when they are a choice. The aim of a plan is that holding your baby for a nap becomes something you do when you want to, not the only way a nap happens. If you do hold your baby for a nap, stay awake. The AAP warns that falling asleep with a baby on a couch, soft armchair or cushion carries a much higher risk of sleep-related death. Your baby’s own sleep space is a firm, flat surface with a fitted sheet and nothing else.

Frequently asked

At what age should I stop contact napping?

There is no deadline, and contact naps are not harmful. Actually Sleeping works with families from the newborn stage, where the early work is laying foundations. When contact naps stop working for your family, a plan written for your baby’s age makes the change.

Is contact napping bad?

No. It is a preference, not a problem, until it stops working for your family. When it does, a Actually Sleeping plan teaches crib naps so that holding your baby for a nap becomes something you choose.

How long do contact nap transitions take?

It depends on your baby’s age, temperament and how nights are going. Naps often follow once nights are solid. At Actually Sleeping, naps are coached until they are solid, with daily check-ins that adjust the plan as your baby responds.
