Why does my overtired baby fight sleep?

When a baby stays awake well past the point their body was ready for sleep, many become more wired and harder to settle, not sleepier. The result looks backwards: the more tired the baby, the harder they fight. Overtiredness feeds itself through short naps and broken nights, and the lasting fix is a schedule and bedtime set for your baby’s age and actual sleep.

What “overtired” means

Sleep pressure builds the longer a baby is awake. Past a certain point, many babies get a second wind instead of settling. For parents the practical version is simple: a tired baby settles; an overtired one is wired, frantic and hard to console.

Tired versus overtired

TiredOvertired
Staring into space, quieter, less interested in toysCrying that escalates quickly, arching, stiffening
First yawn, slower movementsFrantic or jerky movements; pulling ears, rubbing eyes hard
Turning away from stimulationA burst of giddy energy in an older baby or toddler
Falls asleep in 5–15 minutesFights the put-down, or crashes in under 2 minutes and wakes 30–45 minutes later

From about 4–5 months, many babies skip the early cues entirely, so by the time you see a yawn the window has often passed.

Overtiredness is a symptom, and the cause differs from baby to baby. Actually Sleeping works out what is driving it for yours and sets a schedule and bedtime for them, instead of a one-size wake-window chart.

Why it becomes a cycle

  • Overtired babies take short naps, which leave them overtired for the next stretch. See 30-minute naps.
  • They often wake soon after bedtime. See false starts.
  • They wake more at night and earlier in the morning. See early morning waking.

Each bad stretch sets up the next one. Parents respond by rescuing naps with rocking, feeding or the car, which is reasonable on a hard day but means the baby never practises falling asleep alone. A few weeks in, the family has an overtired baby and a sleep association, and fixing one without the other does not hold.

When it is not overtiredness

“Overtired” gets blamed for nearly everything, which is one reason generic advice so often misses:

  • Undertired. A baby put down too soon also fights sleep, but is usually happy about it: playing, babbling, rolling.
  • Sleep association. If your baby only fights when put down awake and sleeps easily when fed or rocked, the issue is how they fall asleep, at any level of tiredness.
  • Evening fussiness in the first months. Fussing that typically peaks at about 3 hours a day around 6 weeks and declines by 3–4 months is common and not caused by the schedule. Long, inconsolable crying deserves a pediatrician’s opinion.
  • Pain or illness. A sudden change in a baby who normally settles well.

Frequently asked

Can a baby be too tired to sleep?

Often, yes. Past a certain point many babies get a second wind and settling becomes harder, so an overtired baby often fights sleep more than a merely tired one.

How do I break the overtired cycle?

By fixing what keeps creating it: short naps, a schedule that does not match your baby, and a way of falling asleep that breaks down at every cycle. In a Actually Sleeping plan, Dr. Sam sets the timings for your baby and adjusts them daily.

Does keeping a baby awake longer make them sleep better at night?

Rarely. Stretching a baby past their window usually produces a harder bedtime, more night waking and an earlier morning.

How early is too early for bedtime?

A very early bedtime can be treated by the body as a nap and lead to false starts or split nights. The right bedtime depends on your baby’s naps and age, which is why it is set for each baby in a plan.
