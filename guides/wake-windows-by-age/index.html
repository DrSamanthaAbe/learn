What are the right wake windows by age?

Roughly 45–90 minutes for newborns, 1.5–2.5 hours at 4–6 months, 2–3.5 hours at 6–9 months, 3–4 hours at 9–12 months, and 4–6 hours once a toddler is on one nap. These are starting points, not rules: watch your baby’s cues and the quality of the sleep that follows, and adjust in 15-minute steps.

How to read your baby, not the chart

  • Too short a window: long time to fall asleep, playing or babbling in the crib, short nap, early waking.
  • Too long a window: fussy or wired before sleep, crying at put-down, short nap, frequent night waking, early waking.
  • Both produce short naps, which is why “my baby only naps 30 minutes” needs the schedule checked before the method is blamed.

Rules of thumb

The first window of the day is usually the shortest; the last before bed is usually the longest. Move one thing at a time, by 15 minutes, and hold it for three days before judging. Bedtime between 6:30 and 8 p.m. for most babies past the newborn stage.

Frequently asked

Are wake windows or sleepy cues more important?

Use windows as the frame and cues to fine-tune. By 4–5 months many babies stop showing clear cues until they are already overtired, so the clock becomes the better guide.

Why are my baby’s naps only 30 minutes?

One sleep cycle. Usually a schedule mismatch or a sleep association that prevents linking cycles. Fix nights first, then naps.

When do babies drop to one nap?

Most between 14 and 18 months. Dropping earlier usually causes night waking.
