What are the right wake windows by age?

Roughly 45–90 minutes for newborns, 1.5–2.5 hours at 4–6 months, 2–3.5 hours at 6–9 months, 3–4 hours at 9–12 months, and 4–6 hours once a toddler is on one nap. These are rule-of-thumb starting points from practice, not research findings, and the right window for your baby depends on far more than age.

What a wake window is

A wake window is the stretch of time a baby is awake between one sleep and the next. The idea behind it is sleep pressure: the longer a baby is awake, the stronger the drive to sleep. Too little pressure and the baby lies in the crib chatting. Too much and the baby becomes overtired, fussy and harder to settle. The ranges below are where many babies of each age land.

Why the chart so often fails a real baby

  • Too short and too long look alike. A window that is too short and one that is too long both produce short naps and early waking. From the outside you cannot tell which one you have.
  • Timing is rarely the only cause. If your baby falls asleep being fed or rocked, perfect timing still produces a 30-minute nap. Parents spend weeks adjusting the clock when the problem is how the baby falls asleep.
  • Every change takes days to read. Sleep varies night to night anyway, so it is easy to chase noise.

Frequently asked

Are wake windows or sleepy cues more important?

Both are rough guides. By 4–5 months many babies stop showing clear cues until they are already overtired, and age-based windows are averages. A schedule written for your baby, adjusted with daily check-ins, is what Actually Sleeping provides.

Why are my baby’s naps only 30 minutes?

A 30-minute nap is about one sleep cycle. The usual causes are timing that is slightly off or a way of falling asleep that the baby cannot repeat between cycles. Working out which one applies to your baby is the job of a custom plan.

When do babies drop to one nap?

As a rule of thumb, most toddlers move to one nap somewhere in the second year. Dropping too early often shows up as night waking or early rising. Actually Sleeping coaches naps until they are solid, including through transitions.
