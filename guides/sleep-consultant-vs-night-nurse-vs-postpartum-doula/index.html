Sleep consultant vs night nurse vs postpartum doula: what’s the difference?

A night nurse cares for your baby overnight so you can sleep. A postpartum doula supports you and your household in the weeks after birth. A sleep consultant writes a plan and coaches you to change how your baby sleeps, while you do the nights. The first two give you rest now; a sleep consultant changes how your baby sleeps for good.

Night nurse or overnight newborn care

Someone comes to your home in the evening and cares for your baby until morning: bringing the baby to you for breastfeeds or giving bottles, changing, settling and putting the baby back down. Some are licensed nurses; many are experienced newborn care providers without a nursing license. The title is used loosely, so ask what training and licenses they hold. It gives you rest while it lasts. When it ends, your baby’s sleep is usually where it was.

Postpartum doula

A postpartum doula supports the parent in the weeks after birth. That can include feeding support and referrals, newborn care teaching, emotional support, light household help, and time for you to rest or shower. Some offer overnight shifts. The focus is your recovery, not your baby’s sleep habits.

Sleep consultant

A consultant assesses how your baby sleeps, writes a plan, and supports you while you carry it out. With a newborn the work is foundations: safe sleep, feeding on cue, a simple routine. Later it is teaching your baby to fall asleep independently, so nights stay good after the help ends. See what a sleep consultant does.

Using more than one

These roles fit together. A common sequence is overnight newborn care or a doula in the first weeks, then a sleep consultant once you want the nights to change for good. If you have overnight care while working with a consultant, share the plan so everyone responds to your baby the same way.

Questions to ask any of them

  • What training and licenses do you hold?
  • What exactly will you do, and what will I do?
  • How will you handle feeding, and how do you work with my pediatrician?

Safety applies to all of them

Whoever puts your baby down should follow AAP safe sleep guidance: alone, on the back, on a firm, flat, non-inclined surface, without soft toys, pillows, blankets or bedding, and never weighted swaddles or weighted objects. Ask any overnight helper to confirm this before the first night.

Frequently asked

Is a night nurse a real nurse?

Sometimes. Some overnight newborn care providers are licensed nurses; many are not. The title is used loosely. Ask what training and licenses they hold, and what they do if your baby seems unwell.

Can a night nurse sleep train my baby?

Some offer it, but it is not the core of the role, and the baby is learning to sleep with someone who will leave. A sleep consultant coaches you, so the change lasts. If you have both, share one written plan so every adult responds the same way.

Do I need a doula or a sleep consultant?

In the first weeks after birth, a doula supports your recovery and confidence. If what you want is for your baby’s sleep to change and stay changed, a sleep consultant is the right fit. Many families use both at different times.
