How do I protect my toddler’s sleep when a new baby arrives?

If you can, make big sleep changes, such as moving to a bed, well before the baby arrives; otherwise wait until the baby is settled at home, as the AAP advises. Keep your toddler’s bedtime routine the same, expect some regression, and give daily one-on-one time. Respond calmly to new night waking without starting habits you will later have to undo.

Timing big changes

The AAP’s advice on preparing your family for a new baby is to finish toilet training or switching from a crib to a bed before the baby arrives if you can. If that isn’t possible, put it off until after the baby is settled at home.

The same logic applies to moving rooms or dropping a nap. Your toddler should not lose their crib and gain a sibling in the same week. If you need the crib for the baby, buy or borrow a second crib or bassinet rather than rushing your toddler into a bed. A newborn can sleep in a bassinet in your room for the first months.

Expect some regression

The AAP says to expect your child to regress a little, such as toilet accidents or wanting a bottle again. It advises against telling them to act their age. Instead, give them the attention they need and praise grown-up behavior.

Sleep can follow the same pattern: more night waking, a harder bedtime, or wanting you to stay. That is normal. In practice, it often settles as family life finds a new rhythm.

Keep the bedtime routine the same

  • Same steps, same order, same time.
  • Same parent if possible, especially in the first weeks. If one parent is feeding the newborn at bedtime, the other takes the toddler.
  • Protect the special parts. The book, the song, the last hug. These matter more now, not less.
  • Plan around the newborn’s evening. A newborn may need feeding right at your toddler’s bedtime. Decide in advance who does what, so your toddler’s bedtime isn’t constantly interrupted.

One-on-one time

The AAP suggests setting aside special time for your child, such as reading, playing or talking together, or a one-on-one outing with a parent or another family member. Ten focused minutes a day, with the phone away, can reduce the need to get attention at bedtime.

Night waking

  • Respond calmly and briefly. Reassure, settle, and leave as you normally would.
  • Be careful with new habits. Lying down with your toddler or bringing them into your bed every night is easy to start when everyone is exhausted and hard to stop.
  • If the baby wakes the toddler, white noise at a low volume in the toddler’s room can help. Keep the machine across the room.

Safe sleep with a toddler and a newborn

  • The newborn sleeps in their own crib, bassinet or play yard. The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign lists a sibling sharing a baby’s sleep surface as a high-risk situation.
  • Keep toys out of the baby’s sleep space. Toddlers love to “share” blankets and stuffed animals. Explain that the baby’s bed stays empty, and check it before each sleep.
  • No bed sharing with the newborn, even when a toddler is in the bed too. The AAP does not support bed sharing under any circumstances.

When the toddler and baby will share a room

The AAP recommends your baby sleep in your room for at least the first 6 months. After that, siblings can share. See a baby and toddler sharing a room.

Frequently asked

Should I move my toddler to a bed before the baby comes?

Only if there is time for it to settle before the birth. The AAP advises finishing the switch before the baby arrives, or waiting until after the baby is settled at home. A second crib avoids rushing.

My toddler started waking at night after the baby came. Is this normal?

Yes. The AAP says to expect some regression after a new baby. Keep the routine the same, respond calmly and briefly, and add daily one-on-one time. In practice it often eases as the family adjusts to the new routine.

Can my toddler nap next to the newborn?

Not in the same sleep space. NIH Safe to Sleep lists a sibling sharing a baby’s sleep surface as a high-risk situation. The baby sleeps in their own crib, bassinet or play yard.
