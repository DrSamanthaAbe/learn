Does Benadryl (diphenhydramine) help a baby or toddler sleep, and is it safe?

No. Diphenhydramine should not be used to make a child sleepy. In a randomized trial of babies 6 to 15 months old who woke at night, it worked no better than a placebo. It can cause excitement instead of drowsiness in children, and too much can cause seizures or heart problems. Use it only for a reason your child’s pediatrician agrees with.

The short answer

Diphenhydramine is an older antihistamine. In adults it causes drowsiness, which is why it appears in many “PM” and nighttime products. Children are not small adults. The same medicine that makes a parent sleepy can make a toddler wired, and the trial evidence in babies shows it does not fix night waking.

What the evidence and the regulators say

  • The label position. The National Library of Medicine’s drug page states that diphenhydramine should not be used to make a child sleepy. It also says not to use it in children 2 to 5 years old unless a doctor directs it, and lists excitement, especially in children, as a side effect.
  • The trial. The TIRED study randomized babies 6 to 15 months old who woke two or more times a night to diphenhydramine or placebo. Its safety board stopped the trial early because the medicine was no more effective than placebo. Only 1 of 22 babies on the medicine improved, compared with 3 of 22 on placebo.
  • Overdose risk. The FDA warns that taking more than the recommended amount can lead to serious heart problems, seizures, coma or death. It asks families to store medicines out of children’s reach and sight, and to check labels, because many combination products also contain diphenhydramine.
  • Travel. CDC travel guidance for clinicians says antihistamines are contraindicated under age 1, and that antihistamines and decongestants have not been shown to help ear pain on flights.

Why it so often backfires

When an antihistamine does sedate, it changes how a child falls asleep for one night. It does not change how the child falls asleep the next night, or what happens between sleep cycles. If your child wakes at 1 and 4 a.m. because they fall asleep being fed or rocked at bedtime, a sedating medicine leaves that pattern untouched. Paradoxical excitement adds a wired, overtired child to the same problem.

Hidden diphenhydramine

Read the active ingredients on every allergy, cold, “nighttime” and itch product in the house. Giving two products that both contain diphenhydramine is one of the ways children get too much. Keep all of them locked up and out of sight.

What to ask the pediatrician

  • “Is there a medical reason my child is not sleeping, such as allergies, eczema itch, ear pain or snoring?”
  • “If my child needs an allergy medicine, which one fits their age, and is it one that affects sleep?”
  • “Is my child’s sleep within the normal range for their age, or is this something to evaluate?”

Frequently asked

Can I give Benadryl to my baby to sleep on a plane?

Not to make them sleep. Its drug label says diphenhydramine should not be used to make a child sleepy, and CDC travel guidance says antihistamines are contraindicated under age 1. Feeding or sucking during descent helps ear pressure. Ask your pediatrician before any travel medicine.

My toddler got hyper after Benadryl. Is that normal?

It is a known reaction. The National Library of Medicine lists excitement, especially in children, as a side effect of diphenhydramine. If your child had an unusual reaction, tell your pediatrician, and call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 if you are worried about the amount given.

Is a children’s nighttime cold medicine the same thing?

Many contain diphenhydramine or another antihistamine alongside other ingredients. Read the active ingredients. The FDA does not recommend over-the-counter cough and cold medicines for children under 2, and labels say not to use them under 4.

What should I do if my child swallowed Benadryl by accident?

Call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 right away, or 911 if your child is hard to wake, having a seizure or struggling to breathe. The FDA warns that too much diphenhydramine can cause serious heart problems and seizures.
