Can a sleep consultant help with a toddler, or is it too late?

It is not too late. Behavioral sleep methods have been studied in toddlers and young children as well as babies, and they work. Toddler plans look different: more talking, clear limits, a predictable routine, a plan for leaving the bed, and often a visual cue for morning. Expect more negotiation than with a baby; progress is often quick once the rules are consistent.

What the research covers

The AASM review of 52 studies covered bedtime problems and night wakings in infants and young children, not only babies. A randomized trial of a self-directed online program enrolled children 6 to 36 months and found faster sleep onset and fewer, shorter night wakings within three weeks. Toddlers can and do change their sleep.

What a good toddler plan includes

  • A short, fixed routine, often shown as a picture chart, so the order is not up for debate.
  • Limited choices inside the routine: this pajama or that one, this book or that one.
  • A plan for curtain calls. One planned extra (a last drink, a last hug), then the same calm response every time.
  • A plan for leaving the bed or climbing out. See toddler won’t stay in bed.
  • A morning signal, such as a light that changes color, so “is it morning?” has an answer.
  • Nap and bedtime timing. Many toddler bedtime battles are a toddler who is not tired yet. See the 2-year-old schedule.
  • Daytime practice. Talk about the new plan when everyone is calm, not at bedtime.

Common toddler situations a consultant sees

  • Bed sharing that worked for a while and now does not.
  • A move from crib to bed that came too early. See when to move to a toddler bed.
  • Bedtimes that stretch past an hour.
  • A new sibling, and an older child who suddenly wakes at night.
  • Separation anxiety at bedtime. See separation anxiety at bedtime.
  • Night waking that started with an illness and never stopped.

What is harder with a toddler

Habits are older, so the first few nights can be louder. Toddlers test whether the new rule is real. They also have more ways to delay. The trade-off is that toddlers understand explanations, respond to praise and simple rewards, and can see their own progress on a chart. Many families find toddlers change faster than they feared once both parents respond the same way.

Getting everyone on the same plan

Toddlers are quick to notice which adult gives in. Before night one, agree on the exact words and actions for each situation: calling out, getting up, asking for one more thing. Write them down and share them with anyone who does bedtime, including grandparents and sitters.

When to check with your pediatrician first

Loud snoring most nights, pauses in breathing, mouth breathing, very restless sleep, or daytime sleepiness that seems unusual. These can point to a medical cause. See sleep consultant vs pediatric sleep specialist.

Frequently asked

Is 2 too old for sleep training?

No. Two-year-olds can learn new sleep habits, and many respond well to clear routines, simple rules and rewards. The approach changes from a baby’s, with more explanation and more planning for stalling and leaving the bed.

Can a sleep consultant help my toddler stay in their bed?

Yes. A plan usually covers what you say and do each time your toddler gets up, a morning signal, and timing changes. Consistency from every adult at bedtime is what makes it work.

Should I move my toddler to a big bed to fix sleep?

Usually not. A move rarely fixes a sleep problem and can add one, because a toddler in a bed can get up. Sort out sleep in the crib first if your child is still in one.
