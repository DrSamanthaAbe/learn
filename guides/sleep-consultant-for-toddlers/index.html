Can a sleep consultant help with a toddler, or is it too late?

It is not too late. Behavioral sleep methods have been studied in toddlers and young children as well as babies, and they work. Toddler plans look different: more talking, clear limits, a predictable routine, a plan for leaving the bed, and often a cue for morning. Expect more negotiation than with a baby; progress is often quick once every adult responds the same way.

What the research covers

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine review of 52 studies covered bedtime problems and night wakings in infants and young children, not only babies. A randomized trial that enrolled 264 mothers of children aged 6 to 36 months found faster sleep onset and fewer, shorter night wakings, with improvements seen by one week and more by week two. Toddlers can and do change their sleep.

What a toddler plan has to account for

  • The routine. Short and fixed, so the order is not up for debate.
  • Curtain calls. The extra drink, hug and question, and how every adult responds to them.
  • Leaving the bed or climbing out. See toddler won’t stay in bed.
  • Mornings. A toddler who does not know when morning starts will test it at 5 a.m.
  • Nap and bedtime timing. Many toddler bedtime battles are a toddler who is not tired yet. See the 2-year-old schedule.
  • Every caregiver. Grandparents, sitters and both parents need the same plan.

Common toddler situations

  • Bed sharing that worked for a while and now does not.
  • A move from crib to bed that came too early. See when to move to a toddler bed.
  • Bedtimes that stretch past an hour.
  • A new sibling, and an older child who suddenly wakes at night.
  • Separation anxiety at bedtime. See separation anxiety at bedtime.
  • Night waking that started with an illness and never stopped.

What is harder, and what is easier

Habits are older, so the first few nights can be louder, and toddlers test whether the new rule is real. The upside is that toddlers understand explanations, respond to praise, and can see their own progress. Many families find toddlers change faster than they feared once every adult responds the same way, which is exactly what daily support helps you hold.

When to check with your pediatrician first

Loud snoring most nights, pauses in breathing, mouth breathing, very restless sleep, or daytime sleepiness that seems unusual. These can point to a medical cause. See sleep consultant vs pediatric sleep specialist.

Frequently asked

Is 2 too old for sleep training?

No. Two-year-olds can learn new sleep habits, and many respond well to clear routines, simple rules and praise. The approach changes from a baby’s, with more explanation and more planning for stalling and leaving the bed. Actually Sleeping works with babies and toddlers from newborn on.

Can a sleep consultant help my toddler stay in their bed?

Yes. A plan covers what every adult says and does each time your toddler gets up, how mornings start, and timing. Consistency from every adult at bedtime is what makes it work, and daily check-ins help you hold it.

Should I move my toddler to a big bed to fix sleep?

Usually not. A move rarely fixes a sleep problem and can add one, because a toddler in a bed can get up. If your child is still in a crib, it usually makes sense to sort out sleep there first.
