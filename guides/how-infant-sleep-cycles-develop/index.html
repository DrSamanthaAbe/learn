How do a baby’s sleep cycles and circadian rhythm develop?

Newborns have no day–night rhythm yet and sleep in short cycles spread around the clock. Over the first months the body clock develops, a nightly melatonin rhythm appears, and sleep consolidates toward night. Every cycle ends in a brief partial waking, which is why a baby who needs feeding or rocking to fall asleep often wakes and asks for it again through the night.

What changes in the first months

  • Short cycles. An infant’s full cycle of non-REM and REM sleep lasts about 50 minutes, compared with about 90 minutes in adults. A brief partial waking between cycles is normal at every age.
  • Active sleep first. Newborns fall asleep through active sleep, the newborn version of REM, which is one reason they rouse so easily.
  • The body clock arrives. The roughly 24-hour circadian rhythm develops over the first months, and with it a nightly melatonin rise. That is when the longest stretch of sleep starts to land at night.
  • Total sleep need. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 12–16 hours per 24 hours at 4–12 months and 11–14 hours at 1–2 years, naps included, and makes no recommendation under 4 months because normal sleep varies so widely.

Families often first contact Actually Sleeping when this maturing shows up as the “4-month regression.” It is not a phase to wait out. It is the point at which the way a baby falls asleep starts to matter at every cycle, and that is exactly what a plan written for your baby addresses.

Why this matters for night waking

Every baby surfaces briefly between cycles. The difference between a baby who sleeps through and one who wakes four times is what happens in that moment. A baby who fell asleep on their own can drift back. A baby who fell asleep feeding or rocking calls for the same thing again. The maturing of sleep in the first months does not create the problem; it reveals it.

Why general advice so often misses

Sleep biology is the same for every baby. What sits on top of it is not: feeding needs, temperament, how long a habit has existed, reflux or prematurity, siblings, work schedules. A tip that suits one baby can make another baby’s nights worse. That is why most families who come to Actually Sleeping have already tried things on their own.

Frequently asked

When do babies start producing melatonin?

A day–night melatonin rhythm develops over the first months of life, alongside the body clock. Melatonin supplements do not speed this up, and the AAP says to use them only after talking with your pediatrician.

Why does my baby wake so regularly through the night?

Each waking often lines up with the end of a sleep cycle. The waking itself is normal; the crying is a request for whatever helped your baby fall asleep at bedtime. Changing that is what a custom sleep plan does.

How much sleep does a baby need?

AASM consensus: infants 4–12 months, 12–16 hours per 24 hours including naps; toddlers 1–2 years, 11–14 hours; children 3–5 years, 10–13 hours. The AASM makes no recommendation under 4 months.
