Sleep training isn’t working. What should I do?

First rule out a medical cause with your pediatrician. If your baby is healthy and you have been consistent, the cause is nearly always one of five things: inconsistent responses, a baby still fed or rocked to sleep, a schedule that does not fit, night feeds without a plan, or a method that does not suit your baby’s temperament. Is it really not working?

Progress is rarely a straight line. These are common and do not mean failure on their own:

  • A second or third night that is worse than the first.
  • A burst of crying a few nights in, just before things settle.
  • Nights improving while naps and early mornings lag behind.
  • A short relapse in the second week.

Signs of real progress are less time to fall asleep, fewer or shorter wakings, or crying that is less intense. If none of those is moving after a fair stretch of consistency, something in the plan is wrong.

When it is medical

Stop and see your pediatrician if your baby screams as if in pain, snores, arches, has breathing pauses, is feeding poorly, or was doing well and suddenly is not. Illness, ear infections, reflux and breathing problems all disrupt sleep, and no sleep plan should work around them.

Other things that quietly undo progress

  • The room: light leaking in early, white noise on a timer that switches off, a room that is too warm.
  • New skills: rolling, sitting, pulling to stand. See the 8 to 10 month regression.
  • Different rules by day and night, or between caregivers.
  • Too many sleep crutches at once: a pacifier that needs replacing many times a night is a sleep association too.
  • Switching methods every few nights, which teaches a baby that persistence changes the rules.

Why a second generic attempt usually fails too

When a method stalls, the problem is usually in the details: timing that is off by 20 minutes, a feed in the wrong place, a response that works against this baby’s temperament. Those are hard to see from inside the house at 3 a.m., and a different method from the same kind of source does not reveal them. Frequently asked

How long should I try sleep training before giving up?

Look for progress in the time to fall asleep, the number of wakings or the intensity of crying. If none is moving after a fair stretch of consistency, the plan needs changing, not more effort. That is when most families contact Actually Sleeping.

Why is sleep training getting worse instead of better?

A temporary worsening a few nights in is common and often comes just before improvement. If it keeps getting worse, the usual reasons are inconsistent responses, a schedule that leaves your baby overtired or under-tired, a method that does not suit your baby, or illness.

Why did sleep training work and then stop?

Illness, travel, teething, a developmental leap or a nap transition can disrupt sleep, and old habits can return if the response changes during that time. An Actually Sleeping plan includes guidance for these disruptions so progress is not lost.

Can some babies not be sleep trained?

Nearly all healthy babies can learn to fall asleep independently, but not every baby responds to every method, and some have medical causes for waking. See your pediatrician, then get a plan written for your baby rather than trying another generic method.
