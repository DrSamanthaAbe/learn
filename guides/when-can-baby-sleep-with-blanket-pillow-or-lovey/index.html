When can my baby sleep with a blanket, pillow or stuffed animal?

Not before 12 months. The AAP says to keep blankets, pillows, stuffed toys and bumpers out of your baby’s sleep space for the first year. Its parent site notes that research hasn’t shown exactly when these items become completely safe, but most experts agree they pose little risk to healthy babies after 12 months. Start small: one small lovey, then a light blanket.

The first year: nothing but a fitted sheet

For babies up to 1 year, the AAP’s parent site says there should be no blankets, pillows, stuffed toys or bumper pads in the bed. Soft objects and loose bedding can increase the risk of entrapment, suffocation or strangulation. The CPSC’s shorthand is “Bare is Best”: nothing but a fitted sheet.

A sleep sack keeps your baby warm in place of a blanket. Dressing your baby in layers is safer than using blankets.

After 12 months

The AAP’s parent site is honest about the evidence: research has not shown when it is 100% safe to have these objects in the crib. It adds that most experts agree they pose little risk to healthy babies after 12 months. If your child was born early or has a medical condition, ask your pediatrician what applies to them.

Introducing a lovey

A lovey, a small comfort object, can help some toddlers settle and cope with separation at bedtime. To make one safer and more useful:

  • Choose small and simple. A small soft toy or a small cloth without long ribbons, strings, buttons or loose parts.
  • One, not a pile. A crib full of toys is something to climb on and something to throw out at 3 a.m.
  • Make it part of the routine. Hold it during the bedtime book and the cuddle, so it carries the feeling of that time.
  • Buy a spare. If it becomes important, a lost lovey on a trip is a hard night.

Pacifier holders and plush pacifiers

The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign says not to attach a pacifier to clothing, stuffed toys, blankets or other items. That includes pacifiers sewn into plush animals, at least in the first year. After 12 months, if you use one, treat it like any other soft toy and keep it small.

Blankets for toddlers

Many toddlers kick blankets off, so a sleep sack often keeps working well into the second and third year. When you introduce a blanket, start with a thin, small one tucked no higher than the chest. Expect your toddler to sleep on top of it for a while.

Pillows

The AAP’s safe-sleep guidance is written for the first year and doesn’t set an age for pillows. Toddlers don’t need one, and many families wait until the move to a bed. When you add one, choose a small, flat toddler pillow, not an adult pillow.

Frequently asked

Can my 10 month old sleep with a small lovey?

The AAP advises keeping stuffed toys and soft objects out of the sleep space for the first year. Wait until 12 months. Until then, you can use the lovey during the routine and remove it before sleep.

Is a muslin or breathable blanket okay before 12 months?

No. The AAP says no blankets in your baby’s sleep space for the first year. A sleep sack does the same job without loose fabric that can cover your baby’s face.

When can my toddler use a pillow?

There is no official age in the AAP’s safe-sleep guidance, which covers the first year. Toddlers don’t need a pillow. Many families add a small, flat one when moving to a bed.
