Are weighted sleep sacks and weighted swaddles safe for babies?

No. The AAP says weighted swaddles, weighted clothing and weighted objects on or near a baby are not safe and not recommended. The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign lists weighted blankets and swaddles among items that can pose dangers in the sleep area. Use an unweighted sleep sack, place your baby on their back, and stop swaddling once they try to roll.

What the AAP says

The AAP’s 2022 safe-sleep recommendations address this directly: “Weighted swaddles, weighted clothing or weighted objects on or near the baby are not safe and not recommended.” Its parent site puts it as an instruction: don’t use weighted blankets, sleepers, swaddles or other weighted objects on or near your baby.

The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign agrees. It lists weighted items, including weighted blankets, weighted swaddles and weighted objects, among the things that can pose dangers in a baby’s sleep area.

Why this matters even if a product is for sale

Some weighted infant products are still sold online and in stores, and they are often marketed as calming or as helping babies sleep longer. Being on a shelf does not mean a product meets safe-sleep guidance. The AAP also advises against relying on commercial products that claim to reduce the risk of SIDS or other sleep-related deaths.

What to use instead

  • A regular, unweighted sleep sack. It keeps your baby warm without loose bedding. Dressing a baby in layers is safer than using blankets.
  • A plain swaddle for young babies, only until your baby shows signs of trying to roll. The AAP says to stop swaddling at that point, and the AAP notes some babies start working on rolling as early as 2 months. See when and how to stop swaddling.
  • Back to sleep, every time, on a firm, flat surface with only a fitted sheet.

If you bought one hoping it would fix sleep

That is understandable. Parents usually reach for a weighted product when a baby startles awake, fights sleep, or wakes every hour after the swaddle comes off. Those problems have safer answers:

Startle reflex wakes a young babyA plain swaddle while it is still safe, then arms-out transition. White noise at a low volume.
Restless after the swaddle comes offExpect a few unsettled nights. A sleep sack and a consistent routine help.

A note on swaddle transitions

The weeks after the swaddle comes off are when weighted products are most tempting. Expect a few unsettled nights while your baby gets used to having their arms free. Keep the routine short and the same, put your baby down on their back in an arms-out sleep sack, and give the change a week before judging it. Many babies adjust faster than parents expect.

Weighted blankets for toddlers and older children

The AAP’s infant safe-sleep guidance covers babies up to 1 year. If you are considering a weighted blanket for an older child, for example one with sensory needs, ask your pediatrician first rather than deciding from product marketing.

When Actually Sleeping writes a plan for a baby who startles or fights sleep, it works within these rules: back to sleep, a bare crib, and nothing weighted.

Frequently asked

Is a lightly weighted sleep sack safer than a heavy one?

The AAP does not set a safe weight. Its guidance says weighted swaddles, weighted clothing and weighted objects on or near the baby are not safe and not recommended, without exceptions.

What about a sleep sack with a weighted chest pad?

That is still a weighted product on the baby. The AAP’s recommendation covers weighted clothing and swaddles, and it does not set a safe amount of weight. Use an unweighted sleep sack instead.

Are weighted blankets okay for my 2 year old?

The AAP’s infant safe-sleep rules apply to the first year. For a toddler, talk with your pediatrician before using any weighted blanket, especially if you are considering it to treat a sleep problem.
