Sleep at 2½ years: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 2½, the AASM range is still 11 to 14 hours per 24 hours, including naps, and most children still nap; about a third have stopped by age 3. Imagination is taking off, so fears, nightmares and night terrors become more common. Big-bed escapes are frequent, and the CDC lists turning doorknobs as a 30-month skill, so room safety matters.

What is changing at 2½

By 30 months, the CDC lists saying about 50 words, using things to pretend, following simple routines when told, and using the hands to twist things, like turning doorknobs or unscrewing lids. Pretend play means a richer inner world, and with it new fears of the dark, monsters and dreams.

The AAP says nightmares can begin as early as 6 months and peak between 3 and 12 years. Night terrors are most common in toddlers and preschoolers. It helps to know the difference.

What is normal

In a review of studies, 2 to 3 year olds averaged 12.0 hours per 24 hours. Among those who napped, naps averaged about 1.9 hours. About one in three children has stopped napping by age 3, so a 2½ year old who still naps is typical, and one who naps on some days is too. The AASM range for 1 to 2 year olds, 11 to 14 hours, still applies; its 3 to 5 year range of 10 to 13 hours starts at the third birthday.

What parents find hardest

  • Leaving the bed. Once in a big bed, many toddlers test it every night.
  • Fears at bedtime. Real to your child, and easy to turn into a nightly negotiation.
  • Naps that make bedtime late, while dropping the nap leaves an overtired evening.
  • Toilet training adding night trips and new reasons to call out.

Safe sleep at 2½

  • A doorknob-turner can leave the room. The AAP suggests a safety gate across the bedroom door and one at the top of the stairs.
  • Anchor dressers and shelves; latch or tape drawers so they cannot be used as steps (AAP).
  • Keep beds away from windows and use window guards above the first floor (AAP).

Night feeds

Night milk is rarely needed now, but your pediatrician decides. The AAP advises against a bottle in bed as part of its tooth decay prevention advice.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Snoring most nights, gasping or breathing pauses, or daytime sleepiness and behavior changes.
  • Night terrors that keep happening.
  • Fears that are severe, daily, and get in the way of daytime life.
  • Few words, no pretend play, or loss of skills.

Frequently asked

Is my 2½ year old having nightmares or night terrors?

Nightmares happen later in the night; your child wakes scared and wants comfort. Night terrors happen early in the night; your child screams but is not really awake. The AAP says to comfort after nightmares and stay calm and keep your child safe during terrors.

Should my 2½ year old still nap?

Most still do. A systematic review found about one in three children have stopped napping by age 3, and among nappers aged 2 to 3, naps averaged about 1.9 hours. Whether to keep, shorten or drop the nap depends on your child, and it is one of the decisions in a Actually Sleeping plan.

My toddler opens the bedroom door at night. Is that a safety issue?

It can be. The CDC lists turning doorknobs as a 30-month skill. The AAP suggests a safety gate across the bedroom door and at the top of stairs, anchored furniture, and window guards above the first floor.
