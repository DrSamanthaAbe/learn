How do you sleep train twins?

On a shared schedule, in a safe shared room with a separate sleep surface for each baby, with a plan that still treats each twin as an individual. One twin usually wakes the other less often than parents fear. Twins add logistics, two temperaments and half the parental capacity, which is why a plan built for both babies and daily support matter even more.

What makes twins different

  • Logistics. Two feeding patterns and two nap patterns can mean no one in the house ever sleeps if the babies drift apart.
  • Two temperaments. One twin may adapt in days and the other take longer. Holding the easier sleeper to the harder one’s pace, or the reverse, causes problems for both.
  • Noise. Parents worry that one baby will wake the other. Babies often get used to a roommate faster than adults expect.
  • Capacity. Twice as much to track every night with half as much energy to track it.

Why a single-baby method often stalls with twins

Most sleep advice is written for one baby and one set of parents taking turns. With twins, the parent responding to one baby is often needed by the other at the same moment. A response that works for a calm twin may not work for an intense one. And when one twin has a bad night, the plan for the other has to hold anyway. Twin families do best with a plan that decides these things in advance: who responds to which baby, what happens when both wake, and how to keep the two schedules together.

Safety with twins

Each baby needs their own safe sleep surface. The AAP says to avoid bed sharing under any circumstances, including with twins and other multiples. Room sharing without bed sharing is recommended for at least the first 6 months.

Frequently asked

Will one twin wake the other?

Occasionally, especially early on. Babies often get used to a roommate faster than adults expect. A plan written for both twins accounts for which baby wakes the other and how to respond.

Should twins sleep in the same crib?

No. Each baby needs their own safe sleep surface. The AAP says to avoid bed sharing under any circumstances, including with twins and other multiples, and recommends room sharing without bed sharing for at least the first 6 months.

Can twins be on different schedules?

They can, but it often doubles the work. Most families find a shared schedule easier, with room for each baby’s needs. Actually Sleeping writes one plan for both twins and adjusts it daily.
