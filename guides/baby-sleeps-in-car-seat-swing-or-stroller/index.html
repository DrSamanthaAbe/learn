What the AAP and CPSC say

The AAP’s 2022 recommendations say sitting devices, such as car seats, strollers, swings, infant carriers and slings, are not recommended for routine sleep, particularly for babies under 4 months. If your baby falls asleep in one, the AAP’s parent site says to move them to a firm sleep surface on their back as soon as possible.

The CPSC tells parents not to let a baby sleep in any inclined product over 10 degrees, such as a rocker, bouncer, swing or glider, and to move a sleeping baby to a crib, bassinet or play yard. Its 2018 to 2020 data showed an average of 15 deaths a year associated with inclined infant products not intended for sleep.

In the car is different

A properly installed car seat is where your baby belongs in a moving car, asleep or awake. The concern is what happens after the trip: a sleeping baby left in the car seat on the floor, carried inside and parked by the sofa for a long nap. When you arrive, take your baby out and move them to their sleep space, even if it wakes them.

A baby who only sleeps in motion has a fixable sleep problem, not a stubborn personality. Actually Sleeping moves sleep into the crib with a plan written for your baby, and coaches naps until they are solid.

Carriers and slings

Babywearing is a lovely way to settle a fussy baby. The AAP lists carriers and slings among sitting devices not recommended for routine sleep. If your baby falls asleep in one, the AAP’s advice is the same as for any sitting device: move them to a firm, flat sleep surface, on their back, as soon as you can.

Why babies sleep so well in these places

Motion, a snug fit, constant noise and being upright all feel calming. Many babies also fall asleep in them at the right moment by accident: the walk or drive happens to line up with the end of a wake window. The crib then gets all the hard moments, overtired or not tired enough, and seems to be the problem.

Over time, motion becomes part of how your baby falls asleep. Every move to the crib wakes them, because the conditions they fell asleep in have changed. Parents end up driving laps, pacing with the stroller or running the swing for every nap, and the transfer fails as often as it works. Frequently asked

Can my baby nap in the car seat if I watch them?

The AAP does not recommend sitting devices for routine sleep, especially under 4 months. Watching does not make the position safer. Move your baby to a crib, bassinet or play yard once you are out of the car.

Is the swing okay if it reclines fully flat?

Swings are not sleep products. The CPSC says not to let a baby sleep in a swing or other inclined product, and to move a sleeping baby to a crib, bassinet or play yard.

My baby wakes every time I transfer them. What should I do?

Transfers fail most often because your baby fell asleep in one place and surfaces in another. The lasting fix is a baby who falls asleep in the crib itself, so there is no transfer at all. That is the first skill a Actually Sleeping plan builds.
