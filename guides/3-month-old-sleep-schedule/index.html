What is a good sleep schedule for a 3 month old?

At 3 months most babies take 4 naps (some 3) and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake about 75 minutes to 2 hours. Total sleep varies widely; there is no official hour target under 4 months. Bedtime moves earlier, to roughly 7:30–8:30 p.m., and many babies still feed 1–3 times overnight.

What is changing developmentally

By about 3 months, melatonin and cortisol follow a day–night rhythm, so nights lengthen and bedtime settles earlier. Naps are the last piece to organize and are often still short and unpredictable. Many babies begin working on rolling, and hands come free as a self-soothing tool. Around 3 to 4 months sleep becomes more adult-like: babies start falling asleep through quiet sleep rather than active sleep, and regular sleep cycles develop, which is what parents often experience as the 4-month regression. How infant sleep cycles develop explains the change.

Common problems at this age

  • The swaddle transition: stop swaddling at the first signs of rolling, even if sleep gets worse for a few nights. Use an arms-free sleep sack; no loose blankets.
  • 30-minute naps: still within normal. Protect the wake windows and offer a short rescue in arms if the day falls apart. See short naps.
  • Feeding to sleep for every sleep: normal now, and a common root of waking later. Begin moving the feed to the start of the routine for one sleep a day. See the feed-to-sleep association.
  • Sudden frequent waking: it may be the early edge of the 4-month sleep regression.

Is this age right for sleep training?

For gentle shaping, yes. From about 10–12 weeks a baby can practice falling asleep in the crib with your support: consistent routine, dark room, drowsy or calm put-downs, feeds on cue. Formal sleep training generally waits until about 4 months and your pediatrician’s agreement. Frequently asked

How many naps should a 3 month old take?

Usually 4, sometimes 3 if naps run long. Total daytime sleep is typically 4–5 hours.

What is a good bedtime for a 3 month old?

Between about 7:30 and 8:30 p.m. for most. If the last catnap ends early, move bedtime earlier rather than stretching the final window past 2 hours.

Can a 3 month old sleep through the night?

A few sleep a long stretch; many still need 1–3 feeds. Both are normal. Night-feed decisions depend on growth and belong to your pediatrician.
