What is the chair method of sleep training?

In the chair method you put your baby down awake and sit in a chair beside the crib until they fall asleep, then move the chair farther away every 2 to 3 nights until you are out of the room. It usually takes 10 to 14 nights and often up to 3 weeks. It works best from about 6 months through the toddler years and suits parents who want to stay present, but it is not tear-free.

How it works, step by step

NightsChair positionWhat you do
1–3Beside the cribQuiet voice, occasional touch or pat; no picking up to sleep
4–6Halfway to the doorVoice only; go to the crib briefly if truly needed, then back to the chair
7–9In the doorwayOccasional quiet reassurance
10–12In the hall, in viewMinimal voice
13–14Out of sightBrief verbal check-ins from the door if needed

At night wakings, return to wherever the chair is that night and do the same thing. The method is also known as camping out or gradual withdrawal.

Rules that make it work

  • Be boring. Sit, stay calm, keep words few and repetitive. Eye contact and conversation keep babies awake.
  • Fade your help within each stage. Less touching on night 3 than on night 1.
  • Move the chair on schedule, even if the last position has not become easy. Staying in one spot for a week creates a new habit: needing you in the chair.
  • Stay until asleep in the early stages, then leave quietly. Later, leave while your baby is drowsy but awake.
  • One adult per night. Swapping mid-bedtime restarts the protest.

What to expect

In practice, crying is usually less intense than with leave-the-room methods, but it can last as long or longer on the first nights, because some babies are frustrated that you are right there and not picking them up. Many families see 20 to 60 minutes on night 1, and a fresh protest on each night the chair moves. Most families see clear improvement by the end of week 1 and finish in 2 to 3 weeks.

Does it work?

The chair method has less direct trial evidence than extinction, graduated extinction or bedtime fading, which are the approaches the 2006 American Academy of Sleep Medicine review lists with support. It rests on the same principle: the child does the final step of falling asleep while your help is reduced. A large Australian randomized trial in which nurses taught parents behavioral settling techniques found fewer infant sleep problems and fewer maternal depression symptoms at 10 and 12 months, and no differences in children’s emotional health or the parent–child relationship at age 6.

Pros and cons

  • Pros: you are present throughout; changes are gradual; works well for toddlers and for children with separation anxiety; easy to adapt when room-sharing.
  • Cons: slow; demands patience at 2 a.m.; your presence stimulates some babies, especially under about 6 months; easy to stall at one position.

If it stalls

The usual causes are staying too long at one position, doing too much from the chair (picking up, lying down next to the crib, holding hands through the slats), or a schedule problem that makes falling asleep hard in the first place. Check wake windows, then tighten the steps. If your baby is more upset with you in the room than out of it, a check-based method may suit them better.

Frequently asked

How long does the chair method take?

As a rule of thumb, 10 to 14 nights to move the chair out of the room, and up to 3 weeks for sleep to be fully settled. Naps can take a similar time.

Is the chair method the same as camping out?

Yes. Camping out, gradual withdrawal and the chair method describe the same approach: a parent sits near the crib and moves away every few nights until they are out of the room.

Does the chair method work for toddlers?

Yes. It is one of the more useful methods from about 12 months to 3 years, including for toddlers in a bed, where the parent can calmly return the child and sit back down.

Can I touch my baby during the chair method?

In the first 2 to 3 nights, brief patting or a hand on the chest is fine. Reduce it each night so that your touch does not become the new way your baby falls asleep.
