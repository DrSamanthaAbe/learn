How long should my baby sleep in our room?

The AAP recommends your baby sleep in your room, in their own crib or bassinet, for at least the first 6 months. After that, the choice is yours. Room sharing without bed sharing helps protect against SIDS. One large study linked room sharing at 4 and 9 months with less night sleep, so many families move their baby out after 6 months.

What the AAP says

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that parents sleep in the same room as their baby, but not in the same bed, for at least the first 6 months. The baby sleeps on their back in their own crib, bassinet or play yard, close to your bed. The AAP’s parent site says room sharing can lower the risk of SIDS by as much as 50%, and that it is safer than bed sharing.

Six months is the minimum, not a deadline. Nothing in the guidance says you must move your baby at 6 months, and nothing says you must keep them in your room after it.

What happens to sleep after 6 months

A 2017 study in Pediatrics followed first-time mothers and their babies. At 9 months, babies who had their own room before 4 months slept 40 minutes more per night than babies still room sharing, and their longest stretch was 100 minutes longer. Room-sharing families also had about four times the odds of moving to overnight bed sharing at 4 and 9 months.

That study was observational. It shows an association, not proof that the room caused the difference. Still, the pattern matches what many parents notice: you hear every grunt, you respond to sounds a baby would have slept through, and your baby hears you come to bed.

Signs it may be time to move your baby

  • Your baby is past 6 months and you wake each other several times a night.
  • You go in at every noise, and your baby has learned to expect it.
  • Your baby wakes when you come to bed or turn over.
  • You find yourself bringing your baby into your bed in the early hours.
  • Your own sleep is suffering more than the convenience is worth.

How to make the move

  1. Set up the room first. A crib with only a fitted sheet, dark curtains, and white noise if you use it at a low volume. All the safe-sleep rules still apply in the new room until your baby’s first birthday.
  2. Start with naps. A few days of naps in the new room makes the first night less strange.
  3. Keep the routine identical. Same order, same song, same sleep sack. Only the room changes.
  4. Pick a calm week. Avoid moving rooms during travel, illness or another big change.
  5. Use a monitor if it helps you rest, but you do not need to watch it all night.

In practice, many babies adjust within a few nights, and some sleep better from the start because the room is quieter.

If you can’t move your baby out

Small homes, shared rooms and travel are real. If room sharing is staying, a few things help: place the crib as far from your bed as is practical while you can still reach it easily, use a screen or curtain so your baby can’t see you, keep the room dark, and wait a moment before responding to small noises. You can still teach your baby to fall asleep independently; see sleep training while room sharing.

Frequently asked

Is it unsafe to move my baby to their own room before 6 months?

The AAP recommends room sharing for at least the first 6 months because it helps protect against SIDS. Moving earlier goes against that guidance. If you are considering it, talk it through with your pediatrician first.

Does room sharing mean the baby can sleep in our bed?

No. Room sharing means your baby sleeps in their own crib, bassinet or play yard in your room. The AAP does not support bed sharing under any circumstances, including with twins.

Will my baby sleep worse after moving rooms?

Usually not for long. Expect a night or two of adjustment if anything. Keeping the routine, sleep sack and white noise the same makes the room the only new thing.
