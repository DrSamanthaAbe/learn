Why parents worry about feeds

Many parents assume sleep training means cutting night feeds. If your baby still needs to eat, or you are breastfeeding and want to keep it that way, that assumption makes any sleep help feel off limits.

The families in this video include exclusively breastfed babies, babies nursed to sleep every time, and babies who needed a bottle to settle. Their stories show that feeding and better sleep are not in conflict.

How Actually Sleeping handles night feeds

The plan never forces night feeds out. Whether and when feeds change is your pediatrician’s call. Dr. Sam writes a plan that works with your baby’s feeding, whether breast, bottle or both, and the bootcamp-style coaching before night one covers how feeds fit into your nights. Daily 1:1 check-ins follow, and naps are coached until solid.

Families in this video started from very different places: five feeds a night, nursing to sleep every time, co-sleeping and nursing all night. Actually Sleeping built a different plan for each of them, and their feeding choices stayed their own.

Frequently asked

Will I have to stop night feeds?

No. Actually Sleeping never forces night feeds out. Feeding decisions belong to your pediatrician. Several families in the reviews kept night feeds while sleep improved, and one mom says her daughter dropped them on her own.

Can I keep breastfeeding?

Yes. Many families in the reviews were breastfeeding, including babies who nursed to sleep every time. Dr. Sam writes the plan around your feeding, and nothing in it asks you to stop breastfeeding.

My baby feeds to sleep. Is that a problem?

It is one of the most common starting points in the reviews. Dr. Sam’s plan takes into account how your baby falls asleep now, including feeding to sleep, and the coaching before night one prepares you for exactly what to expect.
