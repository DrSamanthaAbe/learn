Can I give cough and cold medicine at bedtime to a baby or toddler?

For young children, generally no. The FDA does not recommend over-the-counter cough and cold medicines for children under 2, product labels say not to use them under 4, and the AAP says to use them at 4 to 6 only if your child’s doctor recommends it. Saline, suction, a cool-mist humidifier and, after age 1, honey are the usual night-time helpers.

The short answer

A coughing, stuffy child at 10 p.m. is hard on everyone, and the medicine aisle looks like the answer. For babies and young toddlers it is not. These products do not shorten a cold, and combination products make it easy to double up on an ingredient.

Colds pass. What often lasts is the sleep that slipped during them. Actually Sleeping helps families with that part, with a plan written by Dr. Sam, a pharmacist who takes medicine questions seriously and sends medical ones to your pediatrician.

What the regulators say

  • FDA: does not recommend over-the-counter cough and cold medicines for children younger than 2. Manufacturers label them “do not use in children under 4 years of age.” The FDA also advises not to give homeopathic cough and cold medicine to children younger than 4.
  • FDA: cough or cold medicine “will not change the natural course of a cold or make it go away faster.”
  • AAP: over-the-counter cough and cold medicine is not recommended under 4; from 4 to 6, use only if your child’s doctor recommends it.
  • AAP: many children with a cough or stuffy nose are happy, play normally and sleep well. Treat symptoms when they cause discomfort or trouble sleeping.

Night-time comfort that is recommended

  • Saline: plain saline drops or spray to loosen mucus, followed by gentle suction for babies. Squeeze the bulb before placing it in the nostril. Suctioning before feeds and sleep helps.
  • Cool-mist humidifier near the sleep space but out of reach, cleaned as directed. The FDA advises against warm-mist humidifiers.
  • Fluids through the day, which thin mucus.
  • Honey for children 1 year and older. The AAP says research shows honey is better than store-bought cough syrups at reducing how often and how badly children cough at night. Never give honey to a baby under 12 months; CDC warns it can cause botulism.
  • Mentholated chest rub only for children 2 and older, per the AAP, and stored out of reach.

Safe sleep stays the same when your baby is sick

The AAP says babies should always sleep flat on their backs on a firm surface, and that propping a baby on towels or pillows or inclining the mattress is not safe, even with congestion. Swings, car seats and carriers are not safe for sleep.

When to call

  • The FDA lists a fever of 100.4°F or higher in a baby 2 months or younger, or 102°F or higher at any age, as reasons to call.
  • The AAP says call 911 for a baby struggling for each breath or with bluish lips, and call the doctor for breathing much faster than normal or coughing that stops your baby from sleeping, eating or playing.

What to ask the pediatrician

  • “Is this a cold, or could it be an ear infection, croup or asthma?”
  • “Which fever or pain reliever, if any, is right for my child’s age and weight?”
  • “Is honey okay for my child, and how much?”

Why sleep stays broken after the cold is gone

A sick child deserves comfort, freely given. The trouble is that comfort often changes how a child falls asleep: extra feeds, rocking, a parent’s bed. Frequently asked

What can I give my 1 year old for a cough at night?

The AAP suggests honey for children 1 and older, saline and suction for congestion, a cool-mist humidifier and fluids. Over-the-counter cough and cold medicines are not recommended for this age. Ask your pediatrician how much honey to use.

Can I use vapor rub on my baby?

The AAP lists mentholated rubs for children 2 years and older only. For younger babies, use saline drops, gentle suction and a cool-mist humidifier, and ask your pediatrician about anything else.

Should I raise the crib mattress for a stuffy baby?

No. The AAP says propping a baby on towels or pillows or inclining the mattress is not safe, even with congestion. Keep your baby flat on the back on a firm surface and use saline and suction before sleep.

Do cough medicines help children sleep?

The FDA says they do not change the course of a cold and does not recommend them under 2; labels say not to use them under 4. The AAP notes honey works better than store-bought syrups for night coughing in children over 1.
