Is melatonin safe for babies and toddlers?

No for babies, and not recommended for toddlers without a pediatrician’s direction. Melatonin is a hormone, it is sold as an unregulated supplement in the United States with inconsistent actual doses, and infants’ own melatonin rhythm is still developing. A baby who will not sleep almost always has a behavioral or schedule cause that melatonin does not fix.

Why a pharmacist says no

  • It is a hormone, not a sleep aid. Melatonin signals time of day to the body. It does not sedate; it shifts the clock. Infants are still building that clock — most do not produce a consistent nighttime melatonin rise until roughly 3 to 4 months — and there is no good evidence for adding hormone on top of that process.
  • Supplements are not drugs. In the U.S., melatonin is sold as a dietary supplement, so products are not held to the manufacturing and labeling standards prescription or over-the-counter medicines are. Independent testing has repeatedly found gummies and tablets whose real content differed widely from the label, sometimes by several times.
  • Accidental ingestions are rising. Poison-control reports involving children and melatonin, especially gummies that look like candy, rose sharply over the past decade. That is a storage issue as much as a dosing one; keep it where a toddler cannot reach it.
  • It treats the wrong problem. Babies who wake every two hours are usually not short on melatonin. They have a sleep association, a schedule mismatch, or both — things a plan fixes and a gummy does not.

When a pediatrician might consider it

For some older children with specific conditions (for example certain neurodevelopmental disorders) a pediatrician or sleep specialist may use melatonin deliberately, with a chosen product and timing. That is a medical decision made for a specific child, not a parenting shortcut.

What to do instead

Protect the baby’s own melatonin rhythm: bright light and activity in the morning, dim light the hour before bed, a dark room overnight, a consistent bedtime, and a way of falling asleep that does not depend on you. Those four things do more for a baby’s clock than any supplement.

Frequently asked

Can I give my 1 year old melatonin?

Not without your pediatrician’s direction. Most toddler sleep problems are behavioral and respond to a consistent plan within days.

Does melatonin have side effects in children?

Reported effects include morning grogginess, headache, vivid dreams and, with larger accidental doses, drowsiness severe enough to warrant medical attention. Long-term effects on developing children have not been adequately studied.

Are ‘natural’ sleep gummies for toddlers safe?

‘Natural’ is a marketing word, not a safety standard. Check the ingredients; many contain melatonin or herbs with no pediatric safety data.
