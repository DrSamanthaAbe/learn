What is a good sleep schedule for a 2 month old?

At 2 months most babies take 4–5 naps and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake 60–90 minutes at a time. Total sleep varies widely; there is no official hour target under 4 months. Bedtime is still on the late side, around 8–9:30 p.m., and night feeds on cue are normal and expected.

Treat every time below as a starting point. Shift the whole day to match your baby’s actual wake-up time, then adjust one thing at a time in 15-minute steps and hold it for three days before judging.

What is changing developmentally

The body clock is starting to work: circadian rhythms develop around 2 to 3 months. Night sleep begins to consolidate into one longer stretch, usually the first of the night, while daytime naps stay irregular for another couple of months. Social smiling arrives, awake time becomes more alert, and the fussing that typically peaks at about 6 weeks starts to ease, usually by 3 to 4 months. Bedtime drifts earlier week by week; follow it rather than forcing it.

The AASM consensus gives no sleep-duration recommendation under 4 months because normal variation is so wide, so judge by your baby’s mood, feeding and growth rather than by an hour count.

Common problems at this age

  • Short naps: 30–45 minute naps are developmentally normal at 2 months and are not something to fix yet. See why naps are only 30 minutes.
  • Naps only in arms: common. Practice the first nap of the day in the bassinet, where sleep pressure helps most. Later steps are in how to stop contact napping.
  • Overtired evenings: a window stretched past 90 minutes usually shows up as a hard bedtime. Check wake windows by age.
  • Swaddle safety: some babies show the first signs of rolling around now. Stop swaddling at the first sign and move to an arms-free sleep sack. Sleep stays on the back, on a flat, firm surface, in an empty crib or bassinet.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Not formal sleep training; that generally starts from about 4 months. From roughly 10–12 weeks you can begin gentle shaping: a consistent wind-down, a dark room, and putting your baby down drowsy or calm for one sleep a day, always responding and feeding on cue. Frequently asked

How long should a 2 month old sleep at night without feeding?

It varies widely. Many begin to manage one longer stretch early in the night, and some go longer. How long your baby may go between night feeds depends on weight gain and feeding, so confirm with your pediatrician.

What time should a 2 month old go to bed?

Usually between 8 and 9:30 p.m. Bedtime moves earlier over the next two months as night sleep consolidates.

How long can a 2 month old stay awake?

A common rule of thumb is about 60–90 minutes, counting the feed. Past that, many 2 month olds become overtired and harder to settle. Watch your own baby’s cues too.

Is it too early for a routine?

No. A 5–10 minute wind-down in the same order before sleep is helpful at any age. A fixed clock schedule is what is still too early.
