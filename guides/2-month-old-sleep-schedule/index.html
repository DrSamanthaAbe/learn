What is a good sleep schedule for a 2 month old?

At 2 months most babies take 4–5 naps and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake 60–90 minutes at a time. Total sleep varies widely; there is no official hour target under 4 months. Bedtime is still on the late side, around 8–9:30 p.m., and night feeds on cue are normal and expected.

What is typical at 2 months

At 2 months naps do not land at the same times every day yet, and night sleep is broken by feeds. The ranges below are what is common, not a timetable.

MeasureTypical range
Total sleep per 24 hoursVaries widely; the AASM gives no recommendation under 4 months
What studies of healthy babies foundLongest stretch at 0–5 months averaged 5.7 hours, with a normal spread of about 2 to nearly 10 hours (Galland 2012)
Night sleep (common practice range)Broken by feeds; often one longer first stretch of the night begins to appear
Naps (common practice range)4–5, anywhere from 30 minutes to 2 hours
Wake windows (rule of thumb, not a research finding)60–90 minutes, including feeding time
Night feedsTypically 2–4 a night, on cue. Whether to keep, reduce or drop night feeds is your pediatrician’s decision.

Rows marked AASM are the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommendation, which the AAP supports. Rows marked Galland 2012 come from a systematic review of observational studies. The other rows are common practice ranges, not research findings. They describe an average baby; healthy babies sit on both sides of every one of them.

What is changing developmentally

The body clock is starting to work: circadian rhythms develop around 2 to 3 months, and night sleep begins to consolidate from about 2 months, often as one longer stretch early in the night. Daytime naps stay irregular for longer. Social smiling arrives, awake time becomes more alert, and the fussing that the AAP says usually peaks by 6 weeks begins to ease, declining by 3 to 4 months.

The AASM consensus gives no sleep-duration recommendation under 4 months because normal variation is so wide, so your baby’s mood, feeding and growth tell you more than an hour count.

Why the average 2-month schedule often does not fit

  • Short naps: 30–45 minute naps are common at 2 months. See why naps are only 30 minutes.
  • Naps only in arms: common, and the reason clock times on a page rarely hold. See contact napping.
  • Overtired evenings: the same window that suits one baby overtires another. See wake windows by age.
  • Feeding comes first: a hungry baby feeds, whatever the schedule says. Any plan has to bend around that.

Safe sleep at 2 months

  • Stop swaddling at the first sign of trying to roll. The AAP says some babies start working on rolling as early as 2 months. Move to an arms-free sleep sack, never a weighted one.
  • Sleep stays on the back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib or bassinet, in your room.

Not with anything that drops feeds or leaves a 2 month old to cry. Foundations can start now. Frequently asked

How long should a 2 month old sleep at night without feeding?

It varies widely. In studies, the longest stretch at 0 to 5 months averaged 5.7 hours, from about 2 to nearly 10. How long your baby may go between night feeds depends on weight gain and feeding, so confirm with your pediatrician.

What time should a 2 month old go to bed?

As a common rule of thumb, between 8 and 9:30 p.m. Bedtime usually moves earlier over the next two months as night sleep consolidates. The right time for your baby depends on naps and feeds, which a custom plan accounts for.

How long can a 2 month old stay awake?

A common rule of thumb is about 60–90 minutes, counting the feed. It is a rule of thumb, not a research finding, and babies differ. Reading your own baby’s limit is part of what Dr. Sam works out with you.

Is it too early for a routine?

No. A short, predictable wind-down is part of the foundations that can start now. A fixed clock schedule is what is still too early. Actually Sleeping starts families on foundations from the newborn stage, with night feeds left in place.
