Is there a 2 year old sleep regression?

Yes. Around age 2, many toddlers who slept well start fighting bedtime, refusing the nap, waking at night or rising early. It is driven by development (independence, language, imagination and new fears) and often by a change such as a new sibling, toilet training or a move to a bed. Nearly all 2-year-olds still need their nap, and habits started during the regression often outlast it.

What it looks like

  • Bedtime stretches from 20 minutes to an hour of requests: water, another book, a different blanket.
  • Nap refusal: playing or shouting through the whole nap period.
  • Night waking with calling out, or appearing at your bedside if in a bed.
  • New fear of the dark, shadows or being alone.
  • Waking for the day at 5 a.m.

The nap is not finished

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 11–14 hours of sleep per 24 for ages 1–2 and 10–13 hours for ages 3–5, naps included. A large review found fewer than 2.5% of children stop napping before age 2, while 94% have stopped by 5. A 2-year-old refusing the nap for a week or two is on strike, not finished. Dropping the nap too early usually makes evenings and nights harder, not easier.

Why it sticks

The development behind the regression settles. What lasts is what grew up around it: lying with your child until they fall asleep, extra books every night, a later bedtime, a nap that was dropped. If it has gone on longer than six weeks, it has usually stopped being a regression and become the new routine. Bedtime “scripts” and charts from the internet often fail here because they assume an average toddler, and because two tired parents rarely hold them the same way.

Regression or something else?

Ask your pediatrician about regular snoring, mouth breathing or pauses in breathing, restless sleep with sweating, pain, or a sleep change alongside loss of skills. Night terrors (screaming while still asleep, no memory in the morning) are different from nightmares and are worth mentioning too. Related: the 18 month regression.

Frequently asked

How long does the 2 year sleep regression last?

There is no research-defined length. It lasts longer when new habits, such as lying with the child until asleep, begin during it. Once it has gone on for more than a few weeks it has usually become the new routine, and that is what a Actually Sleeping plan addresses.

Is my 2 year old ready to stop napping?

Probably not. In a large review, fewer than 2.5% of children had stopped napping before 2; most stop during the preschool years. A week or two of refusal is usually a nap strike, not the end of the nap.

Should I move my 2 year old to a bed during the regression?

No, unless there is a safety reason such as climbing out despite a lowered mattress. Changing beds in the middle of a regression usually makes both harder.

Why is my 2 year old suddenly scared at bedtime?

Imagination develops quickly around this age and fears come with it. Take the fear seriously and keep the response calm and predictable. If fears are intense or show up during the day too, mention them to your pediatrician.
