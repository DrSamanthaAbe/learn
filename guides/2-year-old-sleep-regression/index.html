Is there a 2 year old sleep regression?

Yes. Around age 2, often anywhere from 22 to 30 months, many toddlers who slept well start fighting bedtime, refusing the nap, waking at night or rising early. It is driven by development — independence, language, imagination and new fears — and often by a change such as a new sibling, toilet training or a move to a bed. With steady boundaries it typically passes in 2–6 weeks; a 2-year-old still needs a nap.

What it looks like

  • Bedtime stretches from 20 minutes to an hour of requests: water, another book, a different blanket.
  • Nap refusal: playing or shouting through the whole nap period.
  • Night waking with calling out, or appearing at your bedside if in a bed.
  • New fear of the dark, shadows or being alone.
  • Waking for the day at 5 a.m.

Do not drop the nap

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 11–14 hours of sleep per 24 for ages 1–2 and 10–13 hours for ages 3–5, naps included. Most children nap until somewhere between 3 and 5. A 2-year-old refusing the nap for a week or two is on strike, not finished.

  1. Offer the nap every day at the same time, usually starting around 12:30–1 p.m.
  2. Keep your child in the crib or room for at least an hour of quiet time, asleep or not.
  3. On no-nap days, move bedtime 30–60 minutes earlier.
  4. If the nap is happening but bedtime has become very late, cap the nap at 1.5–2 hours and end it by about 3 p.m.

A schedule that fits most 2-year-olds

  • Wake between 6 and 7 a.m.
  • One nap of 1.5–2.5 hours beginning about 5–6 hours after waking.
  • Bedtime between 7 and 8 p.m., about 4.5–5.5 hours after the nap ends.

Bedtime boundaries that work at this age

  1. Front-load attention. Fifteen minutes of one-to-one play before the routine.
  2. A picture chart of the routine. The chart is the boss, which takes the argument away from you.
  3. Requests go inside the routine: last drink, last toilet trip, two books chosen by your child.
  4. One clear ending phrase, then leave.
  5. Brief, boring responses afterwards. In a bed, a silent walk back every time. See my toddler won’t stay in bed.
  6. Screens off for at least an hour before bed.

Regression or something else?

Ask your pediatrician about regular snoring, mouth breathing or pauses in breathing, restless sleep with sweating, pain, or a sleep change alongside loss of skills. Night terrors (screaming while still asleep, no memory in the morning) are different from nightmares and are worth mentioning too.

If it has gone on longer than six weeks, it has usually stopped being a regression and become the new routine. Actually Sleeping works with toddlers up to age 3, and a plan at this age is mostly about clear, kind limits that both parents hold the same way. Related: the 18 month regression.

Frequently asked

How long does the 2 year sleep regression last?

Typically 2–6 weeks when boundaries stay consistent. It lasts longer when new habits, such as lying with the child until asleep, begin during it.

Is my 2 year old ready to stop napping?

Probably not. Most children nap until 3–5 years. Keep offering the nap and use quiet time on days it does not happen.

Should I move my 2 year old to a bed during the regression?

No, unless there is a safety reason. Changing beds in the middle of a regression usually makes both harder.

Why is my 2 year old suddenly scared at bedtime?

Imagination develops quickly around this age and fears come with it. Acknowledge the fear briefly, offer a dim night light, and keep the routine and response the same.
