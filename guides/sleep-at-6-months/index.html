Sleep at 6 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 6 months, the AASM range is 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps. The AAP says it is normal for a 6 month old to wake briefly at night and go back to sleep within a few minutes. In studies, babies 6 to 11 months averaged about 2 daytime naps. Solids usually start around now, and room-sharing is recommended for at least the first 6 months.

What is changing at 6 months

By 6 months, the CDC lists rolling from tummy to back, leaning on the hands to support sitting, knowing familiar people, and laughing. Sitting and rolling give your baby new things to practise at 2 a.m.

Several things also wind down around now. The AAP says periodic breathing of infancy is generally outgrown by about 6 months, and colicky crying, which can last until 6 months, is usually over. Separation anxiety can begin in the second half of the first year, and the AAP notes that nightmares can start as young as 6 months.

Many 6 month olds take 3 naps and are heading toward 2. That is a common pattern, not a rule.

What parents find hardest

  • Waking every 2 hours. Brief waking is normal; needing a full rescue every time usually points to how your baby falls asleep at bedtime.
  • Sitting up in the crib and not knowing how to lie back down. Daytime practice helps.
  • The room-sharing decision. The AAP minimum is about to pass. Some families move the crib now, others later. Both are reasonable if the sleep space is safe.
  • New foods causing a few unsettled nights or changes in digestion.

Safe sleep at 6 months

  • Back to sleep until the first birthday. If your baby rolls both ways, you can leave them in the position they choose, with nothing else in the crib.
  • The NICHD reports more than 90% of SIDS deaths happen before 6 months, but it and the AAP both say safe sleep practices continue to age 1.
  • Lower the mattress before your baby can sit unassisted, and to the lowest setting before standing.
  • No bottle in bed. The AAP says putting a baby down with milk, formula or juice risks tooth decay; feed first, then put down.

Night feeds

Some healthy 6 month olds still take a night feed. Whether to keep it, reduce it or drop it is your pediatrician’s decision. If the pediatrician says feeds are no longer needed, change them gradually.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Frequent snoring, gasping, or loud or heavy breathing in sleep. The AAP lists snoring and sleep apnea among common childhood sleep problems.
  • Fever above 101°F (38.3°C), or waking with ear pulling and fever.
  • Not rolling, or not reaching for toys, by 6 months.
  • Any question about starting solids, which the AAP advises not before about 6 months.

Frequently asked

Is it normal for a 6 month old to wake at night?

Yes. The AAP says it is normal for a 6 month old to wake during the night and go back to sleep after a few minutes. Studies of babies 3 to 6 months recorded 0 to 3 wakings as within the normal range. Frequent wakings that each need a parent are worth addressing.

Should my 6 month old move to their own room?

The AAP recommends room-sharing for at least the first 6 months. After that it is a family decision. Wherever your baby sleeps, keep them on the back on a firm, flat, empty surface until the first birthday.

How many naps should a 6 month old take?

There is no rule. In a review of studies, babies 6 to 11 months averaged 2.2 daytime naps, with a normal spread from about 1 to 3.5. Many 6 month olds take 3 naps and move to 2 over the next couple of months.
