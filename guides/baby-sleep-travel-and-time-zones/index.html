How do I keep my baby sleeping while traveling or changing time zones?

Bring the sleep environment with you (dark, white noise, the usual sleep sack and routine) and keep the same way of falling asleep. For a change of 1–2 time zones or a trip of only a few days, staying on home time is often easiest; for 3 or more, switch to local time on arrival and use daylight, meals and capped naps. Adult clocks shift about 1 hour a day going east.

Safe sleep away from home

  • Same rules as home: on the back, on a firm, flat surface, alone, nothing else in the sleep space.
  • Bring or request a crib or play yard that meets current safety standards. Inspect a hotel or rental crib: firm, snug-fitting mattress, no broken or missing parts, no added padding. Use a play yard as the manufacturer supplied it, with no extra mattress or padding added.
  • Not an adult bed, sofa, or cushion nest, even for one night. Couches and armchairs are never safe places for babies to sleep.
  • Car seats and strollers are for travel. Sleeping in a car seat while riding is expected; when you arrive, move your baby to a firm, flat sleep surface as soon as you can.
  • Scan the room: blind cords, loose cables, anything a baby can reach from the crib.

Packing list for sleep

  • Sleep sack and a crib sheet from home, unwashed, for a familiar smell.
  • Portable white-noise machine.
  • Portable blackout: travel blinds or paper shades and painter’s tape.
  • Pacifiers, if used. For toddlers over 12 months, the usual small lovey.

Sharing a room

Put the crib as far from your bed as the room allows, out of your baby’s line of sight if you can, for example around a corner. Never drape a cover over the crib. White noise between you and the baby helps everyone.

Time zones

TripApproach
1–2 hours’ difference, any lengthStay on home time if it suits the trip (a later local bedtime going west can be convenient), or split the difference.
3+ hours, trip under about 4 daysStay close to home time; partial shifts of an hour or two only.
3+ hours, longer tripSwitch to local time on day one. Adult data suggest about one day per hour of change going east, a little faster going west.

Shifting the clock

  1. Wake at a normal local morning time, even after a rough night, and get into daylight straight away.
  2. Eat on local time. Meals are a clock signal.
  3. Cap naps at their usual length so daytime sleep does not replace the night. Wake a baby from a nap that runs long.
  4. Dim the hour before local bedtime; keep the room dark overnight.
  5. Night wakings: keep them dark and quiet. A hungry young baby gets fed; an older baby awake from jet lag gets calm company, not playtime.

Eastward travel (bedtime arrives before the body is ready) is usually harder than westward (early mornings for a few days), because the body clock shifts later more easily than earlier. Morning light helps after flying east.

In transit

On planes and long drives, take whatever sleep you can get: feeding, holding and motion are all fine. Sedating medicines are not a travel tool; see over-the-counter medicines and infant sleep.

Coming home

Return to the usual rules on the first night back. In practice, most babies who slept independently before a trip are back to baseline within 2–4 nights, or about a week after a large time change. If travel is coming soon after sleep training, try to leave 2 weeks between the two. Families working with Actually Sleeping plan upcoming trips with their coach.

Frequently asked

Should I keep my baby on home time when traveling?

For a 1–2 hour difference or a short trip, often yes. For 3 or more hours and a longer stay, switching to local time is easier in the end.

How long does jet lag last in babies?

There is little research in babies. Adult data suggest the body clock shifts about 1 hour a day after flying east and 1.5 hours after flying west, and strong morning light and consistent local mealtimes help.

Can my baby sleep in my bed just for the trip?

The AAP advises against bed-sharing, and unfamiliar adult beds and soft bedding add risk. Use a crib or play yard in your room.

Will travel undo sleep training?

It can cause a few off nights. Returning to the usual routine as soon as you are home typically restores sleep within several nights.
