Is sleep training safe? What the AAP safe-sleep rules mean for a sleep plan

Yes, when the sleep plan follows the AAP safe-sleep guidance: baby on the back, on a firm flat surface in a crib or bassinet, with nothing else in the sleep space, in the parents’ room ideally for the first 6 months. Behavioral sleep training in babies over about 4–6 months has been studied in randomized trials and follow-ups to age 5 without evidence of harm to attachment, behavior or stress physiology.

The non-negotiables in every plan

  • Back to sleep, every sleep, until age 1. Once a baby can roll both ways independently, you do not need to reposition, but you still place them on the back.
  • Firm, flat surface that meets CPSC standards: crib, bassinet, play yard. Not an incline, not a swing, not a car seat, not an adult bed, not a couch.
  • Nothing in the sleep space: no bumpers, pillows, blankets, positioners, stuffed animals. Use a sleep sack for warmth.
  • Room-share, don’t bed-share, ideally for the first 6 months. A plan can teach independent sleep in a bassinet three feet from your bed.
  • No smoke exposure; offer a pacifier at sleep onset once breastfeeding is established; keep up vaccinations.
  • Swaddling stops at the first signs of rolling, usually by 8–12 weeks.

What the research says about sleep training itself

Randomized controlled trials of behavioral sleep interventions (graduated extinction, bedtime fading, parent-present methods) in infants over 6 months show improved infant sleep and maternal mood. A five-year follow-up of one of the largest trials found no differences in children’s emotional health, behavior, stress regulation or the parent-child relationship. A 2016 trial that measured cortisol found it slightly lower, not higher, in the sleep-trained babies, with no differences in attachment at 12 months. Reasonable people differ on method; the evidence does not support the claim that teaching independent sleep harms babies.

Where coaching adds safety

A written plan removes the 2 a.m. improvisation that leads to unsafe choices: the exhausted parent who brings the baby into bed, falls asleep on the couch holding them, or props a bottle. Families who are sleeping do not make those decisions.

When sleep training should wait

Under about 10–12 weeks adjusted age; during acute illness; when weight gain is a concern; when a pediatrician has flagged a medical sleep issue (for example suspected sleep-disordered breathing). Your pediatrician is the authority on readiness.

Frequently asked

Does cry-it-out cause harm?

Studies of behavioral sleep methods, including extinction-based ones, have not found harm to attachment or stress physiology. Our own approach is not unmodified cry-it-out; the plan is designed to minimize crying, with daily coaching.

Can I sleep train while room-sharing?

Yes. Many families do, following the AAP recommendation to room-share for the first 6 months.

When can my baby have a blanket or stuffed animal?

Not before 12 months. After that, a small lovey is generally fine; discuss with your pediatrician.
