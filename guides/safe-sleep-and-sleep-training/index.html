Is sleep training safe? What the AAP safe-sleep rules mean for a sleep plan

Yes, when the sleep plan follows the AAP safe-sleep guidance: baby on the back, on a firm flat surface in a crib or bassinet, with nothing else in the sleep space, in the parents’ room for at least the first 6 months. In a randomized trial of babies 6 to 16 months old, behavioral sleep methods improved sleep with no differences in attachment or behavior a year later.

The non-negotiables in every plan

  • Back to sleep for every nap and every night, until age 1.
  • Firm, flat surface that does not indent under the baby: crib, bassinet, play yard. The AAP says any surface inclined more than 10 degrees is unsafe for sleep. If a baby falls asleep in a car seat, swing, stroller or carrier, move them to a firm, flat sleep surface on their back as soon as possible.
  • Nothing in the sleep space except a fitted sheet: no bumpers, pillows, quilts or loose bedding. A wearable sleep sack keeps a baby warm.
  • Room-share, don’t bed-share, for at least the first 6 months. The AAP says to avoid bed sharing under any circumstances, including with twins and other multiples. A plan can teach independent sleep in a bassinet next to your bed.
  • No smoke or vape exposure; offer a pacifier at sleep time once breastfeeding is established; keep up with vaccinations.
  • Swaddling stops as soon as your baby looks like they are trying to roll.

What the research says about sleep training itself

In a 2016 randomized controlled trial of 43 infants aged 6 to 16 months, both graduated extinction and bedtime fading improved sleep. Salivary cortisol showed small-to-moderate declines in those groups compared with controls, and at 12-month follow-up there were no significant differences between groups in emotional and behavioral problems or in secure-insecure attachment. Reasonable people differ on method; this trial does not support the claim that teaching independent sleep harms babies.

When a sleep plan waits for the pediatrician

During acute illness; when weight gain is a concern; when a pediatrician has flagged a medical sleep issue, for example suspected sleep-disordered breathing. Your pediatrician is the authority on readiness. Frequently asked

Does cry-it-out cause harm?

In a 2016 randomized trial, babies 6 to 16 months old whose parents used graduated extinction or bedtime fading showed no differences in attachment or behavior a year later. Actually Sleeping does not use cry it out; the plan is written for your baby and supported daily.

Can I sleep train while room-sharing?

Yes. Many families do, following the AAP recommendation to room-share without bed sharing for at least the first 6 months. A plan can be written around a bassinet or crib in your room.

When can my baby have a blanket or stuffed animal?

The AAP safe-sleep recommendations apply until age 1: nothing in the sleep space except a fitted sheet, with a wearable sleep sack for warmth. After the first birthday, ask your pediatrician what is appropriate for your child.
