What credentials should a baby sleep consultant have?

Sleep consulting has no required license. Certifications come from private training programs that set their own standards, so the letters after a name vary in meaning. Ask what training they completed, whether they hold a licensed health care credential, what that credential covers, and how they screen for medical issues. Judge the answers alongside their process, support and written commitments.

What the title does and does not mean

Anyone can call themselves a sleep consultant or sleep coach. There is no single standard credential. A 2018 survey of 142 practicing sleep coaches in 17 countries found education ranging from high school diplomas to doctoral degrees. Only 20% were or had been licensed health care providers, and 38% carried malpractice insurance. Most respondents named a formal sleep coach training program as the most important qualification. The authors suggested their findings could inform future guidelines for training and credentialing.

None of this means consultants are unqualified. It means you have to ask.

Questions to ask

  • “Do you hold a license, and in which field?”
  • “What questions do you ask to rule out medical causes?”
  • “When do you refer a family back to the pediatrician?”
  • “How many families have you worked with?”

Frequently asked

Are sleep consultants certified?

Many hold certifications from private training programs, which vary in content, length and standards. There is no single required credential. Ask which program they completed, what it covered, and whether it included screening for medical issues.

Is a sleep consultant a medical professional?

Usually not. In one survey of practicing sleep coaches, only 20% were or had been licensed health care providers. Even those who are should not diagnose or treat as part of coaching.

Does it matter if my sleep consultant is a nurse or pharmacist?

It can help with screening and with medication questions, and a license brings professional accountability. It does not make them a sleep-medicine specialist, and it does not replace your pediatrician for anything medical.

Who should I see instead of a sleep consultant?

See your pediatrician if you notice snoring, breathing pauses, poor weight gain or unusual daytime sleepiness. They may refer you to a sleep-medicine specialist. Coaching can follow once medical causes are ruled out.
