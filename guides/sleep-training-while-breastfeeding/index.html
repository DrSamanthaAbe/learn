Can I sleep train and keep breastfeeding?

Yes. Sleep training changes how your baby falls asleep, not whether they are breastfed, and you do not have to night wean to do it. A good plan keeps the night feeds your pediatrician recommends and changes only the wakings that are about falling back asleep. Ideally breastfeeding is well established and your baby is growing well before you start.

A baby can fall asleep independently and still have night feeds. Many breastfed babies do exactly that for months.

Why breastfed babies often wake more

Breastfeeding is calming, and many breastfed babies fall asleep at the breast at bedtime. That is natural, and it means the breast becomes the way they fall asleep. When they surface between sleep cycles in the night, they look for the same thing. The AAP’s parent site notes that babies who are helped to sleep may have trouble falling back asleep when they wake. The result is a baby who feeds briefly many times a night, not always from hunger. See the feed-to-sleep association.

Telling hunger feeds from habit feeds, and changing one without touching the other, is the delicate part. It is also where Actually Sleeping spends most of its time with breastfeeding families. Dr. Sam writes the plan around your breastfeeding goals and your pediatrician’s guidance, and the plan never forces dropping night feeds.

Why breastfeeding is worth protecting

The AAP notes that at least partial human milk feeding has been shown to significantly lower the risk of sleep-related infant deaths. That is a strong reason to make sure any change to sleep does not undermine breastfeeding.

Supply and health: when to call someone

These are safety points, not a weaning plan:

  • If you wake painfully full, express just enough for comfort.
  • Call your doctor or a lactation consultant if you develop a painful lump, redness or fever.
  • If wet diapers drop or you are worried about your baby’s intake, pause and call your pediatrician or a lactation consultant.
  • If you pump at work or have had supply concerns, involve a lactation consultant before any change to night feeds.

Hold off on any sleep plan if your baby has slow weight gain, has latch or supply problems still being worked on, or is ill.

Why a generic plan often fails breastfed babies

Most general methods treat every waking the same way, or treat every feed as a habit. Neither works for a breastfed baby who still needs some night feeds. A breastfed baby who smells milk may also protest harder when the nursing parent responds, which changes who should handle which wakings. These are family-specific decisions, and they change as your baby grows.

Frequently asked

Do I have to night wean to sleep train?

No. You can keep night feeds and still teach your baby to fall asleep independently at bedtime and at other wakings. An Actually Sleeping plan never forces dropping night feeds; that decision belongs to your pediatrician.

Will sleep training hurt my milk supply?

Teaching your baby to fall asleep independently does not require dropping feeds. If feeds do change, supply follows demand, so any change should be gradual and guided by your pediatrician or a lactation consultant. Call them if you notice engorgement, a painful lump, redness or fever.

How do I know if my baby is hungry or just waking from habit?

Hunger often looks like a long gap since the last feed, a full active feed, and going straight back to sleep. Habit often looks like frequent brief feeds and dozing off. Your pediatrician can tell you how many feeds your baby needs at this age and weight.

Can I still nurse my baby to sleep for naps?

It is your choice. Babies often learn fastest when they fall asleep the same way at naps and at night, but plans can be built around your goals. Dr. Sam writes Actually Sleeping plans around each family’s breastfeeding preferences.
