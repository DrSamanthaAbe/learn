Is it the 4 month sleep regression or a real sleep problem?

The 4-month regression is a permanent change, not a phase: your baby’s sleep cycles mature into the adult pattern, and any sleep association that worked before now has to be repeated at every cycle. If sleep got worse around 3–5 months and has stayed worse for more than two weeks, it is now a sleep pattern you can change — waiting it out rarely works.

What actually happens at 4 months

Newborns drop quickly into deep sleep. Around 3–5 months the brain reorganizes sleep into distinct light and deep stages cycling every 90–120 minutes. The baby now surfaces to near-waking between cycles. A baby who fell asleep independently drifts back down; a baby who fell asleep feeding or rocking wakes fully and needs it again. Nothing “regresses” — the baby matured and the old method stopped covering for it.

How to tell which one you have

Looks like a phaseLooks like a pattern
Started suddenly with a milestone, illness or travelStarted around 3–5 months and has not improved
Irregular — some nights fineSame wake-ups at roughly the same times nightly
Resolves in under two weeksOver two weeks and counting
Baby goes back down easilyBaby needs the bedtime method again each time

What to do

Because the change is permanent, the fix is teaching independent sleep onset at bedtime. At 4 months this can be done gently and gradually; most babies need a few more weeks of maturity before night feeds can go entirely, so plans at this age usually keep one scheduled feed. The 4-month window is also one of the best times to start: habits are shallow and the baby is not yet mobile.

Frequently asked

How long does the 4 month sleep regression last?

If nothing changes, indefinitely — it is a developmental shift, not a phase. With independent sleep onset in place, sleep usually improves within a week.

Can you sleep train during the 4 month regression?

Yes, and it is often the ideal time. Gentler methods and a kept night feed are typical at this age.

Is my 4 month old hungry at night?

Possibly once; rarely four times. If your baby is gaining well, most wake-ups at this age are cycle transitions, not hunger. Your pediatrician can confirm.
