Is it the 4 month sleep regression or a real sleep problem?

Usually it is both. From about 3 months, a baby’s sleep starts to organize into distinct cycles, and a baby who needs feeding or rocking to fall asleep now needs it again between cycles. That change is permanent, so waiting it out rarely helps. If sleep has stayed worse for weeks, it is a pattern, and a plan from Actually Sleeping can change it.

What actually changes

Young newborns sleep differently from older babies. By about 3 months, sleep starts to organize more like an older child’s: babies begin to fall asleep into non-REM sleep, hormone rhythms that shape day and night start cycling, and sleep runs in cycles of about 50 minutes. At the end of each cycle, your baby surfaces close to waking. A baby who fell asleep independently drifts back down. A baby who fell asleep feeding or being rocked wakes fully and needs it again. Nothing has gone backward. Your baby matured, and the old way of falling asleep stopped covering for it.

Why waiting and quick fixes rarely work

Because the underlying change is permanent, there is no end date to wait for. Quick fixes tend to move the problem rather than solve it: swap feeding for rocking and your baby needs rocking at 2 a.m. instead. Generic plans have another problem at this age. Some 4-month-olds still need night feeds, so a plan has to teach independent sleep while keeping whatever feeds your pediatrician wants. Getting that balance right depends on your baby’s feeding, growth, temperament, naps and how they fall asleep now.

Frequently asked

How long does the 4 month sleep regression last?

The underlying change is permanent, so it has no natural end date. What improves sleep is your baby learning to fall asleep independently. That is the focus of a Actually Sleeping plan, backed by a written guarantee and daily check-ins.

Can you sleep train during the 4 month regression?

Yes, and it is often a good time: habits are newer and your baby is not yet mobile. At Actually Sleeping, plans at this age are paced for the baby, and night feeds stay in until your pediatrician says otherwise.

Is my 4 month old hungry at night?

Possibly. Whether and how often your baby needs to eat at night is your pediatrician’s call. Not every waking is hunger, though. Many are cycle transitions. A Actually Sleeping plan keeps the feeds your pediatrician wants and changes how your baby falls asleep.
