What is a good sleep schedule for a 10 or 11 month old?

At 10–11 months babies still need 2 naps, with wake windows of 3–4 hours as a common rule of thumb. The AASM recommends 12–16 hours of sleep in 24, naps included. Bedtime usually falls between 7 and 8 p.m., with 11–12 hours overnight and 2–3 hours of naps.

Sample day for a 10–11 month old

Treat every time below as a starting point. Shift the whole day to match your baby’s actual wake-up time, then adjust one thing at a time in 15-minute steps and hold it for three days before judging.

What is changing developmentally

Cruising, standing alone and, for some, first steps. First words appear and comprehension runs far ahead of speech. Babies this age test cause and effect on purpose, including what happens when they throw the pacifier out of the crib or stand and shout. Separation anxiety is still present but usually easing.

Common problems at this age

  • The false one-nap transition: many babies refuse a nap for a week or two around 10–12 months. It is usually temporary. In practice most babies are not ready for one nap until somewhere around 14–18 months, and dropping early often causes night waking. See nap transitions.
  • Lingering regression: see the 8-to-10-month sleep regression, and what is coming in the 12-month sleep regression.
  • Early waking: check that the first nap is not before about 9:45 a.m. See early morning waking.
  • Still fed or rocked to sleep: see the feed-to-sleep association.
  • Crib safety: mattress at the lowest setting, and still nothing loose in the crib; the AAP’s safe sleep guidance, which keeps blankets, pillows, toys and bumpers out, covers the whole first year.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Yes. Formal sleep training is appropriate at any point from about 4 months, and 10–11 month olds usually respond quickly to a consistent plan because they understand routines. Most no longer need night feeds, but confirm that with your pediatrician rather than assuming. Actually Sleeping coaches both nights and naps, continuing with naps until they are solid. Frequently asked

Can a 10 month old switch to one nap?

It is rarely a good idea. In practice few babies can handle the long windows a one-nap day needs this early, and in a large review fewer than 2.5% of children had stopped napping altogether before age 2. Nap refusal at 10–11 months is usually a schedule tweak or a phase.

How long should a 10 month old nap?

About 2–3 hours in total across two naps of 1–1.5 hours each.

What time should an 11 month old go to bed?

Between 7 and 8 p.m., about 3.5–4 hours after the second nap ends.

Is the schedule different at 11 months than 10?

Only slightly. Windows stretch by about 15 minutes, typically toward 3.25, 3.5 and 4 hours.
