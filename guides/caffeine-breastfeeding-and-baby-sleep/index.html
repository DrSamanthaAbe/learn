Does my caffeine affect my breastfed baby’s sleep?

A little. Caffeine passes into breast milk at low levels and peaks about one to two hours after you drink it, but a newborn clears caffeine extremely slowly — a half-life of days rather than hours — so it can accumulate in the first months. Moderate intake (commonly cited as up to about 300 mg a day, roughly two to three cups of coffee) is considered compatible with breastfeeding; if your young baby is unusually jittery or wakeful, cutting back is a reasonable experiment.

A note on scope: Dr. Sam is a Doctor of Pharmacy, not your baby’s physician. This guide explains how a medication relates to sleep. Whether and how your child takes any medication is a decision for your pediatrician.

The pharmacology in plain terms

  • Transfer is low. Roughly 1% of the mother’s dose reaches the baby through milk.
  • Clearance is slow in newborns. An adult clears half a dose of caffeine in about 4–6 hours. A newborn can take 2–4 days. By 4–6 months, clearance approaches adult speed. That is why a small daily amount can build up in a very young baby and why the effect fades with age.
  • Timing helps. Milk caffeine peaks 1–2 hours after you drink it. Having coffee right after a feed rather than right before one lowers what the baby gets.

What to watch for

Unusual jitteriness, wide-eyed wakefulness at odd hours, or fussiness that tracks with your intake. If you see that, cut back for a week and compare. If nothing changes, caffeine was not the driver — and in our experience it usually is not. Night waking in a 4-month-old is far more often a sleep-cycle and sleep-association story than a coffee story.

Other sources people forget

Energy drinks, pre-workout powders, some teas, dark chocolate, cola, and over-the-counter products that include caffeine (some headache remedies). Pre-workout products in particular can contain several hundred milligrams per serving.

Frequently asked

How much coffee can I drink while breastfeeding?

Up to about 300 mg of caffeine a day is widely considered compatible with breastfeeding; many clinicians are comfortable somewhat higher. Discuss your situation with your pediatrician or lactation consultant.

Does caffeine lower milk supply?

There is no good evidence that moderate caffeine reduces supply.

Should I pump and dump after coffee?

No. Caffeine leaves milk as it leaves your blood; pumping does not speed that up.
