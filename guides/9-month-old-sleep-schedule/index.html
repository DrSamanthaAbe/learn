What is a good sleep schedule for a 9 month old?

At 9 months babies take 2 naps and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake about 3–3.5 hours at a time. The AASM recommends 12–16 hours of sleep in 24, naps included. Bedtime usually falls between 7 and 7:45 p.m., with 11–12 hours overnight and 2–3 hours of daytime sleep.

What is changing developmentally

Pulling to stand, cruising, pincer grasp, waving, and understanding simple words. Separation anxiety is often building toward its usual peak between 10 and 18 months (AAP). Three meals a day are established for many babies, which changes the feeding picture overnight.

Why the average 9-month schedule often does not fit

  • Regression in full swing: standing, crying at the door, fighting the second nap. See the 8-to-10-month sleep regression.
  • Early waking: often tied to how the first nap is timed, and easy to turn into a habit. See early morning waking.
  • Second nap refused: babies this age are rarely ready for one nap; see nap transitions.
  • Naps only on a parent: see contact napping.

Which of these applies, and in what order to handle them, is different for each baby. A 9 month old who wakes from habit needs something different from one who wakes because the day is spaced wrong. That is the part a custom plan decides.

Safe sleep at 9 months

  • Mattress at the lowest setting now; many 9 month olds can pull up (AAP, MedlinePlus).
  • Crib away from windows and cords, and nothing loose in it before age 1 (AAP).

Frequently asked

How many naps does a 9 month old need?

Two, as a common pattern. Studies found babies 6 to 11 months averaged about 2 naps a day. Total daytime sleep is commonly 2–3 hours, though many babies this age nap less because of how they fall asleep.

How long should a 9 month old be awake before bed?

As a common rule of thumb, about 3.25–3.5 hours after the second nap ends. It is not a research finding, and the right gap for your baby is one of the things Dr. Sam sets and adjusts with you.

Why is my 9 month old suddenly waking at night?

Most often new motor skills plus separation anxiety, which the AAP says usually peaks between 10 and 18 months. Rule out illness with your pediatrician. A consistent response is what Actually Sleeping helps you hold, night after night.
