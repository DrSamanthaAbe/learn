What time should my baby or toddler go to bed? Bedtime calculator by age

Work backward from wake-up time. The AASM recommends 12–16 hours of sleep per 24 hours at 4–12 months, 11–14 hours at 1–2 years and 10–13 hours at 3–5 years, naps included, and makes no recommendation under 4 months. The calculator starts from research and works backward. The research part is the American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) recommended total sleep per 24 hours for your child’s age. The rule-of-thumb part is how much of that sleep usually happens in naps. Night sleep is what is left, and bedtime is your wake-up time minus night sleep.

Keep in mind: a calculator gives an average. The hard part is getting your particular baby to follow it. That is what a custom plan and daily check-ins are for.

A bedtime on paper is the easy part. Many families already know roughly when their child should be asleep; the problem is that the child does not go to sleep then, or does not stay asleep. Actually Sleeping works on that part, with a plan written for your child and daily support while it settles.

The formula

  1. Take the AASM range for the age: 12–16 hours at 4–12 months, 11–14 hours at 1–2 years, 10–13 hours at 3–5 years, naps included.
  2. Subtract the middle of the typical nap total for the age (a rule of thumb). If your child no longer naps, subtract nothing.
  3. The result is the night sleep that keeps the 24-hour total inside the AASM range.
  4. Bedtime window = wake-up time minus that night sleep. The early end gives the most sleep; at the late end, total sleep only just reaches the AASM minimum.

Because the AASM ranges are 3–4 hours wide, the window is wide too. The last column of the table shows the narrower bedtime many families use in practice, which is a rule of thumb, not a research finding. That column describes time in bed, and children rarely sleep every minute of it, which is why it can start slightly earlier than the AASM-based window.

Under 4 months: no number

The AASM makes no recommendation for babies under 4 months, citing the wide range of normal variation and too little evidence linking sleep to health outcomes at that age. This page therefore shows no bedtime for that band. Newborn bedtimes are often late and drift earlier over the first few months.

Children who have stopped napping

With no nap, all of the AASM total has to come at night, so the window moves earlier. A 2020 meta-analysis found fewer than 2.5% of children stop napping before age 2 and 94% have stopped by age 5, so a child under 2 who refuses naps for a few days is more likely going through a nap transition than finished with naps. Frequently asked

What time should a 1 year old go to bed?

The AASM recommends 11–14 hours of sleep per 24 hours at 1–2 years. With about 2–2.5 hours of naps and a 7 a.m. wake-up, that gives an AASM-based window of about 7:15 to 10:15 p.m. In practice many families use 7–8 p.m., a rule of thumb.

Is a 6:30 p.m. bedtime too early?

Not necessarily. For a baby 4–12 months who wakes around 6:30 a.m. and naps a typical amount, a 6:30 p.m. bedtime keeps total sleep within the AASM range of 12–16 hours. Use the calculator with your own wake-up time to check.

Why is there no bedtime for my newborn?

The AASM makes no sleep-hours recommendation under 4 months because normal sleep varies widely and there is too little evidence linking sleep to health at that age. Any number would be invented, so the calculator shows none.

My toddler dropped their nap. How does bedtime change?

Without a nap, all the AASM total has to come at night, so bedtime moves earlier. For ages 1–2 years and a 7 a.m. wake-up, 11–14 hours of night sleep means a bedtime between 5 and 8 p.m.; choose the earlier part during the transition.
