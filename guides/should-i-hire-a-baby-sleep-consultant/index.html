When coaching makes the biggest difference

  • You have tried a book, a course or advice from friends, and it did not hold.
  • Your baby has reflux, was born early, is a twin or triplet, or is a toddler who leaves the bed.
  • You are bed-sharing out of exhaustion and want your baby in their own sleep space.
  • You and your partner disagree about how to respond at night and need one plan you both follow.
  • Broken sleep is affecting your health, your work or your relationship.

Actually Sleeping works with all of these, from the newborn stage on. Starting young is the gentlest way: foundations begin from the newborn stage, and the 14-day guarantee clock starts at the 10-week birthday.

What to look for in any sleep consultant

  • Who writes the plan: a named person, or a template by age.
  • What support looks like: daily human replies, or a weekly email.
  • Whether the result is guaranteed: in writing with a refund, or only in a tagline. Actually Sleeping’s guarantee is in its Terms of Service.
  • Medical common sense: questions about feeding, growth, reflux and prematurity, and feeding decisions left to your pediatrician.
  • Safe sleep: babies on their backs, on a firm, flat surface with nothing else in the crib. A plan that uses inclines, positioners or loose items in the crib goes against AAP guidance.

What is the difference between a sleep consultant and a sleep coach?

Nothing regulated. Neither is a licensed title. Judge the person by credentials, who writes the plan, how often you hear from them, and whether the result is guaranteed in writing with a refund.

Can a sleep consultant help with naps?

Yes, and naps are where coaching matters most, because they take longest and tempt parents to give up. At Actually Sleeping, naps are coached until they are solid as part of every plan, with daily check-ins adjusting them as your baby changes.
