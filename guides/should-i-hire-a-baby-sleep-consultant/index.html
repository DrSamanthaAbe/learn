Should I hire a baby sleep consultant?

Hire one if you have tried a book or online course, stayed consistent for at least a week, and sleep is still broken — or if your situation is complicated (reflux, prematurity, twins, a toddler in a bed) and you want someone to adjust the plan nightly. Skip it if you have not yet tried a single consistent approach; many babies respond to that alone.

What a consultant actually adds

Not secret knowledge. The methods are public. What changes outcomes is a plan matched to your specific baby, and someone to tell you at 2 a.m. on night 3 whether to hold the line or adjust. Most families who fail on their own fail on consistency, not on method.

You probably do not need one if

  • You have a newborn and things are going fine. Early foundations help, but they are optional.
  • You have never tried one approach consistently for 5–7 nights.
  • The problem started this week with a cold or a tooth.

You probably do if

  • You have tried a course or book, done it correctly for a week or more, and it did not take.
  • Your baby has reflux, was premature, is a twin, or is a toddler who leaves the bed.
  • Parents disagree about how to respond at night and need a neutral plan.
  • You are past the point where “they’ll grow out of it” is tolerable for your health or work.

What to look for

  • Who writes the plan — a named person, or a template by age?
  • What support looks like — daily human replies, or a weekly email?
  • Whether results are guaranteed — and in writing with a refund, or in a tagline. Most consultants do not guarantee the outcome. Actually Sleeping does, in its Terms of Service.
  • Medical screening — does the intake ask about weight gain, reflux, prematurity?
  • Safe sleep — any plan that involves inclines, positioners or loose items in the crib is disqualifying.

Frequently asked

Are sleep consultants worth the money?

If you have already tried a consistent approach and it failed, usually yes: the cost is weeks of lost sleep for the whole family. If you have not, try one free method consistently first.

What is the difference between a sleep consultant and a sleep coach?

Nothing regulated. Neither is a licensed title. Judge the person by credentials, process, support and whether results are guaranteed.

Can a sleep consultant help with naps?

Yes, and naps are where coaching matters most because they take longest and tempt parents to give up.
