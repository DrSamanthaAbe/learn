Does a dream feed help a baby sleep longer?

Sometimes. A dream feed — feeding your baby around 10–11 p.m. without fully waking them — helps some babies between about 2 and 6 months shift their longest stretch to line up with yours, and does nothing or causes extra waking in others. Try it for 5–7 nights; if the first waking does not move later, drop it.

What a dream feed is

You lift your sleeping baby roughly 2–3 hours after bedtime, usually just before you go to bed, feed them while they stay drowsy, and lay them back down. The idea is simple: a baby whose longest stretch runs 7 p.m. to midnight has spent it while you were awake. Topping up at 10:30 may move the next waking to 3 or 4 a.m.

What the evidence says

Not much, and it is mixed. Small studies that included a late-evening feed as one part of a larger routine found longer night stretches, but the feed was never tested alone. In practice it is an experiment you run on your own baby, not a proven technique.

Who it tends to help, and who it does not

More likely to helpLess likely to help
Babies about 2–6 months who still need a night feedBabies over about 6–7 months who no longer need night calories
Babies who feed well while drowsyBabies who will not latch or suck while asleep, or who wake fully and stay up
Babies who fall asleep independently at bedtimeBabies who wake every 2 hours because of a sleep association

That last row matters most. A dream feed addresses hunger. It does not address a baby who cannot link sleep cycles, which is the more common reason for frequent waking after 4 months. See why a 6 month old wakes every 2 hours.

How to do it safely

  1. Time it 2–3 hours after bedtime, between about 10 and 11 p.m.
  2. Keep lights off and the room quiet. Lift your baby gently; most will root and feed with eyes closed.
  3. Hold your baby in your arms, semi-upright, for the whole feed. Never prop a bottle and never feed a baby lying flat in the crib.
  4. If your baby is swaddled and will not rouse enough to feed well, loosen the swaddle.
  5. Burp briefly, then place your baby on their back in their own crib or bassinet.
  6. Stay awake yourself. If you might doze off, do the feed sitting up in a lit room rather than in bed or on a sofa.

Signs it is backfiring

  • Your baby now wakes at the dream-feed time on their own, plus the old times.
  • The feed wakes them fully and resettling takes 30 minutes or more.
  • Night waking after the feed is no later than before.

When and how to drop it

Most families drop it between 6 and 9 months, once the pediatrician agrees night feeds are no longer needed. Either move it 15 minutes earlier every few nights until it merges with bedtime, or reduce the amount gradually as you would any night feed. The full process is in when and how to night wean.

In the plans Dr. Sam writes at Actually Sleeping, a dream feed is used when it clearly earns its place for that baby and removed when it does not.

Frequently asked

What time should I do a dream feed?

About 2–3 hours after bedtime, typically between 10 and 11 p.m., ideally right before you go to sleep yourself.

Should I burp or change my baby after a dream feed?

A brief burp, yes. Change the diaper only if it is dirty or leaking; a full change tends to wake the baby completely.

Does a dream feed cause a feeding habit?

It can if it continues long after the baby stops needing night calories. Used for a few months and then phased out, it rarely causes a problem.

My baby will not wake enough to eat. Should I keep trying?

No. Some babies sleep too deeply in the first part of the night to feed well. If a few attempts fail, skip the dream feed and feed when your baby wakes.
