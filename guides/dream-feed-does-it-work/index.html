Does a dream feed help a baby sleep longer?

Sometimes. A dream feed, feeding your baby around 10–11 p.m. without fully waking them, helps some babies between about 2 and 6 months shift their longest stretch to line up with yours, and does nothing or causes extra waking in others. It addresses hunger only. It does not help a baby who wakes because they need help falling back asleep.

What a dream feed is

You lift your sleeping baby roughly 2–3 hours after bedtime, usually just before you go to bed, feed them while they stay drowsy, and lay them back down. The idea is simple: a baby whose longest stretch runs 7 p.m. to midnight has spent it while you were awake. Topping up at 10:30 may move the next waking to 3 or 4 a.m.

What the evidence says

Not much. One small 1993 randomized study of 26 breastfed newborns combined a “focal feed” between 10 p.m. and midnight with gradually stretching middle-of-the-night feeds and strong day–night cues. By 8 weeks, all 13 babies in that group slept from midnight to 5 a.m., compared with 23% of controls, with no difference in 24-hour milk intake. The late feed was never tested alone, so nobody knows how much of the effect it was responsible for.

Who it tends to help, and who it does not

It is most likely to help a baby of about 2 to 6 months who still needs a night feed, feeds well while drowsy, and already falls asleep independently at bedtime. It is least likely to help an older baby who no longer needs night calories, a baby who will not feed while asleep or who wakes fully and stays up, and a baby who wakes every couple of hours.

That last group matters most. A dream feed addresses hunger. It does nothing for a baby who cannot link sleep cycles, which is the more common reason for frequent waking after 4 months. For those babies, a dream feed often just adds one more waking to the night. See why a 6 month old wakes every 2 hours.

Safety, if you do a late feed

  • Hold your baby in your arms, semi-upright, for the whole feed. Never prop a bottle and never feed a baby lying flat in the crib.
  • Afterwards, place your baby on their back in their own crib or bassinet.
  • Stay awake yourself. If you might doze off, feed sitting up in a lit room rather than in bed or on a sofa. The AAP lists sofas and armchairs among the most dangerous places to fall asleep with a baby.

Frequently asked

Does a dream feed work for every baby?

No. It helps some babies who still need a night feed and feed well while drowsy. It does little for a baby who wakes because they need help falling back asleep, and in some babies it adds a waking.

Does a dream feed cause a feeding habit?

It can if it continues long after the baby stops needing night calories. Whether your baby still needs night feeds is your pediatrician’s call, and that answer decides how long a late feed has a place.

Should my baby’s sleep plan include a dream feed?

It depends on your baby. In Actually Sleeping plans, Dr. Sam includes a dream feed when it clearly helps that baby and leaves it out when it does not. Night feeds stay in the plan until your pediatrician says they can go.

Is it safe to dream feed in bed?

Only if you are fully awake. Hold your baby semi-upright, never prop a bottle, and put your baby back on their back in their own crib or bassinet. If you might doze off, feed sitting up in a lit room, never on a sofa.
