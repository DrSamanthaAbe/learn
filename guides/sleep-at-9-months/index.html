Sleep at 9 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 9 months, the AASM range is 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps, and studies found an average of about 12.6 hours for babies from 9 months. Most babies take 2 naps as a common pattern. Bedtime protest is typical because babies now understand that you still exist when you leave, and the CDC lists clinginess with strangers as a 9-month milestone.

What is changing at 9 months

The CDC’s 9-month milestones explain a lot of what happens at bedtime. Each one has a sleep side.

CDC 9-month milestoneHow it can show up at night
Looks for objects when dropped out of sightYour baby knows you are just outside the door and calls for you
Reacts when you leave (looks, reaches for you, or cries)Protest at the moment you put them down
Is shy, clingy, or fearful around strangersHarder settling with a new sitter or at daycare
Gets to a sitting position by herself; sits without supportSitting up in the crib instead of lying down
Lifts arms up to be picked upA very clear request at 2 a.m.

None of these is a problem. They are signs of healthy development that happen to collide with sleep.

What is normal

In a review of observational studies, babies from 9 months averaged 12.6 hours of sleep per 24 hours, with a normal spread from about 9.4 to 15.8 hours. Babies 6 to 11 months averaged 2.2 naps a day. Two naps is a common pattern now, not a rule; some babies still take a short third nap on long days.

What parents find hardest

  • Bedtime protest. Keep the routine short and identical. A predictable routine is what the AAP says helps children accept separation.
  • Standing in the crib and not knowing how to get down. Practise bending and sitting in the day.
  • Daycare naps. New faces and noise can mean short naps at daycare and an overtired evening. An earlier bedtime on daycare days is a reasonable rule of thumb.
  • Early waking. Check light, noise and the length of the afternoon awake stretch.

Safe sleep at 9 months

  • Mattress at the lowest setting now; many 9 month olds can pull up.
  • Crib away from windows and cords. No pillows, blankets, bumpers or toys in the crib before age 1.
  • Back to sleep to start; if your baby rolls both ways, leave them in the position they choose.
  • No bottle in bed. The AAP links falling asleep with a bottle to tooth decay.

Night feeds

Whether a 9 month old needs a night feed is your pediatrician’s decision. If your pediatrician says feeds are no longer needed, reduce them gradually rather than stopping suddenly.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Snoring most nights, gasping or noisy breathing in sleep.
  • Night waking with fever, ear pain, vomiting or diarrhoea.
  • Your baby is not yet sitting without support, not babbling, or does not look when you call their name. These are on the CDC’s 9-month list.
  • Very loud, inconsolable screaming episodes early in the night. Usually harmless, but worth describing to the doctor if they recur.

Frequently asked

How much should a 9 month old sleep?

The AASM recommends 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps, for babies 4 to 12 months. In a review of studies, babies from 9 months averaged 12.6 hours, with a normal spread from about 9.4 to 15.8 hours.

Why does my 9 month old stand up in the crib and cry?

Pulling up is new and exciting, and many babies cannot yet get down. Practise sitting down from standing during the day. Make sure the mattress is at the lowest setting, and use the same short response each time.

Is clinginess at 9 months normal?

Yes. The CDC lists being shy, clingy or fearful around strangers, and reacting when you leave, as 9-month milestones. The AAP says separation anxiety usually peaks between 10 and 18 months.
