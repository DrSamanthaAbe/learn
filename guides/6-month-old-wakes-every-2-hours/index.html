Why does my 6 month old wake every 2 hours, and how do I fix it?

A 6-month-old who wakes every 2 hours is almost always waking between sleep cycles (which last roughly 1.5–2 hours at this age) and needing the same help to fall back asleep that they needed at bedtime. Fix how your baby falls asleep at bedtime and the 2 a.m. wake-ups usually stop within a week.

Why every two hours, specifically

Infant sleep cycles run about 90–120 minutes. Every baby surfaces at the end of a cycle; a baby who can fall asleep independently rolls into the next one, while a baby who fell asleep feeding, rocking, bouncing or with a pacifier wakes fully and calls for the same thing. The wake-ups are not hunger every time — they are the bedtime method being requested again.

Rule out the basics first

  • Hunger: many 6-month-olds can go 10–12 hours without a feed, but not all. Ask your pediatrician if your baby is gaining well; if so, night feeds are usually habit, not need.
  • Schedule: at 6 months most babies need 2–3 naps, wake windows of about 2–3 hours, and a bedtime between 6:30 and 8 p.m. Overtiredness causes more wake-ups, not fewer.
  • Environment: dark room, white noise, 68–72°F, and a safe crib with nothing in it.
  • Illness or teething: real, but temporary. If wake-ups have lasted weeks, it is a pattern, not a tooth.

The fix

  1. Move the last feed earlier in the bedtime routine so your baby is not falling asleep on it.
  2. Put your baby down awake. This is the whole game.
  3. Decide in advance how you will respond to wake-ups — and respond the same way every time, every night.
  4. Expect nights 1–3 to be the hardest, and improvement by night 4–7.

The method you choose (gradual, check-ins, or a parent-present approach) matters less than consistency. What derails most families is changing the response on night 3 when it gets hard, which is why having a coach to message at 2 a.m. changes outcomes.

Frequently asked

Is it the 6 month sleep regression?

There is no universal 6-month regression. Wake-ups at this age are usually a sleep-association pattern or a schedule problem, not a developmental phase that passes on its own.

Should I feed my 6 month old at night?

If your baby is gaining well and your pediatrician agrees, most 6-month-olds do not need night feeds. If you want to keep one, schedule it rather than feeding at every wake-up.

How do I know if it’s a sleep problem or just a phase?

If it has lasted more than two weeks and the pattern is the same every night, it is a pattern. Phases are irregular and short.
