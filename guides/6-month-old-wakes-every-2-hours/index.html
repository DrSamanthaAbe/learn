Why does my 6 month old wake every 2 hours, and how do I fix it?

Most often, your baby is surfacing between sleep cycles and needs the same help to get back to sleep that they needed at bedtime: a feed, rocking, a pacifier or you. Hunger, the day’s schedule and illness can play a part, and your pediatrician decides on night feeds. The lasting fix is a plan that teaches your baby to fall asleep independently, which is what Actually Sleeping builds.

Why the wake-ups keep coming

Babies sleep in cycles, and an infant’s cycles are shorter than an adult’s: about 50 minutes, compared with about 90 minutes for adults. At the end of each cycle, every baby surfaces briefly. A baby who fell asleep on their own usually drifts into the next cycle. A baby who fell asleep feeding, being rocked or bounced, or sucking a pacifier often wakes fully and asks for the same thing again. Many of the wake-ups are your bedtime routine being requested again, not hunger every time.

This is exactly the problem Actually Sleeping was built for. Dr. Sam writes a plan for your 6-month-old that changes how they fall asleep at bedtime, so the wake-ups between cycles stop needing you. A coach is with you live on night one, and you check in 1:1 every day after.

Other things that play a part

  • Hunger. Some 6-month-olds still need a night feed. Whether yours does is your pediatrician’s call, based on growth. A Actually Sleeping plan never forces dropping night feeds.
  • The day’s schedule. Naps and wake times that do not fit your baby can leave them overtired at bedtime, and overtired babies tend to sleep worse, not better.
  • The sleep space. Safe sleep is not negotiable at any age: on the back for all naps and at night, on a firm, flat surface, with a fitted sheet only and nothing else in the crib. The AAP recommends sharing a room, not a bed, for at least the first 6 months.
  • Illness or teething. Real, but usually temporary. If your baby seems unwell, call your pediatrician. Wake-ups that have followed the same pattern for weeks are a pattern, not a passing bug.

Why quick fixes tend to fail at this age

Most families who reach us have already tried things: an earlier bedtime, an extra feed, a method from a book. Generic approaches assume an average baby, and a 6-month-old’s feeding, naps, temperament and way of falling asleep all change what the right plan is. The other common reason is changing the response on night three, when it gets hard. That teaches a baby that protesting longer works, and the next attempt gets harder.

Frequently asked

Is it the 6 month sleep regression?

Wake-ups at 6 months usually have a cause you can find: how your baby falls asleep, the day’s schedule, hunger or illness. If the same pattern has gone on for weeks, it is a pattern rather than a phase, and that is what a Actually Sleeping plan addresses.

Should I feed my 6 month old at night?

That is your pediatrician’s call, based on your baby’s growth. Actually Sleeping plans never force dropping night feeds. What changes is how your baby falls asleep, so feeds your baby needs stay, and wake-ups that are not about hunger stop needing a feed.

How do I know if it’s a sleep problem or just a phase?

Phases tend to be irregular and short. If the waking has lasted weeks and looks similar most nights, it is a pattern, and patterns usually continue until how your baby falls asleep changes. That is the part Actually Sleeping works on, with a plan written for your baby and daily check-ins.
