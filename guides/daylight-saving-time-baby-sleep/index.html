How do I adjust my baby’s sleep for daylight saving time?

Either shift the whole schedule by 15 minutes a day for the four days before the clock change, or change nothing in advance, move straight to the new clock time and let your baby adjust over about a week. In spring, bedtime needs to move earlier by the body clock; in fall, later. Light is the main tool: bright mornings, dim evenings and a dark bedroom.

What the clock change does

Your baby’s body clock does not read the wall clock. After the spring change, a 7 p.m. bedtime feels like 6 p.m. to the body, so falling asleep is harder and mornings run later. After the fall change, 7 p.m. feels like 8 p.m., so your baby is tired early and up an hour early. Fall is the one parents dread, because nobody wants a 5 a.m. start.

Spring forward

SundayNormal schedule on the new clock

The do-nothing version: on Sunday, wake your baby at the usual clock time and run the normal day. Bedtime may take longer for a few nights. If your baby has been waking too early, spring is a gift: a 5:30 a.m. waker becomes a 6:30 a.m. waker. Keep the room very dark in the mornings to hold onto it.

Fall back

DayWake, naps, meals and bedtime
Wednesday15 minutes later than usual
Thursday30 minutes later
Friday45 minutes later
Saturday60 minutes later
SundayNormal schedule on the new clock

The split-the-difference version: on Sunday, put your baby to bed 30 minutes early by the new clock (which feels 30 minutes late to them) for three nights, then move to the normal time.

Tools that move the clock

  • Morning light at the target wake time; outdoors if possible. Morning light shifts the body clock earlier and evening light shifts it later.
  • Dim light for the hour before bed. In spring it will still be bright outside at bedtime, so close the blinds early.
  • Blackout the bedroom, especially for brighter spring evenings and for mornings year-round.
  • Meals move with the schedule; they are a time cue too.
  • Hold the wake time. In fall, treat a waking before the target time like any night waking: dark, quiet, boring, until morning officially begins. See early morning waking.

Which approach suits which child

  • Young babies under about 4 months: schedules are loose anyway. Follow wake windows and ignore the clock.
  • Adaptable babies and families with fixed daycare times: do nothing in advance and adjust afterwards.
  • Sensitive sleepers and early risers in fall: the gradual four-day shift is worth the effort.
  • Toddlers with a wake-up light: move the light’s time in the same 15-minute steps.

What to expect

In practice most babies settle within 3–7 days by either method. Naps may be slightly off; use an earlier bedtime if the day goes badly. Do not add new sleep help during the adjustment week. Frequently asked

Should I wake my baby at the normal time after the clocks change?

Yes. A consistent morning wake time, with bright light, is the fastest way to move the body clock.

How long does it take a baby to adjust to daylight saving time?

In practice, usually 3–7 days. Babies who are sensitive to schedule changes may take up to two weeks.

My baby is waking at 5 a.m. after the fall time change. What do I do?

Keep the room dark and treat it as night until your target wake time, moving that target 10–15 minutes later every day or two. Shift naps, meals and bedtime later to match.

Is spring or fall harder for babies?

Fall is usually harder because it produces early mornings. Spring mostly causes a few slow bedtimes.
