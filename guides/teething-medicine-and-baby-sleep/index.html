Does teething medicine help a baby sleep?

Acetaminophen or ibuprofen can relieve real teething pain for a few hours when your pediatrician says they are appropriate for your baby’s age and weight, but they do not make a baby sleep, and most “teething” night waking is not teething. If waking persists beyond a few nights, treat it as a sleep pattern, not a tooth.

What teething does and does not do

Teething can cause gum soreness, drooling, chewing and fussiness around the time a tooth comes through. That discomfort comes and goes. Night waking that runs on for weeks is a different thing. Often a baby who already needed feeding or rocking to fall asleep gets extra of both during a sore night or two, and the extra help is what keeps the waking going after the tooth is through.

The medicines

  • Acetaminophen (Tylenol and generics) relieves pain and fever. Dosing is by weight, and products come in different concentrations, so use the measuring device that came with the product and the dose your pediatrician or the label gives for your child. Never estimate.
  • Ibuprofen (Motrin, Advil and generics) also reduces inflammation. The AAP says not to use it in children younger than 6 months unless your child’s doctor tells you to. Again, by weight, as directed.
  • Neither is a sleep aid. They relieve pain for a few hours. If the baby sleeps better, the pain was real. If nothing changes, the waking has another cause.
  • Avoid: benzocaine teething gels, which the FDA warns against for teething; homeopathic teething tablets, which FDA testing found had inconsistent belladonna content; and teething jewelry such as amber necklaces, which the FDA links to strangulation and choking.

Why a three-night event becomes a three-month habit

A genuinely uncomfortable baby deserves comfort. The trouble starts when the comfort changes how the baby falls asleep: back to feeding to sleep, rocking, a parent’s bed. Babies learn fast. Once the tooth is through, they still expect the new routine at every waking between sleep cycles, so the nights stay broken.

Frequently asked

Can I give Tylenol before bed every night for teething?

No. Routine nightly dosing is not appropriate for teething and can mask other problems. Use it for clear discomfort, as your pediatrician directs, for a short period, with the measuring device that came with the product.

How long does teething disrupt sleep?

Teething discomfort tends to come and go around the time a tooth comes through. When waking continues night after night for weeks, it usually has another cause, most often how your baby falls asleep. That is what a custom sleep plan from Actually Sleeping addresses.

Do teething gels help at night?

The FDA warns against benzocaine products for teething. Ask your pediatrician what is appropriate for your baby’s age, and whether a pain reliever is needed at all. Gels are not a sleep aid in any case.
