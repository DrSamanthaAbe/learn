Does teething medicine help a baby sleep?

Acetaminophen or ibuprofen can relieve real teething pain for a few hours when your pediatrician says they are appropriate for your baby’s age and weight, but they do not make a baby sleep, and most “teething” night waking is not teething. If waking persists beyond a few nights, treat it as a sleep pattern, not a tooth.

A note on scope: Dr. Sam is a Doctor of Pharmacy, not your baby’s physician. This guide explains how a medication relates to sleep. Whether and how your child takes any medication is a decision for your pediatrician.

What teething does and does not do

Teething can cause gum soreness, drooling, chewing and fussiness for a few days around eruption. Studies that tracked babies through eruption found it does not reliably cause high fever, diarrhea or weeks of sleep disruption. When a parent tells us a baby has been “teething” for a month, what we usually find is a sleep association that teething briefly disguised.

The medicines, from a pharmacist

  • Acetaminophen (Tylenol and generics) is a pain and fever reliever. It is the usual first choice in young infants. Dosing is by weight, and infant and children’s concentrations differ, so use the measuring device that came with the product and the dose your pediatrician or the label gives for your child. Never estimate.
  • Ibuprofen (Motrin, Advil and generics) also reduces inflammation. It is generally not used under 6 months, and should be given with food or milk. Again, by weight, as directed.
  • Neither is a sleep aid. They relieve pain for a few hours. If the baby sleeps better, the pain was real. If nothing changes, the waking has another cause.
  • Avoid: benzocaine gels (Orajel and similar) in children under 2 — the FDA warns of methemoglobinemia, a dangerous blood-oxygen problem; homeopathic teething tablets, which have had quality and belladonna-content problems; amber necklaces, which are a strangulation and choking hazard and have no mechanism.

Teething and your sleep plan

A genuinely uncomfortable baby gets comfort. Give pain relief if your pediatrician agrees, offer a cold teether before bed, then follow the same bedtime and response you always use. Changing the sleep routine for teething is how a three-day event becomes a three-month habit.

Frequently asked

Can I give Tylenol before bed every night for teething?

No. Routine nightly dosing is not appropriate for teething and can mask other problems. Use it for clear discomfort, as your pediatrician directs, for a short period.

How long does teething disrupt sleep?

Typically a few nights around a tooth breaking through. Longer than that points to a sleep pattern.

Do teething gels help at night?

Benzocaine gels are not recommended for children under 2 and wash away quickly in any case. A chilled teether is safer and works about as well.
