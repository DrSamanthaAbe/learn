How do I keep my baby or toddler sleeping well when we move house?

Keep everything about sleep the same except the address: the same crib, sleep sack, routine, white noise and bedtime. Set up your child’s room first and make it safe: anchor dressers and TVs to the wall, gate the stairs, and check the crib is assembled correctly. Expect a few unsettled nights, then return to your usual response.

Before the move

  • One change at a time. Don’t combine the move with switching to a bed, dropping a nap, night weaning or toilet training. The AAP gives the same advice for a new baby: make big changes well before, or wait until things are settled.
  • Talk about it simply. For a toddler: “We’re moving to a new house. Your bed and your bunny are coming too.” Picture books and photos of the new room help.
  • Pack the sleep kit last. Crib sheets, sleep sacks, white noise machine, blackout covers, lovey, favorite books. Label it and keep it with you, not in the moving truck.

Moving day and the first night

  • Set up the child’s room first, before the kitchen.
  • Same crib, same mattress, same sheet. A familiar smell and feel helps.
  • If the crib isn’t ready, use a play yard that meets federal requirements, with only its fitted sheet. The CPSC lists cribs, bassinets, play yards and bedside sleepers as products safe for sleep. Not an air mattress, an adult bed, or a sofa.
  • Blackout and white noise make a new room feel like the old one and mask new household sounds.
  • Keep bedtime and the routine the same, even if boxes are everywhere.

Make the new room safe

A new home has new hazards, and moving week is when furniture is most likely to be left unsecured. The CPSC reports that from 2000 to 2021, 482 children were killed in tip-over incidents, and 88% of them were under 5. It also estimates an average of 19,400 emergency department visits a year for tip-over injuries.

The CPSC advises:

  • Anchor TVs and furniture, such as bookcases and dressers, securely to the wall.
  • Store heavier items on lower shelves or in lower drawers.
  • Don’t put toys or remotes where children might climb to reach them.
  • Keep TV and cable cords out of reach.

The AAP adds: use a safety gate at the top of the stairs, and consider one at the bedroom door, so a toddler who gets out of bed can’t wander. Check where the crib sits too: away from windows, blind cords, curtains, lamps and anything your child could pull in. Corded cameras should be at least three feet from the crib, per the CPSC.

The first week

  • Expect some unsettled nights. More waking, earlier waking, or a harder bedtime are common after a move.
  • Comfort, but keep your usual approach. Extra reassurance is fine. New habits, like lying down until your toddler is asleep every night, are hard to stop later.
  • Get outside in the morning. Daylight and activity help everyone’s body clock, especially after a long move.
  • Explore the house in daylight. A toddler who knows where you sleep, and that you are close, settles more easily.

Moving across time zones

If the move also changes the clock, shift your child to local time using daylight and meal times. See sleep and time zones.

Moving into a shared room

If siblings will share a room in the new house, see a baby and toddler sharing a room.

When sleep doesn’t settle

Many children adjust within a week or two. If sleep is still poor after that, the move may have started a new habit rather than a lasting problem. Return to the routine and response that worked before.

Frequently asked

How long does it take a toddler to adjust to a new house?

Many children settle within a week or two if the crib, routine and bedtime stay the same. If sleep is still poor after that, look for new habits that started during the move and return to your usual approach.

Should I move my toddler to a big bed when we move?

It is usually easier to change one thing at a time. Keep the crib for the move, let your child settle in, then switch to a bed later if needed.

Can my baby sleep in our bed for the first few nights in the new house?

No. The AAP does not support bed sharing under any circumstances. Set up the crib first, or use a play yard with only its fitted sheet until the crib is ready.
