Can you sleep train a baby with reflux?

Yes, once reflux is being managed with your pediatrician and your baby is growing well. Reflux babies still sleep on their backs on a firm, flat surface, with no inclines. First: medical before behavioral

Sleep coaching does not treat reflux. If your baby is in pain, arching, refusing feeds, or not gaining weight, that is a conversation with your pediatrician first. Once reflux is managed (through feeding changes, or medication if your pediatrician prescribes it), sleep becomes something you can change. Many reflux babies keep sleeping poorly for behavioral reasons layered on top of a medical problem that is already under control.

That leftover sleep problem is the fixable part, and it is what Actually Sleeping does. Dr. Sam writes a plan for your baby that takes the reflux history into account, and you are coached hard before night one, so you go in confident.

Safe sleep does not change

The American Academy of Pediatrics says babies with reflux should still sleep on their backs, and that back-sleeping babies are not more likely to choke, even when they spit up. Your baby should sleep on a firm, flat mattress in a crib, bassinet or play yard, free from soft bedding. According to the AAP:

  • Elevating the head of the crib does not reduce reflux, and it can be unsafe.
  • The AAP does not recommend letting infants sleep in inclined products, and there is evidence that a semi-inclined position can make reflux worse.
  • The AAP does not recommend sleep positioners or nests.
  • Car seats, swings and reclined bouncers are not safe places for sleep. If your baby falls asleep in one, move them to their crib or bassinet.

Any sleep plan that relies on inclines, wedges or positioners is not one you should follow.

Why reflux babies often keep waking after the reflux is managed

Reflux babies have usually, and rightly, been picked up at every sound. Many have fallen asleep upright, feeding or being held for months, because that was what made them comfortable. When the reflux settles, the habits stay. Your baby still needs that help between sleep cycles, so the night stays broken even though the pain has eased.

Why a generic plan often goes wrong for a reflux baby

A standard plan assumes a baby with no medical history. For a reflux baby, feed timing, time upright before sleep and the pace of change all need to fit that baby. Go too fast and a baby who learned that crying means pain can become more upset. Rescue too often and nothing changes. Getting that balance right for one particular baby is hard to do from a book.

Frequently asked

Does sleep training make reflux worse?

Sleep coaching does not treat reflux, and it should start once reflux is being managed with your pediatrician. Some babies spit up more when upset, which is one reason Actually Sleeping paces plans for reflux babies carefully and adjusts them daily. Any change in symptoms goes to your pediatrician.

At what age can a reflux baby sleep through the night?

Actually Sleeping works with families from the newborn stage, and the 14-day guarantee clock starts at the 10-week birthday. For a reflux baby, the plan starts once reflux is being managed. Night feeds stay in the plan until your pediatrician says otherwise.

Should I use an incline for a reflux baby?

No. The AAP does not recommend inclined sleep products or sleep positioners, and says raising the head of the crib does not reduce reflux. Babies with reflux sleep on their backs on a firm, flat surface with nothing else in the crib.
