Can you sleep train a baby with reflux?

Yes, once reflux is managed with your pediatrician and your baby is gaining weight well. Reflux babies usually need a few adjustments — an earlier last feed, time upright before lying down, and a slower pace — but the core approach is the same, and they can sleep through the night like any other baby.

First: medical before behavioral

Sleep coaching does not treat reflux. If your baby is in pain, arching, refusing feeds, or not gaining weight, that is a conversation with your pediatrician first. Once reflux is managed (through feeding changes, positioning, or medication if prescribed), sleep becomes trainable. Many reflux babies sleep poorly for behavioral reasons layered on top of a medical one that is already under control.

Safe sleep does not change

The American Academy of Pediatrics is clear: reflux babies sleep on their backs, on a flat surface. Inclined sleepers, wedges and positioners are not safe. Elevation tricks are not part of any responsible plan.

Adjustments that help

  • Finish the last feed 20–30 minutes before lying down. Keep baby upright during that time; make it the quiet part of the routine.
  • Smaller, more frequent daytime feeds if your pediatrician agrees, so the stomach is less full at bedtime.
  • Burp thoroughly and avoid vigorous play after feeds.
  • Go slower on changing the response if your baby has learned that crying means pain. Reflux babies have often been (rightly) picked up at every sound; the plan has to rebuild trust that the crib is comfortable.

What to expect

Nights often resolve on the same timeline as non-reflux babies once the medical side is settled. The difference is mostly in how carefully the first few nights are paced, which is where having a coach who has seen dozens of reflux babies prevents overcorrection in either direction.

Frequently asked

Does sleep training make reflux worse?

Not when reflux is managed and feeds are timed away from sleep. Crying can increase spit-up in the moment, which is one reason reflux babies are coached with a gentler pace.

At what age can a reflux baby sleep through the night?

Many can by 4–6 months once reflux is controlled and weight gain is on track. Your pediatrician decides when night feeds can go.

Should I use an incline for a reflux baby?

No. The AAP advises against inclined sleep products for all infants, reflux or not.
