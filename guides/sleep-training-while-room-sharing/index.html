Can I sleep train while room-sharing?

Yes. The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that babies sleep in the parents’ room, on their own separate sleep surface, ideally for at least the first 6 months, and you can teach independent sleep in that arrangement. It usually takes a few nights longer, because your baby can see, hear and smell you. Blocking the line of sight, using white noise and choosing a parent-present method make it workable.

Room-sharing is not bed-sharing

Room-sharing means your baby sleeps in a crib or bassinet in your room: on the back, on a firm, flat mattress, with nothing else in the sleep space. The AAP recommends it and recommends against sharing a bed. Never let your baby sleep on a sofa or armchair, and do not use inclined sleepers, wedges or positioners.

Why it is harder

  • Babies stir between sleep cycles, see or smell a parent, and call for the usual help.
  • Parents hear every grunt and respond before the baby has fully woken. Babies are noisy in light sleep.
  • Parents’ coughing, phones and movement wake the baby, particularly after about 4 a.m. when sleep is lightest.

Set up the room

  • Distance. Put the crib as far from your bed as the room allows.
  • Break the line of sight. Use a freestanding room divider or screen placed well away from the crib, or position the crib around a corner. Never drape fabric over or on the crib.
  • White noise. Continuous, moderate volume, placed between your bed and the crib and not inside or right beside it.
  • Darkness. Blackout the window; no screens in bed.

Methods that fit

MethodHow it adapts to a shared room
Chair methodThe most natural fit: sit by the crib, then move toward your bed, then to the door
Pick up, put downWorks for babies of about 3–6 months; settle from beside the crib
Timed checksAt bedtime, leave the room and check as usual. Overnight, give brief verbal reassurance from your bed at the same intervals, without getting up
Bedtime fadingUnchanged; it depends on timing, not on where the crib is

Night by night

  1. Bedtime: do the routine, put your baby down awake, and apply your method. Stay out of the room afterwards until you go to bed yourself.
  2. Coming to bed: enter quietly in the dark. No lights, no checking the crib up close.
  3. Night wakings: pause for a few minutes before doing anything; many wakings end without you. Then respond as your method says, and feed at planned feed times.
  4. Early morning: the hardest stretch. Treat anything before your set wake time as night. See early morning waking.

Some parents sleep in another room for the first 3 to 5 nights so the baby can practice without an audience. Under 6 months, weigh that against the AAP recommendation to room-share and talk to your pediatrician.

After 6 months

Moving a baby to their own room after 6 months is a family decision. Many babies sleep longer stretches once they are no longer woken by their parents, and many families room-share far longer by choice or necessity. Both can work. If you share one room for the long term, as in a studio or one-bedroom home, the setup above matters more, and the timeline is usually 1 to 2 weeks.

The same principles apply to siblings sharing a room; see sleep training twins. General safety points are in safe sleep and sleep training.

Frequently asked

Can you sleep train a baby in the same room as you?

Yes. Use a parent-present method or adapted timed checks, block the line of sight between your bed and the crib, and run white noise. Expect it to take a few nights longer than in a separate room.

Should I move my baby to their own room to sleep train?

Not before 6 months unless you have discussed it with your pediatrician; the AAP recommends room-sharing ideally for at least the first 6 months. After that it is your choice, and it often makes training simpler.

Does room-sharing cause more night wakings?

It can after the first few months, because babies and parents disturb each other. Good room setup and independent sleep skills reduce this considerably.

Can I sleep train in a one-bedroom apartment?

Yes. Put the crib as far from the bed as possible, use a divider placed away from the crib and white noise, and consider sleeping in the living room for the first few nights if your baby is over 6 months.
