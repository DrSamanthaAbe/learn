Which sleep training method is gentlest? Methods compared

Measured by crying per night, bedtime fading and the parent-present methods (the chair method and pick-up-put-down) are the gentlest, and they take the longest, typically 1 to 3 weeks. Timed checks and extinction involve more crying on the first nights but usually finish in 3 to 7 nights. Research has not shown that any one method is better or worse for a child’s wellbeing, so the best choice is the one that fits your baby’s age and that you can apply consistently.

What “gentle” means

Parents use the word in three different ways, and they point to different methods:

  • Least crying on any one night: bedtime fading, then pick-up-put-down and the chair method.
  • Least crying in total: not clearly any of them. Gradual methods spread protest over more nights, and no study has shown that total crying is lower.
  • Most parental presence: the chair method and pick-up-put-down.

No method is cry-free. Changing how a baby falls asleep is frustrating for the baby, and crying is how babies express frustration. What you can choose is how you respond to it.

What the research says about gentleness

The 2016 randomized trial that compared graduated extinction, bedtime fading and a control group found that both active methods improved sleep, and found no differences between groups in infant cortisol or in attachment a year later. The five-year follow-up of an Australian trial that used timed checks and a parent-present method found no lasting differences in children’s emotional health. In short, the evidence does not show that a faster method harms a baby or that a slower one protects them. Details are in what the research says.

How to choose

  1. Start with age. Under 4 months: no formal method. At 4 to 6 months: fading, pick-up-put-down or gentle checks. From 6 months: any method. Toddlers in a bed: the chair method with clear limits; see when a toddler won’t stay in bed.
  2. Watch your baby’s reaction to you. If your presence calms them, a parent-present method fits. If seeing you and not being picked up makes them more upset, leaving the room is often kinder.
  3. Be honest about your own stamina. Three hard nights or three slower weeks? Pick the one you will finish.
  4. Agree with the other caregiver before night one.

What matters more than the method

Schedule, feeding plan and consistency account for most of the result. A well-timed bedtime with a gradual method beats a badly timed bedtime with any method. Much of the crying parents fear is avoidable with the right preparation, which is the main thing an individual plan adds. Frequently asked

Is there a no-cry sleep training method?

No method can promise no crying. Bedtime fading and parent-present methods usually involve the least intense crying, and good preparation of schedule and routine reduces it further.

Is gentle sleep training as effective as Ferber?

Gradual methods can reach the same result but take longer, typically 1 to 3 weeks compared with 3 to 7 nights. In the one trial that compared them directly, both bedtime fading and graduated extinction improved sleep.

Which sleep training method is fastest?

Extinction is usually fastest at 3 to 5 nights, followed by timed checks at 3 to 7 nights. Both involve more crying on the first nights than gradual methods.

Can I combine sleep training methods?

Yes, if you plan the combination in advance, for example bedtime fading for a week and then the chair method. What does not work is changing methods in the middle of a night or every other night.
