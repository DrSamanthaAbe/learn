Which sleep training method is gentlest? Methods compared

Measured by crying per night, bedtime fading and the parent-present methods (the chair method and pick-up-put-down) are usually the gentlest, and they tend to be the slowest. Timed checks and extinction involve more crying at first. Research has not shown that a faster method harms a child or that a slower one protects them. What matters most is a plan that fits your baby.

What “gentle” means

Parents use the word in three different ways, and they point to different methods:

  • Least crying on any one night: bedtime fading, then pick-up-put-down and the chair method.
  • Least crying in total: not clearly any of them. Gradual methods spread protest over more nights, and no study has shown that total crying is lower.
  • Most parental presence: the chair method and pick-up-put-down.

No method is cry-free. Changing how a baby falls asleep is frustrating for the baby, and crying is how babies express frustration. What you can choose is how you respond to it, and how well the plan fits your baby so that the protest is short.

What the research says about gentleness

A 2016 randomized trial of 43 babies aged 6 to 16 months compared graduated extinction, bedtime fading and a sleep-education control. Both methods produced large reductions in the time to fall asleep; only graduated extinction clearly reduced night wakings. Infant cortisol showed small-to-moderate declines in both method groups compared with controls, and 12 months later there were no differences in emotional or behavioral problems or attachment. The evidence does not show that a faster method harms a baby or that a slower one protects them. Details are in what the research says.

Why the method name matters less than the plan

Most stalled attempts are not caused by choosing the wrong method from this table. They come from a bedtime that is wrong for the baby, night feeds without a plan, a response that does not suit the baby’s temperament, or adults responding differently. A well-timed bedtime with a gradual approach does better than a badly timed bedtime with any approach. Much of the crying parents fear comes from those details, not from the method itself.

Frequently asked

Is there a no-cry sleep training method?

No approach can honestly promise no crying. Bedtime fading and parent-present methods usually involve the least intense crying. A plan matched to your baby’s schedule and temperament reduces protest further.

Is gentle sleep training as effective as Ferber?

In the one trial that compared them directly, bedtime fading and graduated extinction both shortened the time to fall asleep; only graduated extinction clearly reduced night waking. Gentler approaches tend to need more nights and more precise timing.

Which sleep training method is fastest?

Extinction and timed checks are usually faster than gradual methods but involve more crying on the first nights. Actually Sleeping uses neither and puts the timeline in writing: 8–12 hours through the night within 14 days of consistent, correct implementation, or a full refund.

Can I combine sleep training methods?

Combining approaches can work when it is planned for a specific reason. Switching methods in the middle of a night, or every other night, usually makes things worse. In an Actually Sleeping plan, Dr. Sam decides any change with you during daily check-ins.
