Sleep at 8 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to call the doctor

At 8 months, the AASM range is 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps. Clinginess and bedtime protest are very common because stranger and separation anxiety are emerging; the AAP says separation anxiety usually peaks between 10 and 18 months. SIDS becomes less common after 8 months, but safe sleep rules still apply until the first birthday.

What is changing at 8 months

Your baby now knows exactly who you are, and minds when you leave. The AAP describes stranger anxiety as one of the first emotional milestones and says separation anxiety starts around the same time, usually peaking between 10 and 18 months. By 9 months, the CDC lists being shy, clingy or fearful around strangers and reacting when you leave by looking, reaching or crying.

Physically, many babies are pulling toward sitting, crawling and the first attempts to stand. The AAP and MedlinePlus say the crib mattress should be at its lowest setting before your baby can stand.

Nightmares are possible too. The AAP says they can start as young as 6 months and tend to come in the second half of the night. A baby who wakes frightened and settles quickly once you arrive is behaving normally. Studies of babies 7 to 11 months recorded about one night waking on average, with up to about three still within the normal range.

What parents find hardest

  • Crying the moment you leave the room. The AAP notes children accept separation more easily when it is part of a predictable routine. A short, identical goodbye each night helps.
  • Waking and calling for a parent. The AAP says a child with separation anxiety may need reassurance several times, night after night. Keep visits brief and boring.
  • Practising crawling or standing in the crib. Floor time in the day takes the novelty out of it.
  • The 3-to-2 nap transition. Studies of babies 6 to 11 months found an average of about 2 naps a day; most 8 month olds are on two, as a common pattern.

Night feeds

Separation anxiety can look like hunger. A baby who wants you may also want to feed. Ask your pediatrician whether your baby still needs a night feed; if yes, keep it, and keep it calm and separate from falling asleep.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Waking with fever, ear pain, vomiting, or a cough that is worse lying down.
  • Regular snoring, mouth breathing or pauses in breathing at night.
  • Your baby does not seem to know familiar people, or does not respond to their name by 9 months.
  • Anxiety so intense that daytime life is very hard, or that does not ease at all over months.

Where to go from here

Separation anxiety is a reason to be warm and predictable at bedtime, not a reason to abandon a plan. Frequently asked

Why does my 8 month old cry as soon as I leave the room at bedtime?

This is usually separation anxiety, a normal stage. The AAP says it often begins in the second half of the first year and usually peaks between 10 and 18 months. A short, predictable goodbye and calm, brief returns work better than sneaking out.

Is SIDS still a risk at 8 months?

Less so, but not zero. The NICHD says SIDS is less common after 8 months and that safe sleep practices should continue until the first birthday. Keep your baby on the back to start, on a firm, flat, empty surface.

When do I lower the crib mattress all the way?

Before your baby can stand. MedlinePlus and the AAP say to have the mattress at its lowest position before your child learns to stand. At 8 months, many babies are close, so it is safer to do it now.
