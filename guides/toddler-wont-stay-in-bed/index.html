My toddler won’t stay in bed. How do I fix it?

Most toddlers who will not stay in bed have learned that getting up works: it brings a parent, a drink, a story or a spot in the big bed. Many also left the crib before they had the self-control a bed demands. It is fixable with a clear routine and the same response every time, set by a plan built for your toddler.

Why toddlers get up

  • It works. Every return that comes with a conversation, a cuddle or a negotiation teaches the toddler that getting up is worth it.
  • Freedom before self-control. A bed gives a young toddler freedom to leave with little ability to stop themselves. Many families who moved early find the crib was holding everything together.
  • Inconsistency. Two parents with two responses, or a response that changes after the tenth trip, gives a toddler a reason to keep testing.
  • Real fears. Fears of the dark and of separation appear in the toddler years and are real. How they are handled decides whether they fade or grow.

Crib or bed

A crib does a lot of quiet work. It sets a physical limit, so the only question at bedtime is whether the child falls asleep, not whether they stay put. A bed removes that limit and hands the decision to a toddler who is still learning to control impulses. Families often move to a bed because of a new sibling, a climbing toddler or a sense that it is time. Whether to stay in the crib, move back, or make the bed work is a decision that depends on the child, the reason for the move and safety, and it is one of the first things a plan settles.

Safety first

A toddler who can leave the bed will sometimes be up alone. Make the room safe for that: furniture anchored to the wall, outlets covered, cords out of reach, windows secured, nothing to climb. Many families use a gate at the door. If your toddler climbs out of a crib, talk to your pediatrician about the safest setup.

Why the usual fixes stall

Most of what parents try is reasonable. It stalls because toddlers are expert at finding the one thing that still works, and because exhausted parents vary at 9 p.m. A plan written for your child removes the guessing, and daily check-ins keep both parents on the same response.
