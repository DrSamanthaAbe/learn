My toddler won’t stay in bed. How do I fix it?

First, if your child is under 3 and still fits safely in a crib, consider going back to it; most bed problems are a too-early crib exit. If a bed is where you are, the fix is a short consistent routine, a clear rule (“we stay in bed until the light turns green”), and a boring, silent, repeated return to bed every single time they get up — dozens of times the first night if needed, fewer each night after. Most toddlers stop within one to two weeks.

Before anything else: crib or bed?

The AAP suggests waiting until closer to 3 for a bed unless the child is climbing out dangerously. A 20-month-old in a bed has freedom with no impulse control. If you moved early, moving back is not a failure; it is often the fastest fix. If climbing is the issue, lower the mattress, use a sleep sack (it limits leg lift), and remove anything used as a step.

If the bed is staying

  1. Make the room safe enough to be unsupervised. Anchored furniture, covered outlets, a gate or closed door, nothing climbable. Assume the child will be up alone.
  2. A short routine with a clear ending. Same order nightly, 20–30 minutes, ending with one phrase that means “done.” No renegotiation after it.
  3. State the rule once. “You stay in bed until the light is green.” A toddler clock helps from about 2.5 years.
  4. The silent return. Every time the child gets up, walk them back without talking, without eye contact, without a cuddle, and leave. The first night may be 30–50 returns. It drops fast when the payoff is zero.
  5. Pre-empt the requests. Water bottle in reach, last bathroom trip in the routine, one “bedtime pass” they can trade for one visit if that fits your child.
  6. Morning reward, not bedtime bribes: a sticker or praise at wake-up for staying in bed.

What makes it worse

Lying down with them “just tonight.” Letting them fall asleep on the couch. Long explanations at 9 p.m. Getting angry (attention is attention). Inconsistency between parents. Each of these resets the count.

Frequently asked

Should I lock my toddler’s door?

A gate at the door or a door knob cover is a common compromise; many families use a closed door for safety. Whatever you choose, the room itself must be fully childproofed.

How long will the silent return take to work?

Typically a dramatic drop in 3–4 nights and near-zero in 1–2 weeks, if it is done every time.

My toddler is scared of the dark. Is that real?

Fears emerge around 2–3 years and are real. A dim warm nightlight and a consistent, calm response help; staying in the room until they fall asleep tends to prolong it.
