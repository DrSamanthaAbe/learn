Sleep at 3 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 3 months, crying usually starts to ease and nights may get a little more organized, but there is still no AASM sleep target until 4 months. The main changes are physical: rolling attempts can start any time now (the AAP notes some babies begin as early as 2 months), so the swaddle has to go at the first sign, and the bassinet will soon be outgrown.

What is changing at 3 months

Colic is on its way out for most families. The AAP says colicky fussing declines to 1 or 2 hours a day by 3 to 4 months and generally stops by then, though it can last until 6 months.

Your baby is getting stronger. By 4 months, the CDC lists pushing up onto elbows or forearms during tummy time, holding the head steady, and bringing hands to mouth. Hands to mouth is a real self-settling tool, and many babies begin sucking fingers in the crib now.

The body clock is maturing. The AAP says regular sleep cycles appear at about 4 months, so the next few weeks are a period of change rather than a settled state.

What parents find hardest

  • Leaving the swaddle. It has to go at the first sign of rolling, and many babies sleep worse for a while with free arms.
  • Naps that stay short. One cycle and awake is still common. Daytime sleeps averaged about 3 a day in studies of babies 0 to 5 months, with a lot of variation.
  • Newborn tricks stop working. Bouncing and rocking often lose their effect as your baby becomes more alert, just as sleep is about to change at 4 months.

Safe sleep at 3 months

  • Back to sleep for every sleep. Rolling is about to make positioning harder; the rule does not change until your baby can roll both ways.
  • No weighted products, no inclined surfaces over 10 degrees, no bumpers.
  • Keep room-sharing, ideally for at least the first 6 months, per the AAP. Bed-sharing risk is highest under 4 months.
  • Most SIDS deaths happen between 1 and 4 months, according to the NICHD. Do not relax the basics yet.

Night feeds

Some babies naturally space out night feeds now; others do not. Either way, how night feeds are handled depends on your baby’s age and is set in your custom plan, not on a calendar.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • A rectal temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher in a baby 3 months or younger: call immediately.
  • Crying that is increasing rather than settling, or continues past what you were told to expect.
  • Frequent snoring, struggling to breathe, or breathing pauses longer than 20 seconds (call 911).
  • Not yet bringing hands to mouth or lifting the head and chest on the tummy as 4 months approaches.

Frequently asked

Is it normal for my 3 month old to still cry in the evenings?

Yes, though it should be easing. The AAP says colicky fussing usually falls to 1 or 2 hours a day by 3 to 4 months and can last until 6 months. If it is getting worse, or comes with poor feeding or fever, call your pediatrician.

When should my 3 month old move out of the bassinet?

The CPSC says bassinets and cradles are not intended for use beyond about 5 months, or once a baby can push up on hands and knees, whichever comes first. Check your product’s own limits, which may be lower, and move to a crib in your room.

How much should a 3 month old sleep?

The AASM sets no target before 4 months. In a review of studies, babies from 3 months averaged 13.6 hours in 24 hours, with a normal spread from about 9 to 18 hours. Look at how your baby feeds, grows and behaves, not just the hours.
