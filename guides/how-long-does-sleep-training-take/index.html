Why nights and naps move at different speeds

Night sleep usually improves first. By bedtime your baby has been awake for most of the day and is ready to sleep. Naps are shorter, there is less tiredness behind them, and one short nap can knock the rest of the day off course. So naps often lag behind nights, even when nights are going well. That is normal. It is not a sign the plan has failed.

Actually Sleeping is set up around the things that make sleep training drag on. Dr. Sam writes a plan for your baby, you are coached hard before night one, so you go in confident, and daily 1:1 check-ins catch a slipping response or a schedule that does not fit within days, not weeks.

What makes it take longer

  • Inconsistency. Responding one way on night 1 and another way on night 4 teaches your baby that protesting longer works. This is the most common story behind “we tried for weeks and it didn’t take.”
  • The old way of falling asleep is still in place. If a feed, rocking or a pacifier is needed to fall asleep at bedtime, it is needed again between sleep cycles.
  • A day that does not fit the baby. Overtired and under-tired babies both fight sleep. Age charts describe an average baby, not yours.
  • Illness or a big developmental change in the middle of the plan.

Why a generic timeline does not fit your baby

Timelines you read online assume an average baby. Yours has a particular age, feeding pattern, temperament and way of falling asleep, and a family schedule built around them. Each of those changes what the plan should look like and how quickly it works. A 5-month-old who feeds to sleep, a baby with reflux and a toddler who climbs out of bed need different plans. Frequently asked

How long does sleep training take for a 6 month old?

There is no fixed number of nights for every 6-month-old. It depends on how your baby falls asleep now, feeding, and how consistently the plan is followed. With Actually Sleeping, the guarantee covers 8–12 hours within 14 days of consistent, correct implementation, and night feeds stay until your pediatrician says otherwise.

How long does sleep training take for a toddler?

Toddlers often learn quickly once the message is consistent, and they test any inconsistency. A toddler in a bed who can leave the room needs a plan that covers that. Actually Sleeping works with babies and toddlers from newborn on, including toddlers in beds, with a plan written by Dr. Sam and daily check-ins.

Is it normal for sleep training to take 3 weeks?

For naps, slower progress can be normal. For nights, three weeks without real progress usually means something in the plan or the consistency needs to change. That is what daily check-ins are for: a coach spots the problem within days and adjusts the plan for your baby.
