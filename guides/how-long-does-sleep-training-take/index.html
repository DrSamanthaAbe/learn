How long does sleep training take?

With a consistent plan, most babies over 10 weeks sleep through the night within 3 to 7 nights, and almost all within 14. Naps take longer — typically 2 to 4 weeks to become reliable — which is why nights and naps should be treated as two separate projects.

Nights: 3–14 days

Night sleep responds fastest because sleep pressure is highest at bedtime and the body’s clock is on your side. Families in Actually Sleeping coaching commonly report the first full night in the first one to five nights; the written guarantee is 8–12 hours within 14 days of consistent, correct implementation.

Naps: 2–4 weeks

Daytime sleep pressure is lower, naps are shorter, and one bad nap cascades into the next. Expect naps to lag nights by a few weeks even when nights are solved. This is normal, not a sign the plan failed.

What makes it take longer

  • Inconsistency. Responding differently on night 4 than night 1 teaches the baby that persistence works. This is the single biggest cause of “we tried for three weeks and it didn’t take.”
  • Sleep props still in place. Feeding, rocking or a pacifier that is required to fall asleep will be required again between sleep cycles.
  • Wrong schedule. Overtired or undertired babies fight sleep. Wake windows need to match the baby, not the chart.
  • Illness or a developmental leap mid-plan. Pause, then resume the same plan; do not start a new one.

When to expect regressions

Around 4 months (sleep cycles mature), 8–10 months (crawling, standing, separation anxiety), 12 months and 18 months. A trained baby usually returns to baseline in a few nights if the response stays consistent.

Frequently asked

How long does sleep training take for a 6 month old?

Usually 3–7 nights for nights, with naps settling over the following 2–4 weeks. Six months is one of the easier ages because most babies no longer need night feeds and are not yet mobile.

How long does sleep training take for a toddler?

Toddlers in a crib respond in about the same time as babies. Toddlers in a bed take longer because they can leave the room; expect 1–2 weeks of consistent boundaries.

Is it normal for sleep training to take 3 weeks?

For naps, yes. For nights, three weeks without progress usually means something in the plan or the consistency needs to change, which is exactly where daily coaching earns its keep.
