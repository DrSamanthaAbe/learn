Are baby loungers, inclined sleepers and sleep positioners safe for sleep?

No. Inclined sleepers with a sleep surface over 10 degrees are banned in the U.S. under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act. The CPSC says never to use infant support cushions, which include loungers and nursing-and-lounging pillows, for sleep or naps, and the FDA says never to use sleep positioners. Your baby should sleep flat on their back with only a fitted sheet.

Inclined sleepers

Under the Safe Sleep for Babies Act, an inclined sleeper is a product with a sleep surface inclined more than 10 degrees that is meant to provide sleep for a baby up to 1 year old. Since November 12, 2022, these are banned hazardous products in the United States.

The AAP’s parent site says any surface that inclines more than 10 degrees isn’t safe for a baby to sleep on. The CPSC tells parents not to let a baby sleep in any inclined product over 10 degrees, such as a rocker, bouncer, swing or glider. In its 2018 to 2020 data, the CPSC counted an average of 15 deaths a year associated with inclined infant products not intended for sleep.

Loungers and support cushions

The CPSC has approved a federal safety standard for infant support cushions. The products it covers include:

  • Infant loungers
  • Infant head positioner pillows
  • Infant sleep positioners and anti-rollover pillows
  • Multi-purpose pillows marketed for both nursing and lounging

The CPSC reports 79 infant deaths and 124 injuries associated with these cushions between 2010 and 2022. Its advice is clear: never use infant support cushions for infant sleep or naps. If your baby falls asleep on one, move them to a crib, bassinet or play yard.

Second-hand loungers and pillows are worth a second look too: older products were made before the new standard. A lounger is not a place to sleep even when you are in the room. Many of these products are used for supervised lounging during the day; the danger is a baby dozing off and being left there.

Sleep positioners and wedges

The FDA tells parents to never use infant sleep positioners, the mats with raised side bolsters or wedges meant to hold a baby on their back or side. It warns that they can cause suffocation and death, and that it has never cleared a sleep positioner that claims to prevent or reduce the risk of SIDS. The NIH Safe to Sleep campaign adds that wedges, positioners and similar products do not meet federal guidelines for sleep safety.

What about reflux or a stuffy nose

These are the two most common reasons parents want to raise a baby’s head. The AAP’s answer is the same for both: propping a baby on towels or pillows, or inclining the mattress, is not safe. Babies should sleep flat on their backs on a firm, even surface. For reflux, see sleep and reflux; for congestion, see humidifiers and stuffy noses. If symptoms worry you, call your pediatrician rather than changing the sleep surface.

Frequently asked

Is a baby lounger okay if I am watching?

Supervised awake time is a different use, but the CPSC says never use infant support cushions for sleep or naps. If your baby starts to fall asleep, move them to a crib, bassinet or play yard on their back.

My inclined sleeper was bought before the ban. Is it safe to use?

No. The Safe Sleep for Babies Act treats inclined sleepers as banned hazardous products regardless of when they were made, and the AAP says surfaces inclined more than 10 degrees aren’t safe for sleep.

Can I use a wedge under the mattress for reflux?

No. The AAP says inclining the mattress or propping your baby up is not safe, and the FDA warns against wedges and positioners. Babies sleep flat on their backs. Talk to your pediatrician about reflux symptoms instead.
