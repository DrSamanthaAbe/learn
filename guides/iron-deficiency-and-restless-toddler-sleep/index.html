Can low iron (ferritin) cause restless sleep in a toddler?

It can play a part. Restless legs that disturb sleep are listed by the AAP as a sign of more significant iron-deficiency anemia, and sleep guidelines ask clinicians to check ferritin in children with restless legs. Iron deficiency in infancy has also been linked to altered sleep patterns later. Never start iron on your own; ask your child’s pediatrician about testing.

The short answer

Iron matters for the brain, and low iron has real links to sleep. But restless sleep in toddlers has many possible causes, and iron supplements are dangerous in an accidental overdose. The right step is a conversation with the pediatrician and, if they think it fits, a blood test. Not a supplement from the store.

What the evidence says

  • Signs of iron deficiency. The AAP lists pale skin, irritability, fatigue or needing more naps, and cravings for non-food items such as ice or dirt. In more severe anemia it lists restless leg syndrome that interferes with healthy sleep.
  • Long-term sleep effects. A study of 4-year-olds followed from infancy found that children who had iron-deficiency anemia as babies had differences in how their sleep stages were organized across the night on overnight sleep studies, compared with children who had not.
  • Restless legs in children. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine’s 2025 guideline says clinicians should check iron studies, including ferritin, in patients with clinically significant restless legs syndrome, and it suggests iron treatment for children with restless legs when ferritin is below a set level. Which test, which product and how long are clinical decisions.
  • Restless sleep disorder. Sleep experts have defined a separate “restless sleep disorder” with criteria that include a sleep study, but they note the current evidence applies to ages 6 to 18. In toddlers, restless sleep is a symptom to evaluate, not a diagnosis to assume.

Who is at higher risk

  • Too much cow’s milk. The AAP advises no cow’s milk before 12 months, and after age one no more than 24 ounces a day, because milk is low in iron and fills children up.
  • Breastfed babies after 4 months without iron-rich foods yet. The AAP recommends iron supplementation for partially or fully breastfed babies starting at 4 months until iron-containing foods are eaten; ask your pediatrician how this applies to your baby.
  • Babies born early or small, whom the AAP flags for earlier iron support.

The AAP calls for iron screening around the first birthday, with timing that depends partly on how your baby is fed.

Why not just try an iron supplement

Federal rules require this warning on iron products: “Accidental overdose of iron-containing products is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6.” Iron given without a test can also mask the real cause of a sleep problem. If your child ever swallows iron pills or chewables, call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 immediately.

What to ask the pediatrician

  • “My toddler is restless, kicks or seems uncomfortable at night. Should we check iron and ferritin?”
  • “How much milk should my child drink each day?”
  • “Could snoring, mouth breathing or pauses in breathing be part of this?”

Frequently asked

What are signs of low iron in a toddler?

The AAP lists pale skin, irritability, fatigue or needing more naps, and craving non-food items such as ice or dirt. In more severe anemia it lists restless legs that disturb sleep. Your pediatrician can check with a blood test.

Should I give my toddler an iron supplement for restless sleep?

Not without testing and your pediatrician’s direction. Federal labeling warns that accidental iron overdose is a leading cause of fatal poisoning in children under 6, and restless sleep has many other possible causes.

Can drinking too much milk affect my toddler’s sleep?

Indirectly. The AAP advises no more than 24 ounces of milk a day after age one, because milk is low in iron and can crowd out iron-rich foods. Low iron is linked to restless legs that disturb sleep.

Does iron deficiency as a baby affect sleep later?

One long-term study found 4-year-olds who had iron-deficiency anemia in infancy had differences in how their sleep stages were organized. This is one reason the AAP recommends iron screening around the first birthday.
