Why is my 12 month old suddenly waking at night?

Night waking around 12 months is usually driven by walking, separation anxiety and a nap transition that is not ready yet — most one-year-olds still need two naps until 14–18 months. Keep two naps, keep the bedtime response consistent, and the waking typically settles in 1–2 weeks.

The three usual causes

  • Gross motor leaps. Standing and walking get practiced in the crib at 3 a.m. Practice during the day; respond at night without turning it into a game.
  • Separation anxiety. Peaks around 10–14 months. A predictable routine and a consistent response reassure more than extra rocking does.
  • A premature nap transition. Many one-year-olds briefly refuse a nap and parents drop to one. The result is an overtired baby who wakes more at night. Hold two naps until 14–18 months unless the second nap is consistently refused for two weeks.

What to do

  1. Check the schedule: two naps, wake windows of roughly 3–4 hours, bedtime 7–8 p.m.
  2. Respond to wake-ups the same way you did before the regression. New habits start fast at this age.
  3. Do not reintroduce night feeds or bed-sharing to get through it unless you plan to keep them.

A previously trained baby usually returns to baseline in a few nights. If your baby was never sleeping independently, 12 months is a good age to start; toddlers respond quickly to clear, consistent boundaries.

Frequently asked

How long does the 12 month sleep regression last?

Typically 1–2 weeks with a consistent response. Longer usually means the schedule is off or the response has changed.

Should my 12 month old be on one nap?

Usually not yet. Most toddlers move to one nap between 14 and 18 months.

Is my 1 year old teething?

Maybe, but teething disrupts sleep for a few nights, not weeks. Persistent waking is a pattern.
