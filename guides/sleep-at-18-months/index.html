Sleep at 18 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 18 months, the AASM recommends 11 to 14 hours per 24 hours, including naps, and most toddlers are on one nap. Climbing, separation worries and screen habits are the common sleep disruptors. The AAP says to avoid screens before 18 months, cap entertainment screen time at 1 hour a day from 18 months to age 5, and switch screens off an hour before bed.

What is changing at 18 months

By 18 months, the CDC lists walking without holding on, climbing on and off a couch or chair without help, trying to say three or more words, and moving away from you while checking you are still close. Climbing is the one that matters most at night.

The AAP says separation anxiety usually peaks between 10 and 18 months and fades during the last half of the second year. Many toddlers are right at the top of that curve now.

The AAP notes that screen viewing contributes to problems with sleep and attention. A calm hour without screens before bed is one of the simplest changes with real backing.

What is normal

In a review of studies, children 1 to 2 years averaged 12.6 hours of sleep per 24 hours, with a normal spread of about 10 to 15 hours, about 1 nap a day, and 0.7 night wakings on average. Total nap time among nappers averaged 2.3 hours. One nap is the common pattern now, not a rule. A few toddlers still need a second short nap on some days, and that is fine.

What parents find hardest

  • Bedtime refusal. “No” is a new and powerful word. Offer small choices (which pyjamas, which book) inside a fixed routine.
  • Climbing attempts. A sleep sack makes leg-over-the-rail harder, a practical tip rather than a studied one.
  • Night waking for a parent. Often separation-related. Short, calm, identical responses work best.
  • Early waking after the move to one nap. An earlier bedtime for a few weeks usually helps.

Safe sleep at 18 months

  • Move out of the crib when your child is 35 inches (89 cm) tall or the rail is below about three-quarters of their height (AAP, MedlinePlus), or once they can climb out (AAP).
  • If they climb out before you have a bed ready, the AAP suggests putting the crib mattress on the floor.
  • Anchor dressers and other furniture so they cannot tip; keep chairs away from windows.

Night feeds

If your toddler still wakes for milk, your pediatrician can tell you whether it is needed. The AAP advises against a bottle of milk or juice in bed because of tooth decay.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Snoring most nights, pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness and irritability that could point to sleep apnea.
  • Your child is not walking, has fewer than three words, or does not point to show you things.
  • Night terrors that keep happening.

Where to go from here

Most 18-month sleep trouble is a mix of new independence and old habits. Actually Sleeping helps families sort out which is which; parents describe that on our reviews page. Related: the 18-month regression, the 18-month schedule, and separation anxiety at bedtime.

Frequently asked

How much should an 18 month old sleep?

The AASM recommends 11 to 14 hours per 24 hours, including naps, for children 1 to 2 years, and the AAP supports this. Studies found children this age averaged about 12.6 hours, with healthy children on both sides of that.

Does screen time affect my toddler’s sleep?

The AAP says screen viewing contributes to sleep problems. It advises turning off all screens at least an hour before bed, keeping devices out of the bedroom, and capping entertainment screen time at 1 hour a day from 18 months to age 5.

My 18 month old is trying to climb out of the crib. What now?

Lower the mattress fully and use a sleep sack. The AAP says the transition can happen once your toddler can climb out; if they do before you have a bed, put the crib mattress on the floor. Anchor furniture and clear the room.
