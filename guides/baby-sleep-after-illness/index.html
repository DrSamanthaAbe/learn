My baby’s sleep fell apart after being sick. How do I get back on track?

While your baby is sick, comfort comes first: hold, feed and respond as much as needed, and follow your pediatrician’s advice. Once your baby is fever-free (a day without fever is a common rule of thumb), eating and drinking normally and back to themselves by day, sleep can return to normal. The habits that crept in during the illness are the part that needs a plan.

During the illness

  • Respond freely. Extra feeds, cuddles and night checks are appropriate. Sick babies need fluids and may eat less by day and more at night.
  • Go to them rather than bringing them to you. If you want to be close, sleep on a mattress in your baby’s room instead of bringing a sick baby into an adult bed.
  • Keep sleep safe. Congested babies still sleep flat on their back in an empty crib. Inclined or tilted sleep surfaces are not safe, so do not prop the mattress, use a wedge or pillow, or let your baby sleep in a car seat or swing. Saline drops and gentle suction with a bulb syringe, plus a humidifier if the air is dry, are the usual comfort measures.
  • Medicines: fever and pain relievers only as your pediatrician directs for your baby’s age and weight. The AAP does not recommend over-the-counter cough and cold medicines for children under 4. More in over-the-counter medicines and infant sleep.
  • Let them sleep more. Extra and longer naps are part of recovery.

Comfort comes first while your baby is sick, and nothing on this page changes that. Once your baby is well, getting sleep back is the part Actually Sleeping helps with, through a plan for your baby and daily check-ins.

Call the pediatrician

Call right away for a rectal temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher in a baby 3 months or younger, even with no other symptoms. Also call for trouble breathing or fast, labored breathing; refusing feeds or far fewer wet diapers; a baby who is hard to wake or unusually limp; ear pulling with fever; or symptoms that are getting worse rather than better. Ear infections often cause pain and trouble sleeping, and only an examination can confirm one.

If you are unsure, ask the pediatrician: “Is there any medical reason not to go back to our normal sleep routine?”

Why sleep does not bounce back on its own

A week of illness changes a lot: night feeds come back, naps get long, and your baby gets used to being held or fed back to sleep. Those were the right responses while your baby was sick. Once they are well, the body has relearned a new pattern, and it tends to continue. Parents then try to undo it gradually, which often drags the disruption out for weeks.

If your baby never slept independently before the illness, there is nothing to go back to. The illness simply made an existing problem louder, and that is a first plan, not a reset.

Frequently asked

Should I keep sleep training while my baby is sick?

No. Pause, comfort your baby and follow your pediatrician’s advice. Resume the same plan once your baby is well. Keep the bedtime and routine consistent in the meantime if possible.

How long after an illness can I restart sleep training?

A common rule of thumb is once your baby has gone about a day without fever, is feeding normally and is acting like themselves. Check with your pediatrician if in doubt.

Can I raise the crib mattress to help with congestion?

No. Safe sleep guidance from the AAP and NIH says inclined or tilted sleep surfaces are not safe. Use saline, gentle suction and a humidifier if the air is dry instead.

My baby started waking to feed again when sick. Will that stop?

Often, once extra feeds are no longer offered after recovery and daytime appetite returns. If your baby lost weight during the illness, ask your pediatrician first. Night feeds are always your pediatrician’s call.
