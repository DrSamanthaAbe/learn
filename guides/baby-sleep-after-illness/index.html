My baby’s sleep fell apart after being sick. How do I get back on track?

While your baby is sick, comfort comes first: hold, feed and respond as much as needed. Once your baby has been fever-free for about 24 hours, is eating and drinking normally and is back to their usual self by day, return to your normal bedtime routine and night response that same night. A baby who slept independently before usually returns to baseline in 2–4 nights.

During the illness

  • Respond freely. Extra feeds, cuddles and night checks are appropriate. Sick babies need fluids and may eat less by day and more at night.
  • Keep the frame if you can: same bedtime, same routine, same room. Add comfort inside that frame.
  • Go to them rather than bringing them to you. If you want to be close, sleep on a mattress in your baby’s room instead of bringing a sick baby into an adult bed.
  • Keep sleep safe. Congested babies still sleep flat on their back in an empty crib. Do not prop the mattress, use a wedge or pillow, or let your baby sleep in a car seat or swing. Saline drops and gentle suction before feeds and sleep, plus a cool-mist humidifier, are the usual comfort measures.
  • Medicines: fever and pain relievers only as your pediatrician directs for your baby’s age and weight. Cough and cold products are not for young children. More in over-the-counter medicines and infant sleep.
  • Let them sleep more. Extra and longer naps are part of recovery.

Call the pediatrician

Any fever in a baby under 3 months; trouble breathing or fast, labored breathing; refusing feeds or far fewer wet diapers; a baby who is hard to wake or unusually limp; ear pulling with fever; or symptoms that are getting worse rather than better. Night waking with ear pain is a classic sign of an ear infection, because lying down increases the pressure.

How to know your baby is ready to reset

ReadyNot yet
No fever for about 24 hours without medicineFever within the last day
Eating and drinking close to normalPoor intake or fewer wet diapers
Playing and alert during the dayClingy, listless or clearly uncomfortable
A lingering mild cough or runny nose onlyBreathing that looks like work; untreated ear infection

If you are unsure, ask the pediatrician: “Is there any medical reason not to go back to our normal sleep routine?”

The reset

  1. Pick the night and go back fully. Same routine, down awake, your usual response. A slow fade tends to drag things out.
  2. Drop the extra night feeds that crept in, if your baby was not feeding overnight before and your pediatrician has no concerns. Offer more by day; appetite rebounds after illness.
  3. Trim the recovery naps back to the usual schedule over two or three days so daytime sleep stops borrowing from the night.
  4. Expect protest for 2–4 nights. It is usually shorter than the first time, because the skill is still there.
  5. Both adults respond the same way.

If illnesses keep coming

In the first daycare winter, colds can arrive every few weeks. Reset after each one; the resets get quicker. If your baby never slept independently before the illness, this is not a reset but a first attempt, and waiting until a fully healthy week is sensible. See the best age to sleep train.

Frequently asked

Should I keep sleep training while my baby is sick?

No. Pause, comfort your baby and resume the same plan once they are well. Keep the bedtime and routine consistent in the meantime if possible.

How long after an illness can I restart sleep training?

Once your baby has been fever-free for about 24 hours, is feeding normally and is acting like themselves. Check with your pediatrician if in doubt.

Can I raise the crib mattress to help with congestion?

No. The AAP advises a flat sleep surface for every sleep. Use saline, suction and a cool-mist humidifier instead.

My baby started waking to feed again when sick. Will that stop?

Usually, once you stop offering the extra feeds after recovery and add calories back during the day. If your baby lost weight during the illness, ask your pediatrician first.
