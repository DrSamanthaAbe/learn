Which of my medications could affect my breastfed baby’s sleep?

Most medications are compatible with breastfeeding, but a handful that reach milk can make a baby drowsier or more restless: sedating antihistamines, some sleep aids, certain antidepressants in the early weeks, opioid pain relievers after delivery, and decongestants. The authoritative free reference is LactMed from the National Library of Medicine; check it, and ask your pharmacist or pediatrician about anything you take regularly.

How to think about it

Three questions decide most cases: How much reaches milk? How well does a baby this age clear it? Is there a safer alternative that works as well? LactMed answers the first two for nearly every drug. Your pharmacist or prescriber can answer the third.

The bigger point

A medication explanation for night waking is occasionally right in the first weeks. After about 3 months, with a parent on a standard, breastfeeding-compatible regimen, the medication is rarely why the baby wakes at 1, 3 and 5. The sleep association is.

Frequently asked

Where can I check if a medication is safe while breastfeeding?

LactMed, free from the National Library of Medicine, is the standard reference. Your pharmacist can look up anything on the spot, and your prescriber or pediatrician can tell you what fits your situation.

Can I take something to help me sleep while breastfeeding?

Talk to your prescriber. Some options are compatible; the main cautions are parental sedation around night feeds and the added danger of falling asleep with the baby. The AAP advises against bed sharing in any case.

Will my antidepressant make my baby sleepy?

Usually not meaningfully. Keep taking it unless your prescriber says otherwise; treating postpartum depression protects your baby’s sleep and development. If your baby wakes often, the cause is far more likely a sleep pattern, which a plan can fix.
