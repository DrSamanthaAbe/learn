How do I sleep train my baby? A step-by-step guide

Sleep training means teaching a baby to fall asleep in the crib without being fed, rocked or held to sleep, so they can link sleep cycles on their own overnight. The AAP’s parent site notes that babies do not have regular sleep cycles until about 4 months. It works best with a plan built for your baby, followed consistently, with someone adjusting it as you go.

What sleep training actually changes

Every baby wakes briefly between sleep cycles, many times a night. A baby who falls asleep being fed, rocked or held expects the same help at each of those wakings. The AAP’s parent site makes the same point: babies who are held or rocked to sleep may have trouble falling back asleep when they wake in the night. From about 4 months, it advises putting babies to bed drowsy rather than waiting until they are asleep. Sleep training is the work of moving that last step, falling asleep, from you to your baby.

That sounds simple. In practice the sticking points are specific to each baby: the right bedtime for this baby, which night feeds stay, how this baby reacts when you are in the room, and what to change when night 3 looks nothing like night 1.

The stages, start to finish

Every successful plan moves through the same stages. What happens inside each one is where babies differ.

  • Readiness. Age, health, growth, night-feed needs and a safe sleep space. Your pediatrician has the final word on health and feeds.
  • Foundations. Schedule, routine and room. A baby who is badly overtired or not tired enough will protest any approach, which is why a plan starts here and not with the response at the crib.
  • The response. How you respond at bedtime and at night, chosen to fit your baby’s temperament and your limits, and kept the same by every adult.
  • The first nights. Protest is normal while a baby learns something new. Knowing which changes are progress and which mean the plan needs adjusting is the hardest part to judge alone at 2 a.m.
  • Naps. Daytime sleep usually lags behind nights and needs its own attention.

Why generic plans stall

  • Age-band templates. Two 6-month-olds can need different bedtimes, different feeds and different responses.
  • Mixing methods from several sources, or switching after a hard night.
  • Inconsistency at 2 a.m. Holding steady at bedtime and giving in later teaches a baby to protest longer.
  • Bedtime only. A plan that changes bedtime but leaves night wakings and naps unplanned keeps the old habit alive.
  • No one to adjust it. A written method cannot tell you which detail to change for your baby on night 4.

What the research says

A review of 52 treatment studies for the American Academy of Sleep Medicine found that 94% reported behavioral interventions for bedtime problems and night wakings were effective, and over 80% of children treated showed clinically significant improvement, maintained for 3 to 6 months. The methods are well supported. The method names compared side by side are in methods compared, and the full evidence is in what the research says.

Safety comes first, every night

Whatever plan you follow, your baby sleeps on the back, alone, on a firm, flat, non-inclined surface without pillows, blankets, soft toys or bedding, as the AAP advises. Call your pediatrician before any sleep plan if your baby snores or gasps in sleep, seems to be in pain, is feeding poorly or is not gaining weight.

Frequently asked

What is the easiest sleep training method?

The easiest one is the one that fits your baby and that every adult can repeat the same way each night. That is rarely a named method off the shelf. It is a plan written around your baby’s temperament and your limits, and adjusted as your baby responds.

How long does it take to sleep train a baby?

It depends on the baby, the plan and consistency, and naps usually take longer than nights. At Actually Sleeping the outcome is guaranteed: your baby will sleep 8–12 hours through the night within 14 days of consistent, correct implementation of your sleep plan, or you get a full refund.

Do I have to stop night feeds to sleep train?

No. Sleep training teaches falling asleep independently; night weaning is a separate decision. Keep the feeds your pediatrician says your baby needs. An Actually Sleeping plan never forces dropping night feeds.

Can I sleep train without letting my baby cry?

No approach can promise zero protest, because any change to how a baby falls asleep is frustrating at first. What you can control is how you respond, and thorough coaching before the first night makes it much easier to respond calmly and consistently.
