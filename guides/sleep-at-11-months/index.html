Sleep at 11 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to talk to your pediatrician

At 11 months, the AASM range is 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps. Most babies still take 2 naps: a large review found the big shift to one nap happens between 12 and 18 months. Nap refusal at this age is usually a phase. Keep the crib empty and your baby on the back to start until the first birthday.

What is changing at 11 months

Your baby is close to the CDC’s 1-year milestones: pulling up to stand, walking while holding furniture, waving bye-bye, understanding “no”, and playing games like pat-a-cake. That is a lot of new ability and a lot of new reasons to stay awake.

Separation anxiety is often near its peak; the AAP places the usual peak between 10 and 18 months.

Sleep need does not fall off a cliff at the birthday. A review of studies found babies from 9 months averaged 12.6 hours per 24 hours and babies from 12 months averaged 12.9 hours, so expect much the same total sleep for the next few months.

What parents find hardest

  • Nap strikes. Keep offering the second nap. Babies who drop to one nap too early often start waking at night or before 6 a.m.
  • Bedtime battles. More awareness, more protest. Shorter routines usually beat longer ones.
  • Illness. Each cold can bring a week of rough nights. Comfort your baby fully while sick, then return to the usual routine once they are well.
  • Dreading the first birthday change in daycare rooms, which often move to one nap at a set age.

Safe sleep at 11 months

  • The AAP’s safe sleep recommendations cover babies up to 1 year. Keep the crib empty and start every sleep on the back until then.
  • No bottle in bed: the AAP says milk, formula or juice in bed can cause tooth decay.
  • Mattress at the lowest setting. Check the rail height against your baby as they grow.
  • Keep the crib away from windows and blind cords, and anchor nearby furniture.

Night feeds

Ask your pediatrician at the 12-month check about night feeds and the move to whole milk; both are their call. Changes to night feeds should follow that advice, not a birthday.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Snoring most nights, pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness that seems excessive.
  • Night waking with fever, ear pulling, or crying that suggests pain.
  • Your baby is not yet pulling to stand, babbling, or using gestures like waving as 12 months approaches.
  • Night terrors or screaming episodes that keep happening.

Frequently asked

Is my 11 month old ready for one nap?

Probably not yet. A systematic review of napping studies found the big shift away from two naps a day happens between 12 and 18 months. A few days of nap refusal at 11 months is usually a phase. Keep offering two naps.

Can my 11 month old have a blanket in the crib?

The AAP’s safe sleep recommendations apply until the first birthday and call for a crib with no blankets, pillows, bumpers or soft toys. Use a wearable blanket or sleep sack instead. Ask your pediatrician about changes after age 1.

How much sleep does an 11 month old need?

The AASM recommends 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps, for babies 4 to 12 months. A review of studies found babies from 9 months averaged about 12.6 hours, with healthy babies on both sides of that.
