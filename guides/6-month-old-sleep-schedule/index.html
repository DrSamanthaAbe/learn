What is a good sleep schedule for a 6 month old?

At 6 months most babies take 3 naps, with some starting the move to 2. Wake windows of 2–3 hours are a common rule of thumb, the AASM recommends 12–16 hours of sleep in 24, and bedtime usually falls between 7 and 8 p.m. with 11–12 hours overnight.

Sample day for a 6 month old (3 naps)

Treat every time below as a starting point. Shift the whole day to match your baby’s actual wake-up time, then adjust one thing at a time in 15-minute steps and hold it for three days before judging.

Breast milk or formula remains the main source of nutrition; solids at this age are practice and are not a sleep tool. The AAP suggests starting solids at about 6 months. In one large UK trial, babies started on solids from 3 months slept about 17 minutes longer a night at 6 months: a small difference, and not a reason to start early without your pediatrician’s advice.

What is changing developmentally

Sitting, rolling freely and reaching for everything. Many babies start solids around 6 months. The third nap begins to be resisted on some days, the first sign of the 3-to-2 transition that, in practice, many babies complete between 6 and 8 months. By about 6 months the longest stretch of sleep typically lengthens to around 6 hours, which is why continued waking every 2 hours at this age is often about how the baby falls asleep rather than hunger.

Common problems at this age

  • Waking every 2 hours: see why a 6 month old wakes every 2 hours.
  • Third nap refused: if it is refused for 10–14 days, start the transition. Until then, use an early bedtime on refusal days. See nap transitions.
  • Feeding to sleep at every waking: see the feed-to-sleep association.
  • 5 a.m. starts: see early morning waking.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Yes. Formal sleep training is generally appropriate from about 4 months, and 6 months is one of the most common ages to start. Ask your pediatrician whether your baby still needs a night feed; a plan can keep one feed and still teach independent sleep. Frequently asked

Should a 6 month old have 2 or 3 naps?

Most still need 3. Move to 2 when the third nap has been refused, or has pushed bedtime past 8:30 p.m., for about two weeks.

Do solids help a 6 month old sleep through the night?

Not in any way that matters much. In one large trial, earlier solids were linked to about 17 more minutes of sleep a night at 6 months. Milk remains the main nutrition. Follow your pediatrician on when and how to begin.

How long should a 6 month old sleep at night?

About 11–12 hours in the crib. Whether that includes a feed is individual and a question for your pediatrician.

What time is bedtime for a 6 month old?

Between 7 and 8 p.m., roughly 2.5–3 hours after the last nap ends.
