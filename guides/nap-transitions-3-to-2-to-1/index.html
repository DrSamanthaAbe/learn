When do babies drop naps, and how do I handle the 3-to-2 and 2-to-1 transitions?

As a rule of thumb from practice, many babies go from 3 naps to 2 at around 6 to 8 months, and from 2 naps to 1 at around 14 to 18 months. The sign is a pattern, not a bad day: the last nap is refused or pushes bedtime late for two weeks or more. Transitions go best gradually and timed to your child.

What a nap transition is

As children get older, they can stay awake longer and need less daytime sleep. At some point the day no longer fits the number of naps it used to hold, and one nap has to go. The weeks in between are the transition. They are uneven by nature: some days the child needs the old number of naps and some days the new one. Because total sleep across 24 hours still matters, a nap lost during the day has to be made up somewhere, usually with an earlier bedtime, or it shows up as a harder night.

Signs a transition is real (versus a bad week)

  • The last nap is consistently refused or takes a long time to happen, for a couple of weeks.
  • Naps are fine but bedtime is being pushed late to fit them.
  • Early morning waking appears with no other change.
  • One bad day, an illness or travel is not a transition.

Why transitions go wrong

  • Too early. A few refused naps around the first birthday look like readiness for one nap. Often they are a passing regression. Dropping a nap too soon tends to show up as weeks of night waking and early rising.
  • Too abrupt. A child who goes from two naps to one overnight is awake for a long stretch they are not ready for, and the overtiredness spills into the night.
  • Without a cushion. Days during a transition are uneven. Without a bedtime that moves to match, a short-nap day becomes a bad night.
  • One plan for every child. Some children move through a transition in days, others take weeks. A generic schedule cannot tell you which you have.

Frequently asked

My 12 month old refuses the second nap. Is it time for one nap?

Often not yet. As a rule of thumb from practice, most toddlers still need two naps until somewhere between 14 and 18 months, and a run of refusals around the first birthday is often a passing regression. A plan with daily check-ins can tell the difference.

How long should the single nap be?

It varies by child. What matters is that total sleep stays in the AASM range of 11–14 hours per 24 hours at 1–2 years, naps included. Actually Sleeping coaches naps until they are solid, including the single nap.

When do toddlers stop napping entirely?

Later than many parents expect, and it varies a lot between children. A young toddler who refuses naps for a few days is more likely in a transition or a rough patch than finished with naps. Actually Sleeping coaches naps until they are solid, including through that stage.
