Why is my 8, 9 or 10 month old suddenly waking at night?

The 8–10 month period stacks three things at once: new gross motor skills (crawling, pulling to stand) that get practiced in the crib, the peak of separation anxiety, and the 3-to-2 nap transition. Keep the response to night waking exactly what it was, practice the new skills in daylight, protect two solid naps, and expect 2–3 weeks. A previously independent sleeper usually returns to baseline in a few nights.

What is happening

  • Motor leaps. A baby who just learned to stand will stand up in the crib at 2 a.m. and may not know how to get down. Practice sitting down from standing during the day, many times. At night, lay them down once and leave; repeating it becomes a game.
  • Separation anxiety. Object permanence has arrived: the baby now knows you exist when you leave, and protests. A predictable routine and a brief, confident goodbye reassure more than lingering does.
  • Nap transition. The third nap is disappearing, and a day with too little daytime sleep produces a worse night. Move bedtime earlier on short-nap days.
  • Teething and illness ride along but rarely explain weeks of waking.

What to do

  1. Do not introduce anything new at night: no return of night feeds, rocking to sleep or bed-sharing unless you plan to keep them.
  2. Hold the nap schedule; two naps totaling 2.5–3.5 hours; bedtime 6:30–7:30 p.m.
  3. Daytime practice for every new skill, especially getting down from standing.
  4. If the baby never slept independently, this is a fine age to start; separation anxiety is not a reason to wait, and the plan accounts for it.

Frequently asked

How long does the 8 month sleep regression last?

Typically 2–3 weeks with a consistent response; longer if new habits are introduced during it.

Should I feed my 9 month old at night during the regression?

If your baby was not feeding at night before, do not restart. Offer more during the day.

My baby stands in the crib and cries. What do I do?

Lay them down once, calmly, and leave. Then practice sitting down from standing during the day until they can do it alone.
