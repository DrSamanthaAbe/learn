Why is my baby’s sleep worse since they started crawling or walking?

New motor skills can disturb sleep for a while. One study found crawling babies had more bedtime difficulty and night waking than babies not yet crawling, and another linked early pulling-to-stand with a period of disrupted sleep. Give lots of daytime practice, make the crib safe for a mobile baby, and keep your night response the same so a phase doesn’t become a habit.

What the research shows

A 2005 study of 107 healthy babies aged about 5 to 8 months found that babies who were already crawling had more bedtime difficulties and more night waking than babies who were not yet crawling. Among 7- and 8-month-olds, crawling predicted part of the difference in night waking even after accounting for age.

A later study followed 20 babies from 7 to about 12 months with home sleep recordings. In babies who pulled to stand early, by 8 months, the milestone was linked in time with a period of disrupted sleep.

Both studies are small, and neither proves the skill causes the waking. But they match what many parents see: a baby who crawls in the crib, sits up half asleep, or stands at 2 a.m. and can’t get back down.

Why new skills disturb sleep

  • Practice. Babies rehearse new movements, including at bedtime and between sleep cycles.
  • Getting stuck. Many babies learn to sit, crawl or stand before they learn to undo it.
  • Excitement and energy. A new way to move is thrilling, and a baby who spent the day on the move may also be more overtired by bedtime.

What helps

  1. Lots of daytime floor time. The more practice during the day, the less practice at night.
  2. Teach the way back down. Help your baby go from sitting to lying, and from standing to sitting. See when your baby stands in the crib.
  3. Put them down on their back as usual. If your baby rolls or moves into another position on their own and can move back out of it, you don’t need to reposition them.
  4. Keep your response predictable. Go in, help briefly if they are stuck, and leave. Avoid new habits like rocking to sleep, which can outlast the milestone.
  5. Check the schedule. A mobile baby may need a slightly different wake window, and an overtired baby wakes more. See wake windows by age.

Make the crib safe for a mobile baby

  • MedlinePlus advises lowering the mattress before your baby can sit up alone, and to its lowest level before they can stand.
  • Remove mobiles and hanging toys once your baby pushes up on hands and knees.
  • Keep the crib bare: a fitted sheet only, nothing to climb on.
  • Check that nothing within reach of the crib, such as cords or curtains, can be pulled in.

Walking

First steps usually come after pulling to stand and walking along furniture, which the CDC lists among milestones by 1 year. The same approach applies: daytime practice, a safe crib, and a calm, predictable response at night. Many babies around this age are also moving toward one nap, which can unsettle nights for other reasons. See nap transitions.

When it is not the milestone

If the waking started with the new skill but is still going a month later, look elsewhere. The usual reason is that your baby needs help to fall asleep at bedtime, and needs the same help at every waking. See the 8 to 10 month regression and why a 12 month old starts waking. If your baby seems unwell, in pain, or is not reaching milestones, talk to your pediatrician.

Frequently asked

How long does crawling affect sleep?

Research has not pinned down a typical length. In practice, plenty of daytime practice shortens it. If waking continues for weeks after the skill is solid, the cause is usually a sleep habit rather than the milestone.

My baby crawls around the crib instead of sleeping. Should I go in?

Not straight away if they are safe and content. Many babies settle after a few minutes of practice. If they are stuck or upset, help briefly, lay them down on their back, and leave.

Should I start sleep training during a milestone?

Many families wait until a big new skill is fairly solid, then start. If you are already sleep training, keep going and respond the same way you planned; consistency helps a baby through the change.
