Why is my baby’s sleep worse since they started crawling or walking?

New motor skills can disturb sleep for a while. What the research shows

A 2005 study of 107 healthy babies aged about 5 to 8 months found that babies who were already crawling had more bedtime difficulties and more night waking than babies who were not yet crawling. Among 7- and 8-month-olds, crawling predicted part of the difference in night waking even after accounting for age.

A later study followed 20 babies from 7 to about 12 months with home sleep recordings. In babies who pulled to stand early, by 8 months, the milestone was linked in time with a period of disrupted sleep.

Both studies are small, and neither proves the skill causes the waking. But they match what many parents see: a baby who crawls in the crib, sits up half asleep, or stands at 2 a.m. and can’t get back down.

Why new skills disturb sleep

  • Practice. Babies rehearse new movements, including at bedtime and between sleep cycles.
  • Getting stuck. Many babies learn to sit, crawl or stand before they learn to undo it.
  • Excitement and energy. A new way to move is thrilling, and a baby who spent the day on the move may also be more overtired by bedtime.

New skills come and go. If each one leaves weeks of broken nights behind, that pattern is fixable, and it is what Actually Sleeping works on.

Why the waking outlasts the skill

A milestone is temporary. What parents do to get through it often is not. Rocking, feeding or holding a baby back to sleep after every crawl or stand becomes the way that baby falls back asleep, and the waking continues long after the skill is solid. That is why some families find nights fall apart at every new skill, while others barely notice: the difference is usually whether the baby can fall asleep on their own.

Make the crib safe for a mobile baby

  • MedlinePlus advises lowering the mattress before your baby can sit up alone, and to its lowest level before they can stand.
  • Remove mobiles and hanging toys once your baby pushes up on hands and knees.
  • Keep the crib bare: a fitted sheet only, nothing to climb on.
  • Check that nothing within reach of the crib, such as cords or curtains, can be pulled in.
  • Put your baby down on their back as usual. If they move into another position on their own and can move back out of it, you don’t need to reposition them.

Walking

First steps usually come after pulling to stand and walking along furniture, which the CDC lists among milestones by 1 year. Many babies around this age are also moving toward one nap, which can unsettle nights for other reasons. See nap transitions.

When it is not the milestone

If the waking started with the new skill but is still going a month later, look elsewhere. The usual reason is that your baby needs help to fall asleep at bedtime, and needs the same help at every waking. See the 8 to 10 month regression and why a 12 month old starts waking. If your baby seems unwell, in pain, or is not reaching milestones, talk to your pediatrician.

Frequently asked

How long does crawling affect sleep?

Research has not pinned down a typical length. If waking continues for weeks after the skill is solid, the cause is usually a sleep habit that started during the phase rather than the milestone itself.

My baby crawls around the crib instead of sleeping. Should I go in?

If they are safe and content, many babies settle after a few minutes. If they are stuck or upset, help briefly and lay them down on their back. When this happens at every waking, it usually means your baby needs help to fall back asleep.

Should I start sleep training during a milestone?
