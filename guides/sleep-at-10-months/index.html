Sleep at 10 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 10 months, the AASM range is 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps. Pulling up and cruising are the big sleep disruptors, and the AAP says separation anxiety usually peaks between 10 and 18 months. Most babies still take 2 naps as a common pattern. The mattress must be at its lowest setting before your baby can stand.

What is changing at 10 months

By 12 months, the CDC lists pulling up to stand and walking while holding on to furniture. Many 10 month olds are already doing both, and the crib rail is the perfect thing to pull on. The AAP describes the window from 10 to 18 months as the usual peak of separation anxiety.

Language is coming too. By 12 months, the CDC lists understanding “no” and calling a parent “mama” or “dada”. Babies who can call you, will.

What is normal

In a review of studies, babies 7 to 11 months averaged 1.1 night wakings, with anything from 0 to about 3 within the normal range, and babies 6 to 11 months averaged about 2 naps a day. A temporary dip during a big motor leap is common. Total sleep holds steady: babies from 9 months averaged 12.6 hours per 24 hours in the same review.

What parents find hardest

  • Bedtime standing protests that stretch settling to an hour.
  • Short second naps or nap refusal. This is usually a phase. Most babies are not ready for one nap at 10 months.
  • Early waking as daylight and noise wake a lighter sleeper.
  • Illness. Colds and ear infections often start around daycare age and can reset sleep habits.

Safe sleep at 10 months

  • Lowest mattress position, crib away from windows and blind cords.
  • Still nothing in the crib: the AAP’s recommendations apply until the first birthday.
  • Place on the back to start. If your baby rolls or sits, there is no need to reposition.
  • Furniture near the crib should be anchored so it cannot tip.

Night feeds

If your pediatrician wants a night feed kept, keep it. If they say it is no longer needed, a gradual change is easier on everyone. Ask before you decide either way.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Snoring most nights, gasping, or restless sleep with heavy breathing.
  • Sudden waking with crying that suggests pain, especially with fever.
  • Your baby is not yet pulling up, or not babbling, as the first birthday approaches.
  • Recurring screaming episodes early in the night where your baby does not seem awake.

Where to go from here

Standing protests usually fade in one to two weeks with a consistent response, which is a practical observation rather than a study result. If yours have not, Actually Sleeping can help you set up a plan. Parents talk about how that went on our reviews page. Related: the 10-month schedule, the 8 to 10 month regression, and early waking.

Frequently asked

My 10 month old stands in the crib and will not lie down. What do I do?

Practise getting down from standing during the day, lower the mattress fully, and use one calm response at night. Repeatedly laying your baby down can become a game. Most babies stop once getting down becomes easy.

Should my 10 month old drop to one nap?

Usually not yet. A review of napping studies found the big drop in two-nap days happens at 12 to 18 months. Babies 6 to 11 months averaged about 2 naps a day. A few days of nap refusal is usually a phase.

Is separation anxiety worse at 10 months?

It can be. The AAP says separation anxiety usually peaks between 10 and 18 months and that it can disrupt sleep for several months. A predictable bedtime routine helps children accept the separation.
