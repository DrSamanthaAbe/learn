My baby stands up in the crib and can’t get back down. What do I do?

Lower the mattress to its lowest setting before your baby can stand, and practise sitting down from standing during the day. At night, help them down calmly once or twice, then give them a chance to work it out. Pulling up to stand is a milestone the CDC lists by the first birthday, and early standers can have a short period of disrupted sleep.

Safety first: the mattress

MedlinePlus advises lowering the crib mattress before your baby can sit up on their own, and having it at its lowest level before your baby can stand. If your baby is already pulling up and the mattress is still high, lower it today.

Other crib checks at this stage:

  • Remove hanging crib toys and mobiles. MedlinePlus says to take them down when your baby first pushes up on hands and knees, because they can strangle a baby.
  • Keep the crib bare. Anything inside can become a step.
  • Move the crib away from windows, blind cords, curtains and furniture your baby could reach.
  • MedlinePlus says children should stop using a crib by the time they are 35 inches (89 cm) tall.

Why it happens

The CDC lists “pulls up to stand” among the milestones most babies reach by 1 year. A small study that tracked babies with home sleep recordings found that, in babies who pulled to stand early, by 8 months, the new skill was linked in time with a period of disrupted sleep. Pulling up is easier to learn than getting down. So your baby stands, gets tired, and calls for help.

Teach the way down during the day

This usually helps most. Several times a day:

  1. Stand your baby at the sofa or the crib rail, holding on.
  2. Put a toy on the floor.
  3. Gently bend their knees and help them lower to sitting.
  4. Repeat until they start doing it without help.

Practise in the crib too, during calm awake time, so the motion is familiar there. Make it a game: hold a toy low, cheer when they sit, and keep it short and fun.

What to do at night

  • Put them to bed lying down as usual. Don’t wait for them to sit before you leave.
  • Help them down once or twice, quietly, without picking them up. Lay them down and leave.
  • Then give them space. If you lay them down twenty times, standing becomes a game. Many babies sit or lie down on their own once they are tired enough and no one is playing.
  • A sleep sack can slow standing a little and is a good layer anyway.
  • Check the schedule. A baby who isn’t tired at bedtime has energy to practise. See wake windows by age.

Babies who fall asleep standing or sitting

Some babies doze off sitting up or slumped at the rail. Once they are asleep, lay them down on their back. You don’t need to fight the position while they are still awake.

Is it time for a bed

Standing is not a reason to leave the crib. Climbing out is a different matter. If your toddler is climbing out or has reached 35 inches, see when to move from a crib to a bed.

If standing has turned into weeks of night waking, the cause is often how your baby falls asleep at bedtime rather than the skill itself. See the 8 to 10 month regression.

Frequently asked

Should I keep laying my baby down every time they stand?

Once or twice, yes. After that, repeated laying down tends to become a game. Practise sitting down during the day, put them to bed lying down, and give them a chance to settle themselves.

When should I lower the crib mattress?

MedlinePlus advises lowering it before your baby can sit up on their own, and having it at the lowest level before they can stand. If your baby is already pulling up, lower it now.

How long does the standing phase last?

It varies. Daytime practice of getting down is what shortens it. If night waking continues for weeks, look at bedtime habits and the schedule, which often keep the waking going after the skill is learned.
