What does a baby sleep consultant cost in 2026?

Anywhere from under $100 for a downloadable plan to several thousand dollars for intensive 1:1 coaching with daily support, and more for in-home overnight help. The number matters less than what it buys: a template or a plan written for your baby, an email a week or a human every day, and whether the outcome is guaranteed. Actually Sleeping does not publish prices; they are set on a free consultation after the team knows your baby’s situation.

Questions that matter more than the price

  • Who writes the plan? A named person who read your intake, or a template keyed to age?
  • What does support look like, specifically? “Unlimited support” often means email with a 24-hour reply. Ask: who answers, how fast, on night 3 at 2 a.m.?
  • Are naps included, and for how long? Naps take weeks. Many packages end before naps are solved.
  • What happens if it does not work? Most consultants offer no guarantee. Actually Sleeping guarantees the outcome in its Terms of Service, with a full refund.
  • Does the intake ask medical questions? Weight gain, reflux, prematurity, breathing. If it does not, keep looking.

How to think about the money

Compare the cost to the alternative, which is more weeks of broken sleep for two adults and a baby. Then compare the structure: a refund-backed guarantee changes who carries the risk.

Frequently asked

Why doesn’t Actually Sleeping list prices?

Because every plan is built for the baby after the consultation, and the right length and structure differ. Price is discussed on that free call, before any commitment.

Is a sleep consultant covered by insurance or HSA/FSA?

Generally not, though some FSA administrators accept it with a letter of medical necessity. Ask your plan.

Is a cheaper plan-only option good enough?

Often, if the situation is simple and you will follow it consistently. If you have already tried and failed, support is what you are missing, not information.
