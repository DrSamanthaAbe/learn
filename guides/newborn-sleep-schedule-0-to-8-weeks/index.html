What is a realistic newborn sleep schedule (0–8 weeks)?

There is no clock schedule for a newborn, only a rhythm: feed on cue, stay awake about 45–60 minutes (60–90 minutes by 6–8 weeks), sleep, repeat. Expect 5 or more naps, sleep spread across day and night in 2–4 hour stretches, a total that varies widely around 14–17 hours, and a late “bedtime” of roughly 9–11 p.m.

A sample day at about 6 weeks

This is an illustration of the rhythm, not a timetable to enforce. Feeding on cue always comes first, and a younger newborn will be awake for less time than shown.

What is changing developmentally

A newborn has no body clock. The day–night rhythm and the baby’s own melatonin production develop over the first 3 to 4 months, which is why sleep is scattered evenly across 24 hours and why day–night confusion is normal. About half of newborn sleep is light, active sleep, so grunting, twitching and brief cries during sleep do not always mean the baby is awake. Fussiness typically peaks around 6 weeks and then eases.

The AASM consensus on sleep duration deliberately gives no recommendation under 4 months, because the normal range is so wide. See how infant sleep cycles develop for what comes next.

Common problems at this age

  • Day–night confusion: bright, busy days and dark, boring nights. Do not let a single daytime nap run much past 2–3 hours without offering a feed.
  • Only sleeps when held: expected in these weeks. Hold your baby while you are fully awake, and try one put-down a day in the bassinet for practice. Later, contact napping can be phased out.
  • Overtired by evening: watch the clock as well as the baby; see wake windows by age.
  • Safe sleep: on the back, on a flat, firm surface, in an empty bassinet or crib, in your room. Stop swaddling at the first signs of rolling.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Not with any method that drops feeds or leaves a newborn to cry; those are not for this age. What can start now is the foundation: feeding well, safe sleep and a simple, repeatable wind-down. Gentle shaping can begin around 10–12 weeks and formal sleep training generally from about 4 months; Actually Sleeping works with families from the newborn stage (foundations first, night feeds stay in place, and the 14-day guarantee clock starts at 10 weeks). Frequently asked

Should I wake my newborn to feed?

Follow your pediatrician’s guidance. Many newborns are woken to feed every 2–3 hours until they are back to birth weight and gaining well; after that, most pediatricians allow longer night stretches.

When do newborns start sleeping longer at night?

Many babies produce one longer stretch of 4–6 hours somewhere between 6 and 12 weeks, usually the first stretch of the night. The range is wide and depends on feeding and growth.

What time should a newborn go to bed?

Late is normal: roughly 9 to 11 p.m. in the first 8 weeks. Bedtime drifts earlier on its own as the body clock develops, typically toward 7–8 p.m. by 3–4 months.

Can a newborn be on a schedule?

Not a clock schedule. A repeating pattern of feed, short awake time and sleep is realistic. Fixed feeding or nap times are not, and feeds should never be delayed to fit a timetable.
