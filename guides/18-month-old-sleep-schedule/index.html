What is a good sleep schedule for an 18 month old?

Between 15 and 23 months most toddlers settle on one midday nap of 1.5–3 hours. As a rule of thumb, wake windows are about 4.5–5.5 hours just after the switch and 5–6 hours from 18 months. The AASM recommends 11–14 hours of sleep in 24, and bedtime usually falls between 7 and 8 p.m.

What is changing developmentally

The 2-to-1 nap transition, which a large review places mostly between 12 and 18 months. A language burst, and with it the word “no.” A strong drive for independence alongside separation anxiety, which the AAP says usually peaks between 10 and 18 months and fades during the last half of the second year. First molars (about 13–19 months) and canines (about 16–23 months) come through in this window.

Why the average toddler schedule often does not fit

  • A messy nap transition: some days need two naps and some one. See nap transitions.
  • Bedtime battles and night waking around 18 months: see the 18-month sleep regression.
  • Short single nap: see short naps.
  • Early waking: see early morning waking.

Toddlers test whether rules hold. A schedule without a consistent response behind it rarely survives that test.

Safe sleep at this age

  • Keep the mattress at its lowest setting. The AAP says it may be time for a bed once your toddler can climb out, and to put the crib mattress on the floor until a bed is ready.
  • Move out of the crib at 35 inches (89 cm) tall (AAP, MedlinePlus).

Frequently asked

When should my toddler drop to one nap?

A large review found two-nap days drop sharply between 12 and 18 months, and fewer than 2.5% of children stop napping altogether before age 2. The right moment for your toddler depends on how they cope, which Dr. Sam judges with you.

How long should an 18 month old nap?

In studies, naps among 1 to 2 year olds averaged about 2.3 hours. A common practice range is 1.5–3 hours in a single nap. How the nap fits your toddler’s bedtime is set in their plan.

What time should an 18 month old go to bed?

As a common rule of thumb, between 7 and 8 p.m., and earlier during the nap transition. The right time depends on your toddler’s nap and how bedtime goes, so a Actually Sleeping plan sets it for them.

How long does the 18-month sleep regression last?

There is no research-defined length. Toddlers test limits, and inconsistent responses tend to stretch it out. Helping every caregiver respond the same way is the core of a Actually Sleeping plan.
