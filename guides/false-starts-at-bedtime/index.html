Why does my baby wake 30–45 minutes after bedtime?

A waking 30–45 minutes after bedtime is called a false start: your baby reached the end of the first sleep cycle and could not move into the next. The usual causes are falling asleep with help (feeding, rocking, a replaced pacifier), a last wake window that is too long or too short, or a bedtime early enough that the body treats it as a nap. Fix how your baby falls asleep first, then adjust the last wake window by 15 minutes at a time.

Why it happens at exactly that time

A baby’s sleep cycle is typically about 50 minutes, compared with about 90 minutes in adults. At the end of the first one, every baby surfaces briefly. If conditions match what they were at sleep onset and sleep pressure is high, the baby slides into deep sleep. If something has changed, or the drive to sleep is weak, they wake fully. What to do in the moment

  1. Pause. Give your baby a few minutes. Some false starts end on their own.
  2. Respond the way you do at bedtime. If your baby normally falls asleep on their own, use the same brief reassurance you would use then.
  3. Avoid adding a new feed if your baby ate well at bedtime. A top-up 40 minutes later becomes an expected second course. For a young baby who is cluster feeding in the evening, feed; that is normal in the early months.
  4. Do not get them up. Lights on and playtime tell the body that was a nap.

How to test the schedule

Change one variable, by 15 minutes, and hold it for three nights before judging. Starting points are in wake windows by age. As a rule, the last window of the day is the longest, and total daytime sleep should fit your baby’s age.

When false starts mean something else

Under about 3–4 months, evening waking is common and developmental; nothing needs fixing. During a nap transition, expect a week or two of false starts while the day reshuffles. And if your baby also snores, struggles to breathe or seems in pain lying down, that is a pediatrician question, not a schedule one.

False starts are among the first things to disappear in a Actually Sleeping plan, because the first skill taught is falling asleep independently at bedtime.

Frequently asked

Are false starts a sign of overtiredness or undertiredness?

Either. A baby who falls asleep instantly and wakes crying is usually overtired; one who takes a long time to fall asleep or wakes cheerful is usually undertired.

How long do false starts last?

With the right fix, usually less than a week. If they continue beyond two weeks, the cause is almost always how the baby falls asleep.

Should I feed my baby after a false start?

If your baby had a full feed at bedtime, generally no. A young baby who cluster feeds in the evening is a different case; feed them.
