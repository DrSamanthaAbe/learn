Why does my baby wake 30–45 minutes after bedtime?

A waking 30–45 minutes after bedtime is called a false start: your baby reached the end of the first sleep cycle and could not move into the next. The usual causes are falling asleep with help (feeding, rocking, a replaced pacifier), a last wake window that is too long or too short, or a bedtime early enough that the body treats it as a nap.

Why it happens at exactly that time

A baby’s sleep cycle is typically about 50 minutes, compared with about 90 minutes in adults. At the end of the first one, every baby surfaces briefly. If conditions match what they were at sleep onset and sleep pressure is high, the baby slides back into deep sleep. If something has changed, or the drive to sleep is weak, they wake fully. False starts are one of the most fixable patterns in baby sleep, and one of the most frustrating to troubleshoot, because several causes look alike. Actually Sleeping works out which ones apply to your baby and writes the plan around them.

Why guessing rarely fixes it

These causes overlap. An overtired baby who is also fed to sleep shows both sets of clues, and fixing one leaves the other. It is common to try moving bedtime, shortening a nap and adding a top-up feed all in the same week, and then be unable to tell which change did what. Most families who come to us have already tried things on their own. A schedule tweak also cannot fix a sleep association: if your baby needs a feed or a cuddle to fall asleep, they will ask for it again at the end of the first cycle whatever time bedtime is.

That is why false starts need a plan built for the individual baby: their age, feeding, temperament, how they currently fall asleep, and when your family’s evening actually happens.

When false starts mean something else

Under about 3–4 months, evening waking is common and developmental; at that age Actually Sleeping works on foundations rather than expecting long stretches. During a nap transition, the day reshuffles and false starts can come and go. Frequently asked

Are false starts a sign of overtiredness or undertiredness?

Either. A baby who falls asleep instantly and wakes crying is usually overtired; one who takes a long time to fall asleep or wakes cheerful is usually undertired. Many babies also have a sleep association on top.

How long do false starts last?

False starts linked to a sleep association usually continue until the way your baby falls asleep changes. In Actually Sleeping plans they are usually among the first things to go, because falling asleep independently at bedtime is taught first.

Should I feed my baby after a false start?

If your baby had a full feed at bedtime, often not; a top-up soon after bed can become an expected second course. A young baby who cluster feeds in the evening is a different case. Night feeds are your pediatrician’s call.
