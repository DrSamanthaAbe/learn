Sleep at 7 months: what’s normal, what’s hard, and when to get help

At 7 months, the AASM range is still 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps. Night waking often creeps back: in studies, babies 7 to 11 months woke slightly more on average than babies 3 to 6 months. Sitting, early crawling and separation anxiety are common reasons. Many babies move from 3 naps to 2 around now, as a common pattern rather than a rule.

What is changing at 7 months

Your baby is working toward the CDC’s 9-month milestones: getting into a sitting position alone, sitting without support, and moving things from hand to hand. New motor skills tend to get practised in the crib.

The emotional side is shifting too. The AAP says separation anxiety can begin in the second half of the first year and can disrupt sleep for several months, with babies waking and crying anxiously for a parent.

A first tooth may also arrive. The AAP says teething typically starts by about 4 to 7 months and can cause a slight temperature rise, but not above 101°F (38.3°C). A higher fever is not teething.

What parents find hardest

  • New night wakings. Often tied to new skills or separation anxiety. Keep your response calm and consistent so a two-week phase does not become a habit.
  • Sitting or rolling and getting stuck. Give plenty of floor time in the day to practise getting down.
  • Fighting the third nap. In studies, babies 6 to 11 months averaged about 2 naps a day. Many drop the late catnap around now; bedtime often needs to move earlier while that happens.
  • Early waking. Light leaks and a bedtime that is too late are frequent causes.

Safe sleep at 7 months

  • Back to sleep until the first birthday; if your baby rolls both ways, let them settle where they land, in an empty crib.
  • The mattress should already be lowered for sitting. Move it to the lowest setting before your baby can pull to stand.
  • Keep the crib away from windows, blind cords and anything climbable.
  • No pillows, blankets or soft toys yet. The AAP’s safe sleep recommendations run to age 1.

Night feeds

A 7 month old waking more is not automatically hungry, and is not automatically not hungry. Ask your pediatrician whether night feeds are still needed before you change anything.

When to talk to the pediatrician

  • Waking that comes with fever, ear pulling, vomiting, rash, or a cough that worsens lying down.
  • Snoring most nights or breathing that looks like hard work.
  • A fever above 101°F (38.3°C) blamed on teething: the AAP says teething does not cause that.
  • Your baby is not yet rolling or bearing weight on the hands while sitting.

Frequently asked

Why did my 7 month old start waking at night again?

Common causes are new motor skills, separation anxiety, illness and a change in naps. Studies found slightly more night waking on average at 7 to 11 months than at 3 to 6 months. If there is fever or pain, see the pediatrician; otherwise, keep your response consistent.

Should my 7 month old be on 2 naps?

Many are, but it is a common pattern rather than a rule. Babies 6 to 11 months averaged 2.2 daytime naps in a review of studies. If the third nap makes bedtime late or is refused most days, it may be time to drop it.

How much should a 7 month old sleep?

The AASM recommends 12 to 16 hours per 24 hours, including naps, for babies 4 to 12 months, and the AAP supports this. Studies found an average of about 12.9 hours for babies from 6 months.
