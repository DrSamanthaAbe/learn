What should a bedtime routine include, and when should I start one?

Keep it short, calm and the same every night: for example a feed, bath or wash, pajamas, teeth, a book, a song, then into the crib awake. A study of more than 10,000 young children found that the more nights a week a routine happened, the better children slept, with earlier bedtimes and fewer night wakings. You can start one in the first months.

What the research found

A 2015 study in the journal Sleep asked mothers of 10,085 children from birth to 5 years, across 13 countries and regions, how often their child had a bedtime routine. The more nights a week a routine happened, the better the sleep. Children with a consistent routine had:

  • Earlier bedtimes
  • Less time taken to fall asleep
  • Fewer and shorter night wakings
  • More total night sleep

The authors concluded that the earlier a routine starts, the better. The study was based on parent reports and shows an association, not proof of cause, but the pattern was strong and consistent.

The AAP’s simple version: Brush, Book, Bed

  • Brush: help your child brush their teeth every night.
  • Book: read a favorite book, or two.
  • Bed: get to bed at a regular time each night.

The AAP also warns against putting a baby to bed with a bottle of milk, formula, juice or any sugary drink.

Sample routines

AgeExample
Newborn to 3 monthsDiaper, sleepsuit, feed with lights low, short song, into the bassinet. Keep it flexible; timing varies a lot.
4 to 12 monthsFeed in a lit room, bath or wash, diaper and pajamas, sleep sack, teeth or gums, book, song, lights out, into the crib awake.
ToddlersBath, pajamas, potty if toilet training, brush teeth, two books, drink of water, song, lights out.

Five principles that matter more than the details

  1. Same order, every night. Predictability is the point. Your child learns that each step leads to sleep.
  2. Short. Most families find 20 to 30 minutes is plenty. Longer routines invite stalling.
  3. Move the feed earlier. If your baby falls asleep feeding, put the feed at the start of the routine so they go into the crib awake. See the feed-to-sleep association.
  4. End in the bedroom. The last steps, book and song, happen in the room where your child sleeps, with the lights low.
  5. Down awake. The AAP’s parent site advises putting babies to bed when they are drowsy, not waiting until they are asleep, so they learn to fall asleep on their own.

Where the routine happens

Do the last part of the routine in your child’s room, with the lights low and the door closed. That makes the room itself part of the cue. A bath can happen elsewhere; the book, song and goodnight belong in the bedroom.

When there are two caregivers

Write the routine down and keep it the same with either parent, a grandparent or a sitter. It does not need to be identical to the minute, but the order and the ending should be.

Naps

A shorter version of the bedtime routine before naps helps too: diaper, sleep sack, curtains, a short song, into the crib.

When a routine isn’t enough

A routine sets your child up to fall asleep well. It doesn’t, on its own, solve a baby who needs to be rocked or fed back to sleep every hour. If that is where you are, see how to sleep train a baby. At Actually Sleeping, the routine is one part of a custom plan written by Dr. Sam for your baby.

Frequently asked

When should I start a bedtime routine?

You can start in the first weeks with something very simple, like a diaper change, feed, and song in a dim room. A large study of bedtime routines concluded that the earlier a routine starts, the better.

How long should a bedtime routine be?

Most families find 20 to 30 minutes is plenty. The order and consistency matter more than the length. Long routines tend to invite stalling, especially with toddlers, so keep it short and predictable.

Should a bath be part of the routine every night?

Not necessarily. A bath can be a calming cue, but a quick wash works just as well. What matters is doing the same steps in the same order most nights.

Is it okay to feed my baby to sleep as part of the routine?

Feeding is a normal part of the routine. If your baby wakes often at night, try moving the feed to the start so they go into the crib awake. Night feeds are your pediatrician’s call.
