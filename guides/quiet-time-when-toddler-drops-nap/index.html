My toddler is dropping their nap. What is quiet time and how do I set it up?

Quiet time is a daily rest period, often 30 to 60 minutes, when your child stays in their room with books or calm toys and may fall asleep. Most children stop napping between ages 3 and 5: a large review found about a third had stopped by 3 and about 80% by 5. How it is set up, and when, depends on your child’s sleep need and bedtime.

When toddlers stop napping

A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies of napping in children up to 12 years. Its estimates:

AgeChildren who had stopped napping
Before 2 yearsRare, under 2.5%
About 3 yearsAbout 33%
About 4 yearsAbout 57%
About 5 yearsAbout 80%
Over 5 yearsAbout 94%

The ranges between studies were wide, so treat these as a guide, not a deadline. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 11 to 14 hours of sleep per 24 hours at 1 to 2 years and 10 to 13 hours at 3 to 5 years, including naps. A child who drops the nap needs that sleep at night.

Signs the nap is really ending

  • Your child regularly lies awake for most of nap time and is still in a good mood by evening.
  • On nap days, bedtime takes a long time or gets pushed much later.
  • This has gone on for a few weeks, not a few days.

Before age 3, nap refusal is often a phase: a new skill, a cold, a move, or a schedule that has drifted. Because stopping napping before 2 is rare, a 2-year-old who skips naps for a week usually still needs one. See a 2 year old’s sleep schedule.

What quiet time is

Quiet time replaces the nap with a predictable rest period at the same time of day. Your child stays in their room or a calm spot, with low-key activities. If they fall asleep, that’s fine. If they don’t, they have still rested, and you have had a break. Screens are not rest, and they make the transition back harder.

Because your child will be awake and alone in the room, it has to be safe: childproof the room, anchor furniture to the wall, and use a gate at the door if your child is young.

Why the transition so often goes wrong

  • The nap is dropped too early. A toddler on a nap strike is treated as finished, and evenings fall apart.
  • Bedtime does not move. A child who has lost an hour or two of daytime sleep is put down at the old bedtime, overtired.
  • Late naps creep in. A 4 p.m. doze in the car pushes bedtime late.
  • Quiet time becomes a battle. Without a clear, consistent limit, it turns into an hour of coming out.

Getting it right depends on your child’s age, actual sleep need, nap history and how bedtime is going. Generic advice cannot see any of that.

Frequently asked

At what age do toddlers stop napping?

A 2020 review found napping stops before age 2 in under 2.5% of children. About a third had stopped by 3, about 57% by 4, and about 80% by 5. There is wide variation between children.

How long should quiet time be?

Many families aim for 30 to 60 minutes, at the time the nap used to be. The right length depends on your child’s age, total sleep and bedtime. It works best when it happens at the same time every day.

Should I make my 2 year old nap if they refuse?

You cannot force sleep, but stopping naps before 2 is rare, so most 2-year-olds who refuse are on strike rather than finished. How the nap is offered, and what happens on no-nap days, is part of a Actually Sleeping toddler plan.
