My toddler is dropping their nap. What is quiet time and how do I set it up?

Quiet time is a daily rest period, often 30 to 60 minutes, when your child stays in their room with books or calm toys and may fall asleep. Most children stop napping between ages 3 and 5: a large review found about a third had stopped by 3 and about 80% by 5. Quiet time keeps the rest without the nap fight.

When toddlers stop napping

A 2020 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled studies of napping in children up to 12 years. Its estimates:

AgeChildren who had stopped napping
Before 2 yearsRare, under 2.5%
About 3 yearsAbout 33%
About 4 yearsAbout 57%
About 5 yearsAbout 80%
Over 5 yearsAbout 94%

The ranges between studies were wide, so treat these as a guide, not a deadline. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 11 to 14 hours of sleep per 24 hours at 1 to 2 years and 10 to 13 hours at 3 to 5 years, including naps. A child who drops the nap needs that sleep at night.

Signs the nap is really ending

  • Your child regularly lies awake for most of nap time and is still in a good mood by evening.
  • On nap days, bedtime takes a long time or gets pushed much later.
  • This has gone on for a few weeks, not a few days.

Before age 3, nap refusal can be a phase: a new skill, a cold, a move, or a schedule that needs a small adjustment. Because stopping napping before 2 is rare, a 2-year-old who skips naps for a week usually still needs one. See a 2 year old’s sleep schedule.

What quiet time is

Quiet time replaces the nap with a predictable rest period at the same time of day. Your child stays in their room or a calm spot, with low-key activities. If they fall asleep, that’s fine. If they don’t, they have still rested, and you have had a break.

How to set it up

  1. Same time every day, usually when the nap used to be, after lunch.
  2. Start short. 20 or 30 minutes, then lengthen as your child gets used to it.
  3. A quiet-time box. Books, puzzles, stickers, soft toys, drawing supplies. Rotate the contents so they stay interesting. Save them for quiet time only.
  4. A clear end. A timer, a toddler clock that changes color, or a quiet song you play when it’s over.
  5. Same simple rules each day. Stay in your room, play quietly, come out when the light changes.
  6. No screens. A show is not rest, and it makes the transition back harder.
  7. Safety. Childproof the room, anchor furniture, and use a gate at the door if your child is young.

Adjusting bedtime

When the nap goes, the evening can get hard. Move bedtime earlier, sometimes by 30 to 60 minutes, at least during the transition. Many families alternate for a while: nap on some days, quiet time and an early bedtime on others.

Daycare and preschool

Many programs have a rest period for all children. If your child naps there and then fights bedtime at home, ask whether they can rest without sleeping, or adjust bedtime on those days. See daycare naps.

Common problems

  • Keeps coming out: calmly return them with the same short phrase. Praise the minutes they did stay.
  • Falls asleep at 4 p.m. in the car: try to avoid late car trips, or keep the late nap very short, so bedtime isn’t pushed late.
  • Falls apart before dinner: bring bedtime earlier and keep the late afternoon calm.

Frequently asked

At what age do toddlers stop napping?

A 2020 review found napping stops before age 2 in under 2.5% of children. About a third had stopped by 3, about 57% by 4, and about 80% by 5. There is wide variation between children.

How long should quiet time be?

Start with 20 or 30 minutes and build toward about an hour if it suits your family. Use a timer or toddler clock so your child knows when it ends.

Should I make my 2 year old nap if they refuse?

You can’t force sleep, but you can keep offering a nap at the usual time. Stopping naps before 2 is rare, and most 2-year-olds still need one. Treat a week of refusal as a phase before dropping it.
