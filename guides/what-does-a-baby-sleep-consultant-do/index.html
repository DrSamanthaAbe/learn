What does a baby sleep consultant actually do?

A baby sleep consultant assesses how your baby falls asleep, wakes and naps, writes a plan to change the behavioral parts, and supports you while you carry it out. Good consultants screen for medical red flags and refer you to your pediatrician when they find them. They do not diagnose, prescribe, or make feeding decisions that belong to your pediatrician.

The usual sequence

  1. Intake. A questionnaire or call covering age (and corrected age if your baby was born early), how your baby falls asleep, what happens at each waking, feeding, naps, the sleep space, health history and what you have already tried.
  2. Assessment. The consultant looks for the drivers: a sleep association, a schedule mismatch, an overtired or undertired baby, early morning light, or something that needs a doctor rather than a plan.
  3. The plan. A written document covering the routine, the schedule, how you respond to wakings, how night feeds are handled (with your pediatrician’s input), and how naps will be approached.
  4. Support while you do it. This is where consultants differ most, and where results are won or lost.
  5. Adjustment. Babies rarely follow a script exactly. The consultant reads your notes and changes timing or responses as needed.
  6. Wrap-up. Guidance for what comes next: nap transitions, illness, travel, a new sibling.

What a consultant brings that a book does not

Pattern recognition. Someone who has worked with many babies can often tell from a short description whether an early waking is a schedule problem or a light problem, or whether a setback on night 4 is normal. The value is less in knowing the method and more in knowing which detail to change for your baby, today.

What a consultant should not do

  • Diagnose. Snoring, breathing pauses, poor weight gain, reflux symptoms and suspected allergies belong with your pediatrician. See sleep consultant vs pediatric sleep specialist.
  • Recommend medication or supplements for sleep. Questions about medicines go to your pediatrician or pharmacist.
  • Override your pediatrician on feeding. Whether your baby still needs night feeds is a growth and health question.
  • Bend safe sleep rules. The AAP advises a firm, flat, non-inclined surface, on the back, without soft bedding, and says weighted swaddles and weighted objects on or near the baby are not safe. A plan should never work around these.

What it is like for you

You still do the nights. A consultant does not usually come to your home and put your baby down; you do it, following the plan, and report what happened. That is deliberate. Your baby is learning to fall asleep with you as the parent in the room, and you are learning what to do the next time sleep goes sideways. A short daily log is what lets a consultant adjust the plan instead of guessing.

Frequently asked

Does a sleep consultant come to my house?

Most work remotely by video, phone and messaging. Either way, you are usually the one putting your baby down, because your baby needs to learn to sleep with you, not with a visitor. Actually Sleeping is fully remote, with live support on night one.

Is a sleep consultant the same as a sleep coach?

In practice, yes. Neither title is a licensed profession, and people use them interchangeably. Judge the person by their process, support, credentials and what they put in writing, not by the title.

Will a sleep consultant make me stop night feeds?

A good one will not decide that for you. Whether your baby still needs to eat at night is a question for your pediatrician. An Actually Sleeping plan never forces dropping night feeds and teaches independent sleep while keeping the feeds your baby needs.

What information should I have ready for a sleep consultant?

A few days of notes on bedtime, wakings, feeds and naps, your baby’s birth date and due date, any health history, and a list of what you have already tried and for how long. It helps the consultant see quickly where things break down.
