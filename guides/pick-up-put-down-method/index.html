Does the pick-up-put-down method work?

It can, mainly for younger babies of about 3 to 6 months, and it is slow: in practice it often takes 1 to 3 weeks and many repetitions each night. You pick your baby up when they cry, calm them, and put them back down awake, repeating until they fall asleep in the crib. After about 6 to 7 months it often backfires, because being picked up and put down repeatedly stimulates and frustrates older babies more than it calms them.

How it works

  1. After the bedtime routine, put your baby down awake.
  2. If your baby fusses, pause for a minute or two. Try your voice and a hand on the chest first.
  3. If fussing becomes crying, pick your baby up and hold them until calm. Calm, not asleep.
  4. Put your baby back down as soon as the crying stops, even if they are wide awake.
  5. Repeat until your baby falls asleep in the crib.

On the first nights that can mean 20, 30 or more pick-ups over 45 to 90 minutes. The number should fall night by night.

Who it suits

AgeHow it tends to go
Under 3 monthsToo early for a method; soothe and feed on cue
3–6 monthsThe best fit: babies are light, calm quickly when held, and are not yet strongly stimulated by the up-and-down
6–8 monthsMixed; many babies cry harder at each put-down
8 months and upOften counterproductive; the chair method or timed checks usually fit better

What the evidence says

We found no controlled trial that tests pick-up-put-down on its own, so there is no direct research on how well it works compared with other methods. It rests on the same principle as the studied methods: the baby does the final step of falling asleep in the crib while your help is gradually reduced. That principle is well supported; this particular technique is supported mostly by clinical and parent experience.

Pros and cons

  • Pros: very responsive; you never leave a crying baby; usable from about 3 months; fits room-sharing.
  • Cons: physically tiring; slow; easy to slide into holding the baby to sleep; stimulating for older babies; hard to keep up at 3 a.m.

Making it work

  • Put your baby down the moment they are calm. The longer you hold, the closer you are to rocking to sleep.
  • Stretch the pause before each pick-up a little every night.
  • Replace pick-ups with crib-side soothing as the nights go on: voice, then a hand, then nothing.
  • Get the schedule right first. An overtired 4-month-old will not settle with any method; see wake windows by age.
  • Always put your baby down on the back in an empty crib, however many times you repeat it.

Signs it is not the right method

Switch if, after 5 to 7 consistent nights, the number of pick-ups is not falling; if your baby cries harder each time they are put down; or if your baby falls asleep in your arms nearly every time. Good next steps are the chair method or timed checks, depending on your baby’s age. Babies around 4 months are also going through a permanent change in sleep structure, covered in the 4-month sleep regression.

Frequently asked

What age is pick-up-put-down best for?

About 3 to 6 months. Younger babies need soothing and feeding on cue rather than a method, and older babies are often stimulated or frustrated by repeated picking up.

How long does pick-up-put-down take to work?

Typically 1 to 3 weeks. You should see the number of pick-ups per bedtime dropping within the first 5 to 7 nights; if not, the method or the schedule needs to change.

How many times should I pick my baby up?

As many as it takes on the first nights, which can be 20 to 30 or more. The goal is fewer each night, with crib-side soothing gradually replacing the pick-ups.

Is pick-up-put-down a no-cry method?

No. There is usually crying between pick-ups, and total crying time can be similar to other methods, spread over more nights. The difference is that you respond every time.
