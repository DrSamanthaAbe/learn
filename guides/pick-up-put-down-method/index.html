Does the pick-up-put-down method work?

It can, mainly for younger babies, and it is slow. You pick your baby up when they cry, calm them, and put them back down awake, repeating until they fall asleep in the crib. We found no controlled trial testing it on its own. In older babies it often backfires, because being picked up and put down repeatedly can stimulate and frustrate more than it calms.

What it is

Pick-up-put-down is a hands-on, responsive approach. When your baby cries in the crib, you lift them until they are calm, not asleep, and put them back down awake. You repeat that until they fall asleep in the crib, and the help is meant to shrink night by night.

What the evidence says

We found no controlled trial that tests pick-up-put-down on its own, so there is no direct research on how well it works compared with other approaches. It rests on the same principle as the studied methods: the baby does the final step of falling asleep in the crib while your help is reduced. That principle is well supported. A review of 52 studies for the American Academy of Sleep Medicine found that 94% reported behavioral interventions effective. This particular technique is supported mostly by clinical and parent experience.

Who it tends to fail for, and why

  • Older babies. Many babies past the early months find being lifted and lowered over and over stimulating. They cry harder at each put-down.
  • Babies who fall asleep in your arms. The longer each hold, the closer it gets to rocking to sleep, which is the habit you are trying to change.
  • Tired parents. It is physically demanding and slow, and very hard to keep up at 3 a.m.
  • Babies whose schedule is off. An overtired baby will not settle with any approach; see wake windows by age.
  • Babies around 4 months, who are also going through a permanent change in sleep structure, covered in the 4-month sleep regression.

The appeal is real: you never leave a crying baby. The problem is that the method is generic, and whether it helps or winds your baby up depends on your baby.

Frequently asked

What age is pick-up-put-down best for?

It is usually tried with younger babies, because older babies are often stimulated or frustrated by repeated picking up. Under about 4 months, babies need soothing and feeding on cue. Actually Sleeping writes an approach for your baby’s actual age and temperament.

How long does pick-up-put-down take to work?

It is one of the slower approaches, and for some babies it does not work at all. If pick-ups are not falling after a week of consistency, the approach does not fit. Actually Sleeping guarantees 8–12 hours through the night within 14 days of consistent, correct implementation.

How many times should I pick my baby up?

There is no right number, which is part of the problem: it can mean dozens of pick-ups on early nights. If the number is not falling, the approach does not fit your baby, and repeating it harder rarely helps.

Is pick-up-put-down a no-cry method?

No. There is usually crying between pick-ups. The difference is that you respond every time. No approach can promise zero protest, because any change to how a baby falls asleep is frustrating at first.
