What is a good sleep schedule for a 4 month old?

At 4 months most babies take 3–4 naps and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake 1.5–2 hours at a time. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 12–16 hours of sleep in 24 from 4 months, including naps. Bedtime usually falls between 7 and 8 p.m., with 10–12 hours in the crib overnight and often 1–2 night feeds.

When naps start running longer than an hour, the day naturally compresses to 3 naps with windows closer to 2 hours. Until then, a fourth catnap keeps the last window from overshooting.

What is changing developmentally

Sleep matures into more regular cycles of light and deep sleep, each lasting about 50 minutes in infancy, with a brief arousal between them. This change is permanent. A baby who falls asleep with help now notices that the help is gone at each arousal and calls for it. Rolling arrives for many babies, and feeds get distractible in daylight.

Common problems at this age

  • Waking every 1–2 hours: see 4-month sleep regression or sleep problem for how to tell a passing phase from a pattern.
  • Short naps: one sleep cycle and done. Usually the same cause as the night waking; see 30-minute naps.
  • Feeding back to sleep at every waking: see the feed-to-sleep association.
  • Rolling in the crib: the swaddle must be gone. Keep placing your baby on the back in an empty crib; a baby who rolls both ways on their own can stay as they land.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Generally yes. Many families start formal sleep training from about 4 months with healthy, full-term babies, once the pediatrician is happy with growth and feeding. The AAP’s parent site suggests that from 4 months you put babies down before they are asleep so they learn to fall asleep on their own. Teaching independent sleep does not require dropping night feeds; those are separate decisions. Frequently asked

Should a 4 month old take 3 or 4 naps?

Either. Four short naps or three longer ones can both add up to about 4 hours. Let nap length decide: if the third nap ends before 4:30 p.m., a catnap usually helps.

What time should a 4 month old go to bed?

Between 7 and 8 p.m. for most, about 1.75–2 hours after the last nap ends.

How long does the 4-month regression last?

The change in sleep cycles is permanent. There is no research-defined length for the disruption; in practice it often settles within a few weeks for babies who can already fall asleep on their own, and lasts longer for babies who depend on feeding, rocking or holding.

Does a 4 month old still need night feeds?

Many do, often 1–2. Ask your pediatrician what is appropriate for your baby’s growth; do not cut feeds on your own.
