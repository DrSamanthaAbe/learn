How do I break the feed-to-sleep association?

The association loosens when your baby learns to fall asleep without feeding, while still getting every feed they need. It is a change in how sleep starts, not a cut in milk, and night feeds stay for as long as your pediatrician wants them. Why feeding to sleep causes wake-ups

Feeding is the most powerful sleep cue a baby has. A baby who falls asleep eating learns that eating is how sleep starts, at bedtime and at every return to near-waking between sleep cycles after it. Some of the night feeds that follow are requests for the sleep cue rather than for milk. Feeding to sleep is biologically normal. It becomes a problem when it is the only way your baby can fall asleep and you are up every couple of hours.

This is a problem Actually Sleeping is built to fix. Dr. Sam writes a plan that separates feeding from falling asleep for your baby, while keeping every feed your pediatrician wants, and a coach is with you live on night one.

Why it is harder to change than it looks

  • The feed is comfort as well as food. Change it and your baby protests the change, not hunger.
  • Quick swaps move the problem. Replace the feed with rocking or a pacifier and your baby needs that between cycles instead.
  • Partial attempts backfire. Feeding back to sleep after a long protest teaches a baby that protesting longer works.
  • Generic advice assumes an average baby. Age, growth, breast or bottle, number of night feeds and temperament all change what the right plan looks like.

Most families who come to us have already tried moving the feed or cutting feeds on their own. Often the feed was never the only thing their baby relied on to fall back asleep.

For breastfeeding families

Breaking the association works with breastfeeding. The change is to how sleep starts, not to the amount of milk. If supply or weight gain is a concern, talk with your pediatrician or a lactation consultant before any night feed changes. Your supply and comfort are part of how a plan is paced.

Frequently asked

Is it bad to feed my baby to sleep?

Not bad. It is biologically normal. It becomes a problem when it is the only way your baby can fall asleep and you are up every couple of hours. That is when a Actually Sleeping plan helps, keeping the feeds your baby needs.

At what age can I stop feeding to sleep?

Actually Sleeping works with families from the newborn stage, laying foundations early, and the 14-day guarantee clock starts at the 10-week birthday. Separating feeding from falling asleep is not the same as dropping night feeds, which is always your pediatrician’s call.

Will my baby be hungry if I don’t feed at night?

Whether your baby still needs night feeds is your pediatrician’s call. Actually Sleeping plans never force dropping night feeds. Changing how your baby falls asleep is separate from how much they eat, so needed feeds stay while wake-ups that are not about hunger stop needing a feed.
