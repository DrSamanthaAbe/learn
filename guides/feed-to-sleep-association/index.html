How do I break the feed-to-sleep association?

Move the feed to the start of the bedtime routine instead of the end, so your baby finishes eating 15–20 minutes before lying down awake, and then respond to wake-ups without feeding (or with one scheduled feed if your baby still needs it). The association usually loosens in 3–5 nights.

Why feeding to sleep causes wake-ups

Feeding is the most powerful sleep cue a baby has. A baby who falls asleep eating learns that eating is how sleep starts — at bedtime and at every cycle transition after it. The night feeds that follow are often requests for the sleep cue, not calories.

The routine swap

  1. Feed first in a lit room, then diaper, pajamas, book, song, bed.
  2. Keep baby awake through the feed. Unlatch or remove the bottle at the first slow, drowsy suck.
  3. Down awake. Drowsy-but-awake is a myth for most babies; aim for clearly awake.
  4. Decide on night feeds in advance with your pediatrician. Under about 6 months many babies keep one; after that most do not need any if gaining well.

For breastfeeding families

This works with breastfeeding. The change is the order of events, not the amount of milk. If supply or weight gain is a concern, loop in your pediatrician or lactation consultant before dropping any night feed.

Frequently asked

Is it bad to feed my baby to sleep?

Not bad — it is biologically normal. It becomes a problem when it is the only way your baby can fall asleep and you are up every two hours.

At what age can I stop feeding to sleep?

From about 10–12 weeks you can start separating the feed from sleep onset. Dropping night feeds entirely is a pediatrician call, usually somewhere between 4 and 6 months.

Will my baby be hungry if I don’t feed at night?

If daytime intake is adequate and growth is on track, most babies over 6 months are not. Offer more during the day as night feeds go.
