Which over-the-counter medicines affect a baby’s sleep?

Fever and pain relievers can help a sick baby rest but do not cause sleep; antihistamines and cough-and-cold products should not be used for sleep in young children at all; gas drops and gripe water are largely inert for sleep. No over-the-counter product is a substitute for a sleep plan, and every one of them is a pediatrician decision in a baby.

A note on scope: Dr. Sam is a Doctor of Pharmacy, not your baby’s physician. This guide explains how a medication relates to sleep. Whether and how your child takes any medication is a decision for your pediatrician.

The pattern behind the aisle

Everything that helps does so by removing discomfort so the baby’s own sleep can happen. Nothing in the aisle teaches a baby to fall asleep or stay asleep. If your baby sleeps badly when well, the answer is in the routine, the schedule and the sleep association, not the pharmacy.

When to call the pediatrician instead

Fever in a baby under 3 months; trouble breathing; refusing feeds; fewer wet diapers; a baby who is hard to wake; any question about dose. Those are not sleep questions.

Frequently asked

Can Benadryl help my baby sleep on a flight?

Pediatricians advise against it. It can cause the opposite reaction in young children and dosing errors are a real risk.

Do gas drops help a baby sleep?

Trials have not shown simethicone to reduce crying or improve sleep. It is generally safe but rarely the fix.

Is it okay to give medicine ‘just in case’ before bed?

No. Give a medicine for a reason, at the dose and interval your pediatrician or the label specifies.
