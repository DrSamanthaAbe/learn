Which over-the-counter medicines affect a baby’s sleep?

Fever and pain relievers can help a sick baby rest but do not cause sleep; antihistamines and cough-and-cold products should not be used for sleep in young children at all; gas drops and gripe water are not sleep aids. No over-the-counter product is a substitute for a sleep plan, and every one of them is a pediatrician decision in a baby.

The pattern behind the aisle

Everything that helps does so by removing discomfort so the baby’s own sleep can happen. Nothing in the aisle teaches a baby to fall asleep or stay asleep. If your baby sleeps badly when well, the cause is in the routine, the schedule and the way your baby falls asleep, not in the pharmacy.

When to call the pediatrician instead

Fever in a baby under 3 months; trouble breathing; refusing feeds; fewer wet diapers; a baby who is hard to wake; any question about dose. Those are not sleep questions.

Frequently asked

Can Benadryl help my baby sleep on a flight?

No. The drug label says diphenhydramine should not be used to make a child sleepy, it can cause excitement instead, and CDC travel guidance says antihistamines are contraindicated under age 1. Ask your pediatrician about any travel medicine.

Do gas drops help a baby sleep?

Gas drops are not a sleep aid. Ask your pediatrician whether they are worth using for your baby. If your baby sleeps badly when comfortable, the cause is usually how they fall asleep, which is what a sleep plan from Actually Sleeping changes.

Is it okay to give medicine ‘just in case’ before bed?

No. Give a medicine for a reason, at the dose and interval your pediatrician or the label specifies. Medicine given to prevent a bad night does not change the pattern behind the bad nights.
