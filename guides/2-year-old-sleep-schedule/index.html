What is a good sleep schedule for a 2 year old?

Between 2 and 3 years most children take one nap of 1–2 hours and, as a rule of thumb, stay awake 5–6 hours on either side of it. The AASM recommends 11–14 hours of sleep in 24 at ages 1–2 and 10–13 hours at ages 3–5, naps included, and bedtime usually falls between 7 and 8 p.m.

Sample day for a 2 year old

Treat every time below as a starting point. Shift the whole day to match your baby’s actual wake-up time, then adjust one thing at a time in 15-minute steps and hold it for three days before judging.

If bedtime turns into an hour of chatting in the dark, shorten the nap in 15-minute steps (wake by 2:30, then 2:15) before you consider dropping it.

What is changing developmentally

Sentences, imagination and a strong sense of self. Imagination brings the first real fears of the dark or of “monsters.” Two year olds are skilled at stalling: one more book, water, a different blanket. Many families also face toilet learning, a new sibling, or the move from crib to bed in this year, and each can disturb sleep for a couple of weeks.

Common problems at this age

  • Getting out of bed repeatedly: see toddler won’t stay in bed. If your child is safe in the crib and not climbing out, there is no need to rush the move; closer to 3 is easier.
  • Nap refusal: common around age 2 and usually temporary. Keep offering a daily rest in the dark room. In a large review, fewer than 2.5% of children had stopped napping before age 2, and most stopped somewhere between 3 and 5.
  • Bedtime stalling: build the requests into the routine (one drink, two books, one song) and end it the same way every night.
  • Early waking: see early morning waking. A color-changing clock gives a 2 year old a rule they can see.
  • Night fears: a dim, warm-colored night light is fine. Acknowledge the fear briefly and keep the response short and the same.

Is this age right for sleep training?

Yes, though at this age it is better described as sleep coaching: clear expectations explained in daylight, a visual routine, limits that hold, and a calm, repeatable response. Frequently asked

Does a 2 year old still need a nap?

Almost always. A large review found fewer than 2.5% of children stop napping before 2, while 94% have stopped by 5. A 2 year old who refuses the nap for a week or two usually returns to it if you keep offering.

How much sleep does a 2 year old need?

The AASM recommends 11–14 hours per 24 hours at ages 1–2 years and 10–13 hours at ages 3–5 years, naps included.

What time should a 2 year old go to bed?

Between 7 and 8 p.m. for most, about 5.5–6 hours after the nap ends.

When should I move my toddler from a crib to a bed?

When the crib is no longer safe because your child is climbing out or has reached 35 inches (89 cm), the height at which MedlinePlus says children should no longer use a crib, or when your child is close to 3 and ready. Moving earlier often means more night-time visits, not better sleep.
